# Look Before You Move: Agent-Interface-Aligned Supervision for Open-World Mobile Manipulation

## Abstract
Open-world mobile manipulation requires a robot to find an object, approach it, and move it to a target receptacle from a language instruction. Training vision-language model (VLM) agents for these tasks requires demonstrations of both action selection and active vision. Existing pipelines often convert planned trajectories into high-level action labels, but shortest-path progress provides limited supervision for deciding where to look. We introduce Agent-Interface Data Alignment (AIDA), a data collection pipeline in which a teacher directly selects and executes actions through the deployed agent's skill interface. For mobile manipulation, we design teachers that maintain target visibility, recover lost targets, and approach for picking or placing. We add template-based spatial reasoning supervision and use a shared harness to organize observations, history, and outputs during training and inference. The resulting agent, AIDA-OWMM, achieves 71.76% full-task success on 170 held-out OWMM episodes. Teacher sensitivity analysis shows that active-vision demonstrations improve student success. The simulation-trained agent achieves 38% end-to-end success on a real mobile robot without additional training. Applying AIDA to a navigation subset of EmbodiedBench further demonstrates the pipeline's utility for navigation tasks.

## 1 Introduction
Open-world mobile manipulation (OWMM) studies how robots can follow language instructions to find objects and move them to target receptacles in everyday environments [ 1 , 2 ] . Solving these tasks requires coordinating perception, navigation, and manipulation: the robot must decide where to look, how to approach a target, and when it is ready to pick or place. In an agentic mobile manipulation system, a vision-language model (VLM) selects high-level actions from visual observations, task instructions, and interaction history, while dedicated controllers execute the corresponding motions. A central challenge is training the VLM to make effective decisions as the robot moves, its view changes, and new actions become possible. Prior work constructs training data by converting planned or executed robot trajectories into high-level action labels [ 2 , 3 ] . When these trajectories are driven primarily by shortest-path progress, they can provide limited supervision for active vision: keeping a target visible during approach, recovering it after it leaves view, and obtaining a suitable view for manipulation. This motivates collecting demonstrations from a teacher that actively decides where to look and how to move. To this end, we introduce Agent-Interface Data Alignment (AIDA) , a pipeline for constructing demonstrations through the deployed agent’s decision interface, and apply it to mobile manipulation. At each step, the teacher selects a skill and its arguments, executes the call, and uses the resulting observation to choose the next action (Figure 1 ). High-level actions thus guide demonstration collection instead of being inferred afterward from motion trajectories. The teacher can use privileged simulator information, while the student’s inputs contain only observations and context available at deployment. Figure 1: AIDA-OWMM overview. Left: the teacher generates demonstrations by selecting and executing high-level actions. Right: spatial annotations and a shared agent harness support training and deployment. The real-robot rate shown includes low-level grasp failures among recorded attempts. Within AIDA, we design the teacher to demonstrate active target acquisition . Finding a target is only the beginning: it may leave view during approach or remain out of reach when the robot stops. Building on active perception [ 4 , 5 ] , the teacher moves to collect useful views, turns to recover lost targets, and approaches to prepare for picking or placing. These behaviors teach the student to coordinate looking and moving in preparation for manipulation. We further provide spatial reasoning supervision to make the judgments behind these actions explicit. For each teacher action, an offline annotator describes whether the target is visible, where it lies relative to the robot, whether the next skill can be executed, and how far the task has progressed. A shared agent harness assembles prompts, maintains episode memory, and defines the model’s output format consistently during training and deployment. We use supervised fine-tuning to teach the VLM to predict spatial state, reasoning, and actions from its observations and history. At deployment, the model generates these outputs itself. Our mobile manipulation agent, AIDA-OWMM , achieves 71.76% full-task success on 170 held-out OWMM episodes. Teacher sensitivity analysis shows that demonstrations from teachers that maintain visibility and recover lost targets yield better student performance than those from a simple approach policy; harness ablations further support action history and spatial reasoning supervision. Trained entirely in simulation, the agent also performs pick-and-place tasks on a real mobile robot in an office environment without additional model training. Finally, we evaluate the approach on short-horizon navigation tasks in EmbodiedBench to examine its applicability beyond the OWMM benchmark. Our contributions are: • A pipeline for agent-aligned data collection : AIDA generates teacher demonstrations directly through the deployed agent’s high-level action interface. • A teacher for active-vision demonstrations : we design behaviors for maintaining visibility, recovering targets, and approaching for manipulation, and evaluate their effect on student performance. • A harness for spatial reasoning supervision : structured target state and task progress guide action learning through a shared prompting and memory interface.
