# Guava: Distilling Frontier VLMs into a Compact Agent through a Robotic Manipulation Harness

## Abstract
Language models trained on large-scale vision-language data have demonstrated strong potential for embodied agents. Harnessing models through embodied tools use offers a promising alternative to end-to-end vision-language-action systems by combining high-level reasoning with external modules for perception, planning, and control. However, it remains unclear what makes an effective harness for embodied manipulation, and to what extent such a harness can unlock embodied capabilities in a wide range of reasoning models. In this work, we present Guava, a harness framework for embodied tool use developed through systematic exploration of the design space of agent workflows, action spaces, and observation spaces. Our study identifies three key ingredients for effective embodied agents: iterative perception-reasoning-action loops, semantic action abstractions, and multimodal observations. To understand whether these design principles are universal even to small models, we develop an end-to-end training pipeline that distills embodied manipulation capabilities into a 4B open-source model using fewer than 2K trajectories collected entirely in simulation. Experimental results in both simulation and real-world environments show performance comparable to frontier proprietary models while exhibiting strong generalization to unseen objects, novel instructions, and long-horizon tasks. Results suggest that a well-designed harness can serve as a scalable, model-agnostic interface for embodied manipulation, enabling strong emergent embodied capabilities in compact open-source models with minimal training data.

## 1 Introduction
Figure 1 : Overview. A frontier model teacher generates manipulation trajectories in simulation for training. Supervised fine-tuning distill these behaviors to Guava-4B , which operates through a manipulation harness at deployment. Recent progress in vision–language models (VLMs) and VLM-driven agents has demonstrated broad capabilities in visual understanding, reasoning, and tool use Driess et al. (2023) ; Qwen Team (2026) ; Team et al. (2026) . When equipped with an agentic harness that exposes reusable perception (e.g., object detection) and robotic manipulation tools (e.g., grasping), frontier VLMs can interpret natural-language instructions, reason about the observed scene, and generate real-world robot actions in diverse environments for diverse goals Liang et al. (2022) ; Shi et al. (2025a) ; Fu et al. (2026) . Leveraging the general-purpose reasoning and tool-use capabilities of VLMs, such agentic systems can support a broad range of manipulation tasks without additional task-specific finetuning. However, using frontier VLMs for closed-loop robotic manipulation requires repeated remote model calls, resulting in high inference costs, added latency, and dependence on external services Shi et al. (2025b) . A potential solution is to deploy a compact model on a single commodity GPU, reducing reliance on external services and keeping robot observations and task instructions local Boyle et al. (2025) . This motivates our study on distilling the tool-using capabilities of frontier VLMs into compact models that can be deployed directly on robotic platforms. The central question is therefore how can the tool-using manipulation capabilities of a frontier VLM be distilled into a compact model? Learning from a more capable teacher provides a natural starting point Xu et al. (2024) ; Cai et al. (2025) , but distilling closed-loop manipulation capabilities poses two key challenges: the design of a shared interaction interface and a data collection protocol for efficient and generalizable capability distillation. First, the compact model requires an abstract interaction interface that exposes useful perception and action abstractions while reducing the burden of low-level perception, geometric reasoning, and control. Second, the data collection protocol must cover diverse interaction states across objects, scene configurations, and task progress. In particular, the teacher and compact model may follow different execution paths and therefore encounter different states. Training only on successful teacher trajectories risks overfitting to those paths, limiting the compact model’s ability to adapt when its own decisions or physical perturbations lead to states outside the training distribution. To address these challenges, we develop Guava , a framework for distilling the tool-using manipulation capabilities of frontier VLMs into compact models. The framework has two core components: a manipulation harness and a simulation-based data collection protocol. We first study how harness design affects both frontier and compact models, and use the resulting interface for teacher data generation and student deployment. The harness exposes reusable perception and manipulation tools together with execution feedback, allowing models to reason at a higher level while delegating low-level perception, geometry, and control to tool implementations. The data collection protocol uses this harness to execute and validate teacher trajectories across diverse tasks and scene configurations in simulation. We also introduce perturbations to collect trajectories that capture deviations, broadening state coverage beyond successful teacher execution paths. The resulting interaction data supports efficient and generalizable fine-tuning of a compact VLM, which can be directly deployed in the real world and further refined through reinforcement learning. We instantiate Guava using GPT-5.4 as the teacher and Qwen3.5-4B as the compact model. Using the shared harness and simulation data engine, we collect 2,268 physically validated interaction trajectories, including 714 perturbation branches. Supervised fine-tuning on these interactions produces Guava-4B , a compact tool-using manipulation agent. We additionally study reinforcement learning as a refinement for challenging long-horizon tasks. We evaluate Guava-4B on our custom agentic manipulation benchmark, LIBERO-PRO, and a suite of real-world robot tasks. On our custom benchmark, supervised fine-tuning improves the compact model’s average success rate from 22.2% to 87.1%, approaching GPT-5.4 at 90.4%. Harness studies across models validate the importance of interaction interface design, while perturbation trajectories particularly benefit generalization to unseen tasks. We further evaluate zero-shot real-world transfer, reinforcement-learning refinement, and inference efficiency. In summary, our contributions are: • A framework for distilling tool-using manipulation capabilities from frontier VLMs to compact models through a manipulation harness and a simulation-based data engine that generates executed and validated teacher interactions with environment randomization and perturbation branches. • A systematic study of manipulation harness design across frontier and compact VLMs, examining the effects of model-visible feedback, action abstraction, and replanning granularity on manipulation performance. • Guava-4B , a compact manipulation agent trained on only 2,268 simulation-generated teacher trajectories. It substantially outperforms its base model and approaches GPT-5.4 across our custom simulation benchmark, LIBERO-PRO, and real-world manipulation, while substantially reducing inference latency and token usage.
