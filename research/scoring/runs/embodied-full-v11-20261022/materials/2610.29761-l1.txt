# OMAR: Open-World Multi-Attribute Reasoning for Scene-Conditioned Shelf Organization

## Abstract
With the advent of foundation models, robots are increasingly capable of understanding and executing explicitly specified human tasks. Extending these capabilities to open-ended tasks, however, requires robots to infer appropriate actions from the observed scene. Household organization exemplifies this challenge, as robots must often act without detailed instructions, predefined object vocabularies, or known object-receptacle associations. While robots may follow instructions or learn organization preferences from demonstrations, inferring the latent organization of an occasionally changing scene and placing a novel object consistently with it remains challenging. We propose an open-world multi-attribute reasoning pipeline that grounds unknown objects across views and constructs an instance-level 3D scene graph for placement reasoning. First, a vision-language model proposes labels together with attributes such as content and packaging type, which are grounded through segmentation, depth, and multi-view association. Then, an LLM reasons over this representation to infer the latent shelf organization and rank scene-consistent object placements without explicitly user-provided preferences. The resulting placement rankings guide subsequent geometric and kinematic feasibility checks for execution. Our experimental evaluation shows that our approach leads to improved instance mapping and placement coherence over single-view language-grounding and geometry-only baselines, enabling autonomous placement of previously unseen objects without predefined object-receptacle mappings or class-specific retraining.



## I Introduction
Households are constantly changing environments, and their organization reflects implicit and evolving human conventions. To effectively organize objects in such environments, service robots must act beyond explicitly specified tasks. When storing groceries, a robot must determine where a previously unseen object belongs, even without detailed instructions or user-provided preferences. As object locations change, predefined object vocabularies and object-receptacle mappings cannot fully specify appropriate placements. We study scene-conditioned shelf organization, in which the robot must infer the latent organization from the observed arrangement and place a novel object consistently into it. This task requires reasoning over multiple object attributes, including content, packaging type, edibility, and orientation. These attributes can suggest competing placements, and their relative importance depends on the organization of the particular scene. Category labels alone therefore provide insufficient context. Placement reasoning requires object-level descriptions grounded in the observed shelf. Multi-view grounding provides this object-level context by associating instances across views and revealing occluded objects. Fig. 1 : The robot’s task is to pick an unknown object from the table and find the semantically most coherent and geometrically feasible placement in the shelving unit across multiple shelf boards (top left). Our system creates a 3D representation from multiple views (bottom left) to predict placement poses considering multiple object attributes (right). Our approach combines open-world multi-attribute and multi-view instance mapping to generate a 3D semantic scene graph, which enables scene-conditioned placement reasoning with feasibility-aware grasp-placement planning to execute placements of previously unseen objects into space-limited shelving units. Vision-language models (VLMs) and large language models (LLMs) offer semantic knowledge beyond predefined object sets, supporting the prediction of semantically plausible placements [ 1 , 2 , 3 ] , but plausibility alone does not ensure geometric or kinematic feasibility. Joint pick-and-place pipelines address grasp-placement compatibility through task-aware 6-DoF grasp selection and feasibility evaluation [ 4 , 5 , 6 ] , but do not infer the shelf’s latent organization from semantic object attributes. The resulting challenge is to connect grounded, scene-dependent semantic reasoning with feasibility-aware planning so that placements of unseen objects preserve the observed organization and remain executable in confined shelf spaces. We address this gap via open-world, multi-attribute reasoning (OMAR), which places previously unseen objects consistently with the observed shelf organization. As shown in Fig. 1 , our pipeline grounds VLM-generated object descriptions across views in an instance-level 3D scene graph, capturing content, packaging type, edibility, and orientation rather than category labels alone. An LLM reasons jointly over these attributes and their scene-dependent relevance to infer the latent shelf organization and propose placements that preserve its coherence, without user-provided preferences, predefined object-receptacle mappings, or class-specific retraining. These proposals guide grasp prediction and geometric and kinematic feasibility checks, linking multi-attribute shelf coherence to executable placements. To summarize, the main contributions of our work are: • An open-world multi-attribute mapping pipeline that grounds unknown objects across views and creates an instance-level 3D scene graph for placement reasoning. • An LLM-based reasoning pipeline leveraging instance-level scene representation to infer the latent shelf organization and rank scene-consistent object placements. Our evaluation shows that our approach leads to improved instance mapping and placement coherence over single-view language-grounding and geometry-only baselines.
