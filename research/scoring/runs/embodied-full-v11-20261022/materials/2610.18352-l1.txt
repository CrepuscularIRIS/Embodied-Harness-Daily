# Let the Mind Conceive, Let the Hand Achieve: Following Multimodal Instructions for Long-Horizon Robotic Manipulation

## Abstract
A robot following a multi-step instruction needs to act on the right objects in the right order, even as the scene changes. It must also maintain awareness of task progress and determine when to advance. To address these challenges, we introduce Orchestra, an agent harness that separates task planning and execution management from action generation. Orchestra decomposes a complete multimodal instruction into ordered subtasks, tracks completed steps, and updates the locations of relevant objects and destinations during execution. It maintains a shared context containing the active subtask, spatial grounding, and recent observations. An action policy uses this context to generate actions, while a separately trained progress evaluator estimates when to advance to the next subtask. To evaluate whether robots trained on atomic instructions and short trajectories can execute longer subtask sequences, we introduce a benchmark named RMBench-Compose. Orchestra achieves a mean success rate of $68.22\%$ across nine RMBench tasks, outperforming Mem-0 by $26.22$ percentage points. On RMBench-Compose, it achieves an Average Success Length (ASL) of $7.35$ subtasks, $2.46$ times that of HarnessVLA. Across four real-world multi-step tasks, it improves mean ASL by $25.0\%$ over Orchestra using the base $π_{0.5}$ policy. These results show that Orchestra helps robots carry out longer sequences of subtasks while preserving the requested subtask order and acting on the specified objects. Our code and project page are available at https://youpengwen.github.io/Orchestra.

## 1 Introduction

Generalist robot policies and vision-language-action (VLA) models have expanded language-conditioned manipulation ( Brohan et al., 2023 ; Octo Model Team et al., 2024 ; Kim et al., 2024 ; Physical Intelligence et al., 2025 ) . While these advances improve local action capabilities, executing long-horizon tasks remains challenging because task progress must stay aligned with changing object states and goals. This raises a system-level question: how can an action policy execute a complete user instruction across a long sequence of interactions? Addressing this question requires coordination beyond local action generation, including maintaining the requested operation order, grounding objects and destinations, and managing subtask transitions. The first challenge is maintaining task continuity by tracking completed and pending operations while retaining relevant observations. Memory-based approaches combine short-horizon visual history with task-level information ( Torne et al., 2026 ) , and RMBench studies memory-dependent manipulation through its benchmark and Mem-0 policy ( Chen et al., 2026 ) . However, these memory mechanisms alone do not ensure that perceptual history and task records remain aligned with the active subtask throughout execution of a complete instruction. The second challenge is preserving instruction grounding: a mechanically successful action can still target the wrong object or destination ( Hou and Zhao, 2026 ) . Multimodal prompts and spatial representations support task specification and grounding ( Jiang et al., 2022 ; Huang et al., 2023 ) , but task-relevant grounding must be refreshed as objects move and goals change. Grounding and continuity are therefore coupled: each operation must target the intended entity at the correct point in the sequence. Robotic systems have increasingly drawn on language-model agent architectures to organize task execution. These architectures coordinate reasoning, action, and feedback ( Yao et al., 2023 ) , while their robotic applications incorporate affordances, execution feedback, and programmatic control ( Ahn et al., 2022 ; Huang et al., 2022 ; Liang et al., 2022 ) . More recently, Harness VLA uses a memory-guided agent to compose a frozen VLA with analytic manipulation primitives ( Zhang et al., 2026 ) . Building on this perspective, we focus on a shared execution context that aligns the active subtask, spatial grounding, observation history, and progress-driven transitions (Figure 1 ). Guided by the principle “Let the mind conceive, let the hand achieve” , we introduce Orchestra , an agent harness for task-level interpretation and execution management. Orchestra separates these functions from local action generation through a shared execution context. Given a complete multimodal instruction, it constructs ordered subtasks and maintains this context for the active subtask. Perception tools update task-relevant spatial grounding, while working memory preserves task records and recent observations. Moreover, we instantiate the action policy with an adapted \pi_{0.5} model ( Physical Intelligence et al., 2025 ) , conditioned on the active subtask, spatial prompts, recent RGB observations, and current proprioception. We also train an independent progress evaluator that uses aligned context to estimate subtask progress and guide transitions. At each subtask transition, the harness preserves task records while resetting local observation buffers and spatial grounding. We evaluate Orchestra on both simulated benchmarks and real-world manipulation tasks. The simulated benchmarks include RMBench ( Chen et al., 2026 ) and RMBench-Compose, which we introduce by adapting five RMBench environments to support variable-length, state-feasible compositions of atomic skills. On RMBench-Compose, the action policy is fine-tuned only on atomic instructions and short trajectories, while evaluation requires executing longer subtask sequences from complete instructions as object states change. Together, these evaluations assess memory-dependent manipulation, composition beyond the training horizon, and sustained execution on real hardware. Our contributions are threefold: • We introduce Orchestra , an agent harness that maintains a shared execution context to coordinate task interpretation, spatial grounding, working memory, and subtask transitions throughout complete instructions. • We adapt a VLA policy to combine subtask instructions, spatial prompts, and recent visual history, and train an independent progress evaluator to separate action generation from subtask-completion assessment. • We introduce RMBench-Compose to evaluate long-horizon task execution and evaluate Orchestra on RMBench, RMBench-Compose, and real-world manipulation tasks. Orchestra improves mean RMBench success rate by 26.22 percentage points over Mem-0 and achieves 2.46\times the Average Success Length of HarnessVLA on RMBench-Compose. On real-world tasks, it improves mean Average Success Length by 25.0\% over Orchestra with the base \pi_{0.5} policy.
