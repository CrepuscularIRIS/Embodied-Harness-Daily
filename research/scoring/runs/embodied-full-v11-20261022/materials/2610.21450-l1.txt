# X-Fly: Learning to Reach and Stop in Full-Chunk Aerial Vision-Language Navigation

## Abstract
Aerial vision-language navigation (VLN) agents built on large vision-language models are expensive to query. Executing a complete predicted action chunk before replanning amortizes this cost, reducing model inference from 195 to 42 ms per executed waypoint for our policy. It also moves the states at which the policy is queried away from expert demonstrations, which supervise neither recovery from these states nor termination at the learner's own arrival. On OpenFly test-seen, no-stop failures account for 47.78% of full-chunk behavior cloning (BC) outcomes. We present X-Fly, a staged training framework for full-chunk aerial VLN. Recovery DAgger labels learner-visited decision states with smooth chunks that reconnect to future expert segments and with STOP at the first in-region state. Flow-GRPO then jointly refines the chunked flow controller and a Bernoulli STOP policy from same-task closed-loop outcomes, supplemented by outcome-verified stopping supervision. With eight controls per decision, X-Fly reaches 74.18% SR on test-seen and 51.73% on test-unseen, compared with 16.45% and 18.43% for full-chunk BC and 32.04% and 29.46% for DreamFly.

## 1 Introduction

On test-seen, full-chunk BC reaches the goal but often does not stop. Recovery DAgger makes full-chunk execution viable, and Flow-GRPO, trained and evaluated at k{=}8 only, gives the highest SR among the evaluated operating points at 4.7 \times less amortized model inference time per executed waypoint than single-step execution. Inference timings apply to the X-Fly backbone; DreamFly [ 9 ] is shown as an SR reference at k{=}1 . Aerial VLN asks an embodied agent to follow a free-form instruction through a large urban environment and terminate within a target radius [ 19 , 11 ] . Compared with indoor graph navigation [ 1 , 6 ] , the agent must produce continuous translation and rotation, cope with large viewpoint changes, and recover from control errors that alter every subsequent observation. Success therefore depends on two coupled abilities: flying to the described goal, and deciding when the goal has been reached. Recent aerial agents condition on large vision-language models (VLMs), which makes every policy query expensive [ 9 , 35 ] . Action-chunk policies predict several future controls per query [ 34 , 8 ] , yet aerial VLN agents such as DreamFly execute only the first control and query again [ 9 ] . Executing the complete chunk amortizes policy inference across its controls: for our fast–slow policy, executing all eight cuts model inference from 195 to 42 ms per executed waypoint (Sec. 4.1 ). Full-chunk execution also changes where the policy is queried. The aircraft moves through eight controls before new visual feedback can correct it, so the states at which the policy is next queried, its decision anchors , drift away from the expert trajectories it was trained on. Expert-only data cover these learner-visited anchors poorly in two ways: they contain no chunk that starts at an off-route learner pose and returns it to the route, and their positive STOP labels are confined to expert terminal windows, leaving the anchors where the learner itself arrives poorly represented. We call this the full-chunk support gap . On test-seen, switching BC from single-step to full-chunk execution drops SR from 46.57% to 16.45% (Fig. 1 ), although navigation failures barely change (28.02% vs. 30.77%); no-stop failures account for 47.78% of the full-chunk outcomes (Sec. 4.3 ). Reaching the goal is not the same as stopping there. X-Fly addresses both sides of the gap with two training stages applied after multi-domain BC (Fig. 2 ). Recovery DAgger [ 24 ] runs the learner with the deployed full-chunk executor and labels every visited anchor with a smooth chunk that decays the learner’s offset and reconnects to the future expert segment; the first anchor inside the success region receives a positive STOP label. Recovery reduces navigation and no-stop failures, while premature stopping remains common. Flow-GRPO [ 18 ] then compares closed-loop attempts of the same task and updates the chunked flow controller and a separate Bernoulli STOP policy from their outcomes. With eight controls per decision, X-Fly reaches 74.18% SR on test-seen and 51.73% on test-unseen, all from explicit in-region STOP. Our contributions are: • We study full-chunk execution in aerial VLN and characterize the support gap it creates at learner-visited decision anchors, with separate effects on goal reaching and termination. • We present X-Fly , a staged training framework for full-chunk aerial navigation that combines multi-domain BC, recovery supervision at learner-visited anchors, and closed-loop outcome optimization of chunked control and STOP. • We demonstrate improved full-chunk navigation on OpenFly and characterize distinct stage-level gains: recovery improves goal-region reachability and reduces no-stop failures, while outcome post-training further reduces premature termination.
