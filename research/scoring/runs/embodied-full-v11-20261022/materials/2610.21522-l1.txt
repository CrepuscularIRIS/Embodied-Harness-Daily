# Multi-Teacher On-Policy Distillation for Flow-Based Vision-Language-Action Models

## Abstract
Specialized vision-language-action (VLA) policies acquire strong domain-specific skills, yet consolidating these skills into one shared policy remains challenging. On-policy distillation (OPD) transfers teacher knowledge on inputs induced by the student, addressing the distribution mismatch encountered during deployment. For flow-based VLAs, this mismatch arises at two levels: environment observations and intermediate action-generation states. We propose a two-level on-policy distillation framework that couples student-driven environment interaction with teacher velocity supervision along student-generated action flow trajectories. Frozen domain specialists jointly supervise one student. After each update, the student collects new environment experience and generates fresh action-flow trajectories. Starting from a joint supervised fine-tuning (SFT) initialization, Velocity Loss OPD yields absolute success-rate gains of 22.55% on LIBERO-40, 33.4% on CALVIN five-instruction sequences, and 22.6% on Meta-World MT50. Compared with Action Loss OPD and Teacher-rollout SFT, it achieves absolute success-rate gains of 6.4-9.5% on LIBERO-40, 9.7-18.3% on CALVIN, and 3.2-4.2% on Meta-World MT50. Analysis by sequence length further shows larger success-rate gains on longer CALVIN sequences. These results demonstrate effective multi-domain capability consolidation through two-level on-policy supervision, with a single student deployed across domains.

## 1 Introduction
Vision-language-action (VLA) models map visual observations and language instructions to robot actions. Generalist policies such as OpenVLA, \pi_{0} , and \pi_{0.5} provide a foundation for learning diverse manipulation skills [ 14 , 2 , 26 ] . Further specialization can improve individual domains, leaving useful capabilities distributed across separate policies. Multi-teacher consolidation seeks to transfer these capabilities into one student whose learned parameters are shared across domains. Across LIBERO, Meta-World, and CALVIN, the student must accommodate distinct observation and native action interfaces while integrating specialist supervision through the same learned parameters. Teacher-generated data provide a direct route to capability transfer [ 34 , 32 ] . During deployment, however, the student may encounter observations that differ from the teachers’ demonstrations: a missed grasp changes both the next observation and the recovery action required. On-policy distillation (OPD) obtains teacher guidance at inputs induced by the student, following the principle of interactive imitation learning [ 28 , 1 , 38 ] . For flow-based policies, the choice of supervision also extends inside action generation. A teacher can supply a final action chunk or local velocity predictions at intermediate states along the student’s noise-to-action trajectory. Flow-based VLA consolidation therefore involves two evolving input distributions. The student’s executed actions determine which environment observations it encounters, while its velocity field determines which intermediate states it traverses during action generation. Guidance on teacher demonstrations can leave both distributions mismatched to the deployed student. Recent work develops student-trajectory supervision for continuous generators and robot policies [ 8 , 25 , 20 ] . For cross-domain consolidation, the central design question is how to couple teacher guidance to the shared student’s behavior at both levels as its capabilities evolve. We propose a two-level on-policy distillation framework for consolidating domain-specialized flow-based VLAs into a shared student. The framework couples student-driven environment interaction with teacher velocity supervision along student-generated action flow trajectories, aligning supervision with the evolving student at both the environment and action-generation levels. Each frozen specialist supplies local velocity targets in its domain. We aggregate weighted domain-loss gradients to update the student once, then use the updated policy to collect new environment experience and action-flow trajectories. The student shares learned parameters while retaining the preprocessing, normalization, and action decoding required by each domain interface. Teachers are used only during training. Our contributions are threefold: • We introduce a two-level on-policy framework that consolidates domain-specialized flow-based VLAs through teacher supervision on student-induced environment and action-generation states. • We demonstrate cross-domain capability consolidation in a shared \pi_{0.5} student. It outperforms the SFT initialization, Action Loss OPD, and Teacher-rollout SFT on all aggregate metrics across LIBERO, Meta-World, and CALVIN. • We characterize the roles of supervision targets and joint input sources through matched comparisons. Learning curves establish persistent advantages over endpoint matching, while suite-level results and domain-weight comparisons identify how transferred capabilities are distributed across tasks.
