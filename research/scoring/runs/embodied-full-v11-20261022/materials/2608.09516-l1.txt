# HarnessWAM: Bridging Prediction and Deliberation in World Action Models

## Abstract
World Action Models~(WAMs) jointly learn environmental dynamics and robot actions, introducing priors over physical evolution into embodied control. However, finite-horizon prediction and action generation are insufficient for complex embodied tasks that require global planning, cross-stage state maintenance, execution verification, and failure recovery. We refer to this mismatch as the prediction-deliberation gap. To address this gap, we propose HarnessWAM, an agentic framework for WAMs. HarnessWAM employs a vision-language-model-based Task Manager to maintain an evidence-grounded scene belief and organize the global instruction into a structured task graph. Capability-conditioned executable-space projection then compiles the graph into WAM-supported skill sequences that satisfy task dependencies and embodiment-state constraints. During execution, HarnessWAM couples a lightweight progress estimator with event-triggered Task Manager deliberation. The estimator supplies frequent execution cues, while the Task Manager uses observations, task state, and interaction history to verify outcomes and decide whether to advance, acquire evidence, revise the plan, or recover. Local recovery preserves acquired scene knowledge so that execution can resume after failure. HarnessWAM achieves state-of-the-art average performance on RoboMemArena, with full-task and subtask success rates of 64.4% and 76.8%, respectively, and reaches 23.7% success on RoboCerebra Ideal. On a physical UR3 arm, full-task success reaches 95% for shape-conditioned tabletop tidying and 90% for memory-dependent box exploration. These results demonstrate the effectiveness of model-external agentic orchestration in bridging the prediction-deliberation gap for complex embodied tasks.

## 1 Introduction
World Action Models (WAMs) jointly model future observations and robot actions, connecting predictions of physical dynamics to embodied control ( Li et al., 2026 ; Ye et al., 2026 ) . These learned dynamics support manipulation and generalization to unfamiliar tasks and environments ( Ye et al., 2026 ) . However, complex tasks require decisions that extend beyond the model’s prediction horizon. Placing a cookie in a non-empty drawer requires inspecting drawers, remembering their contents after closure, selecting the target, and verifying placement. A failed grasp further requires recovering the robot and resuming execution while retaining the evidence identifying the drawer. These requirements connect local action generation to persistent state, planning, verification, and recovery. We refer to this mismatch between predictive control and task-level deliberation as the prediction-deliberation gap , illustrated in Figure 1 . Recent efforts address aspects of this gap through persistent visual memory ( Yang et al., 2026 ; Wang et al., 2026 ) and hierarchical skill prediction ( Sun et al., 2026 ) . These mechanisms provide policies with historical context and temporal structure for skill execution. However, access to history and skill predictions does not ensure that target selection is grounded in observations, that operations satisfy their prerequisites, or that attempted actions achieve their intended effects. Language-model agents suggest a harness that maintains state and incorporates feedback across model calls ( Yao et al., 2022 ; Shinn et al., 2023 ; Yang et al., 2024 ) . However, extending these harnesses to robots requires handling incomplete observations, physical constraints, and uncertain action outcomes. An embodied harness must therefore preserve task knowledge, ground semantic plans in supported skills, and verify physical effects before advancing execution. Figure 1: The prediction-deliberation gap. Vanilla WAM denotes direct execution of the global instruction without external task state. HarnessWAM retains drawer observations, binds the target after occlusion, and coordinates skill execution, verification, and recovery. To address this gap, we introduce HarnessWAM , an agentic framework for WAMs. HarnessWAM employs a vision-language-model (VLM) Task Manager to maintain evidence-grounded scene belief and construct a task graph containing physical and cognitive operations. The graph includes evidence acquisition before unresolved targets are bound, allowing later skills to use targets identified through exploration. Capability-conditioned executable-space projection then compiles the graph into validated WAM skills, checking dependencies against represented scene and robot constraints. Since admissible plans can still fail during execution, HarnessWAM couples a lightweight progress estimator with event-triggered semantic verification. Verification updates the task state and determines whether to advance, observe, revise, or recover. Revised suffixes undergo projection while executed history remains fixed. Recovery returns the robot toward its saved configuration while retaining evidence for subsequent decisions. The same task state therefore connects initial planning to verification, revision, and recovery throughout execution. HarnessWAM achieves state-of-the-art performance on RoboMemArena, with full-task and subtask success rates of 64.4% and 76.8%, and reaches 23.7% SR on RoboCerebra Ideal. Gains with FastWAM further demonstrate applicability across WAM backbones. On a UR3 arm, full-task success reaches 95% for tabletop tidying and 90% for box exploration. Our main contributions are: • We identify the prediction-deliberation gap and introduce a WAM harness that connects local predictive control to persistent task-level deliberation. • Evidence-grounded task graphs link target binding to information acquisition; capability-conditioned projection checks initial and revised plans against execution constraints. • An event-driven runtime uses semantic verification to govern graph transitions, preserving executed history and acquired evidence across plan revision and recovery. • State-of-the-art results across RoboMemArena, RoboCerebra Ideal, and UR3 experiments demonstrate the effectiveness of HarnessWAM.
