# From Reasoning to Action: Benchmarking Frontier AI Models on LIBERO-10 Robot Manipulation Tasks

## Abstract
Recent advances in frontier AI models have demonstrated remarkable capabilities in visual understanding, reasoning, planning, and tool use, raising the possibility of using general-purpose AI models for robot manipulation without task-specific training. However, their manipulation capabilities have not been systematically evaluated on standardized robotics benchmarks. In this report, we present a unified closed-loop manipulation framework for evaluating frontier AI models on the LIBERO-10 benchmark. Our framework enables models to control a robot directly through visual observations, robot proprioception, and structured tool calls, primarily by specifying target end-effector poses and gripper commands, without task-specific fine-tuning or pretrained manipulation skills. We evaluate six frontier AI models, including GPT-6 Astra, GPT-6.1 Sol, GPT-6 Luna, Claude Opus 5, Claude Opus 5.5, and Gemini-4 Argon, across three reasoning effort levels, resulting in 18 configurations and 1,800 evaluation episodes. Our results show that GPT-6 Astra achieves the highest success rate of 44%, followed by Gemini-4 Argon at 43%. Increasing reasoning effort generally improves manipulation performance, but the benefits vary across models and come at additional inference cost. We also observe substantial differences across tasks, with models performing relatively well on object placement tasks but struggling with precise spatial arrangements and complex multi-step interactions. These findings demonstrate both the potential and limitations of frontier AI models for robot manipulation, highlighting the challenges of translating reasoning capabilities into reliable physical actions. We release our evaluation framework and experimental results to support reproducible benchmarking and future research on reasoning-based robot manipulation.



## 1 Introduction
Recent advances in frontier artificial intelligence (AI) models have demonstrated remarkable capabilities in visual understanding, reasoning, planning, and tool use Arena AI [2026] . In particular, recent demonstrations of GPT-6 Astra have shown impressive robot manipulation behaviors, including interpreting visual observations, generating robot actions, and correcting mistakes through closed-loop interaction Zhang et al. [2026] , Su et al. [2026] , Team et al. [2026] . NVIDIA’s Astranomy report Yang et al. [2026a] further demonstrates the potential of frontier AI models for robot manipulation through different control configurations, including direct action generation, agentic tool use, and supervision of pretrained robot policies. These results suggest that general-purpose AI models may be capable of performing robot manipulation without task-specific policy training. Despite these promising demonstrations, an important question remains: How well do frontier AI models perform on established robot manipulation benchmarks? Existing evaluations often use different tasks, robot platforms, and control interfaces, making systematic comparisons difficult. Connecting frontier AI models to standardized benchmarks is essential for understanding their capabilities, identifying limitations, and comparing their performance with conventional robot learning approaches. Motivated by this question, we develop a unified manipulation pipeline to evaluate frontier AI models on LIBERO-10 (also known as LIBERO-Long) Liu et al. [2023] , a subset of LIBERO-100 containing ten held-out, long-horizon manipulation tasks. LIBERO-10 has been widely adopted for evaluating robot learning methods, including generalist robot policies and vision-language-action models such as Octo Team et al. [2024] , OpenVLA Kim et al. [2024] , \pi_{0.5} Intelligence et al. [2025] , UniVLA Bu et al. [2025] and UniAct Zheng et al. [2025] . Built upon the open-source Inspect Robots framework Robocurve [2026] , our pipeline enables frontier AI models to interact with the simulation environment through visual observations and structured tool calls, allowing closed-loop manipulation without task-specific training or fine-tuning. We evaluate multiple frontier AI models, including GPT and Claude models, under a common protocol, exploring different reasoning modes to investigate the impact of reasoning effort on manipulation performance. Beyond task success rates, we analyze API calls, inference latency, token usage, and monetary cost to understand the tradeoffs between reasoning capabilities, manipulation performance, and efficiency. Our work provides a standardized evaluation of frontier AI models on robot manipulation tasks, offering insights into their capabilities and limitations. By releasing our code and evaluation results, we provide reproducible, ready-to-use baselines for future research, reducing the need to repeatedly evaluate these computationally expensive models and lowering evaluation costs.
