# RoboFAC: A Comprehensive Framework for Robotic Failure Analysis and Correction

## Abstract
Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models have recently advanced robotic manipulation by translating natural-language instructions and visual observations into control actions. However, existing VLAs are primarily trained on successful expert demonstrations and lack structured supervision for failure diagnosis and recovery, limiting robustness in open-world scenarios. To address this limitation, we propose the Robotic Failure Analysis and Correction (RoboFAC) framework. We construct a large-scale failure-centric dataset comprising 9,440 erroneous manipulation trajectories and 78,623 QA pairs across 53 scenes in both simulation and real-world environments, with systematically categorized failure types. Leveraging this dataset, we develop a lightweight multimodal model specialized for task understanding, failure analysis, and failure correction, enabling efficient local deployment while remaining competitive with large proprietary models. Experimental results demonstrate that RoboFAC achieves a 34.1% higher failure analysis accuracy compared to GPT-4o. Furthermore, we integrated RoboFAC as an external supervisor in a real-world VLA control pipeline, yielding a 29.1% relative improvement across four tasks while significantly reducing latency relative to GPT-4o. These results demonstrate that RoboFAC enables systematic failure diagnosis and recovery, significantly enhancing VLA recovery capabilities. Our model and dataset are publicly available at https://github.com/MINT-SJTU/RoboFAC.

## 1 Introduction
Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models have demonstrated impressive generalization in robotic manipulation [ 1 , 2 , 3 , 4 , 5 , 6 , 7 ] . By grounding language instructions into actions via visual feedback, these models handle various tasks. However, in complex, long-horizon scenarios, physical disturbances and execution errors are almost inevitable. Because most VLA models are trained predominantly on expert demonstrations, they lack critical exposure to failure trajectories. Consequently, when an anomaly occurs, these models lack the intrinsic capability to detect the error, diagnose its root cause, and re-plan effectively, often resulting in brittle behaviors and cascading failures [ 8 , 9 , 10 ] . While general-purpose Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) show strong reasoning capabilities, directly deploying them as external critics is challenging: First, they often falter in specialized robotic domains because they are not trained on fine-grained, physics-grounded manipulation failures [ 15 , 11 , 16 , 17 ] . Second, their massive parameter scale and high API latency severely hinder real-time, on-device deployment for reactive control [ 18 , 19 ] . Although existing specialized datasets for robot failure analysis [ 12 , 13 , 14 ] attempt to alleviate some domain-shift issues, they remain constrained by simplistic task setups, coarse-grained diagnostics, and a lack of multi-level corrective strategies (Table 1 ). To overcome these limitations, we introduce RoboFAC , a unified framework for robotic failure analysis and correction. As illustrated in Figure 1 , the foundation of our approach is the RoboFAC Dataset , a large-scale collection of manipulation failures spanning both simulated and real-world environments. To ensure broad coverage of robotic failure modes, we define a taxonomy of six fundamental error types, spanning multiple levels from task planning and motion planning to execution control . This structured decomposition enables the dataset to capture diverse and representative failure patterns, providing a strong foundation for training MLLMs to analyze and reason about errors. Leveraging this structured dataset, we develop the RoboFAC model , a lightweight MLLM tailored for failure video reasoning. By explicitly training on our hierarchical data, this open-source model seamlessly integrates task understanding, failure diagnosis, and corrective reasoning, overcoming the limitations of base models lacking failure-specific priors. Extensive empirical evaluations demonstrate the efficacy of our framework. Despite its compact 7B parameter scale, RoboFAC model substantially outperforms its pre-trained base model and achieves performance comparable to—and in some scenarios surpassing—proprietary general-purpose models like GPT-4o in failure reasoning. Crucially, when integrated as an external critic in real-world VLA pipelines, RoboFAC not only improves task success rates through effective failure recovery, but also reduces inference latency by approximately 3\times compared to GPT-4o-based systems, enabling highly responsive, on-device deployment. Our primary contributions are summarized as follows: 1. We introduce the RoboFAC Dataset , a large-scale, hierarchically annotated video QA dataset comprising 78K pairs across eight distinct question types. It systematically categorizes failures across the control hierarchy and incorporates diverse visual variations to support robust, comprehensive failure understanding and correction. 2. We develop the RoboFAC model , a lightweight, deployable MLLM specialized for robotic failure reasoning. It serves as a unified critic for real-time failure detection and recovery in VLA systems. 3. We demonstrate that integrating RoboFAC into real-world manipulation pipelines yields higher recovery success rates and a 3\times reduction in inference latency compared to state-of-the-art proprietary models like GPT-4o.
