# Goal2Skill: Long-Horizon Manipulation with Adaptive Planning and Reflection

## Abstract
Recent vision-language-action (VLA) systems have demonstrated strong capabilities in embodied manipulation. However, most existing VLA policies rely on limited observation windows and end-to-end action prediction, which makes them brittle in long-horizon, memory-dependent tasks with partial observability, occlusions, and multi-stage dependencies. Such tasks require not only precise visuomotor control, but also persistent memory, adaptive task decomposition, and explicit recovery from execution failures. To address these limitations, we propose a dual-system framework for long-horizon embodied manipulation. Our framework explicitly separates high-level semantic reasoning from low-level motor execution. A high-level planner, implemented as a VLM-based agentic module, maintains structured task memory and performs goal decomposition, outcome verification, and error-driven correction. A low-level executor, instantiated as a VLA-based visuomotor controller, carries out each sub-task through diffusion-based action generation conditioned on geometry-preserving filtered observations. Together, the two systems form a closed loop between planning and execution, enabling memory-aware reasoning, adaptive replanning, and robust online recovery. Experiments on representative RMBench tasks show that the proposed framework substantially outperforms representative baselines, achieving a 32.4% average success rate compared with 9.8% for the strongest baseline. Ablation studies further confirm the importance of structured memory and closed-loop recovery for long-horizon manipulation.

## 1 Introduction
Robotic foundation models advance the range of manipulation tasks that robots can perform from natural-language instructions. Vision-language-action (VLA) models connect visual and linguistic representations to motor control, transferring semantic knowledge from pretrained models and manipulation skills from robot demonstrations ( Kawaharazuka et al., 2025 ; Ma et al., 2024 ; Black et al., 2024 ; Kim et al., 2024 ) . More recently, world-action models (WAMs) and video-based policies couple action generation with predictions of future observations, leveraging spatiotemporal priors to capture physical dynamics and support anticipatory control ( Ye et al., 2026 ; Kim et al., 2026 ) . These advances improve the generalization of learned behavior. Nevertheless, long-horizon tasks remain challenging because they require coordinating interdependent operations: retaining relevant information across interactions, verifying which outcomes have been achieved, and adapting subsequent decisions when execution changes the assumptions underlying an earlier plan. Long-horizon manipulation requires coordinating planning, memory, and verification under partial observability and uncertain execution ( Zhou et al., 2023 ; Chen et al., 2026a ; Li et al., 2023 ; Li et al., 2026b ; Han et al., 2026 ) . An initial plan may need revision as conditions change, information required by later steps may become unavailable, and executed actions may fail during execution. As shown in Figure 1 , a robot asked to scan a Coke can must first remember which side carries the barcode. After the can is moved to the scanner, verification reveals that the scan has failed because the barcode is not visible. Reflection attributes the failure to a wrong orientation rather than a failed grasp, so the system keeps the completed pick and move steps and rewrites only the remaining plan to rotate, align, and rescan the can. Such adaptations must combine current evidence with task history to revise the remaining plan while preserving valid progress. This illustrates the central challenge: deciding whether to continue, advance to the next sub-task, or revise the plan. Recent dual-system architectures and related embodied frameworks offer a promising foundation by combining high-level reasoning with low-level execution, where a high-level model interprets goals and guides execution through plans, language subgoals, or skill invocations, while a low-level policy or controller realizes these decisions through physical interaction ( Team et al., 2025 ; Li et al., 2026b ; Zhang et al., 2026 ; Chen et al., 2026b ) . However, separating planning from execution does not ensure reliable adaptation as task conditions change. The system must retain context for future decisions and verify execution outcomes to determine when and how to revise the plan. Crucially, the evidence needed to establish success varies across tasks and scenes, motivating a self-evolving verifier that refines its assessment criteria and procedures through validated execution feedback. This motivates coupling planning and proactive memory with evolving execution verification, so that improved progress assessment supports more reliable plan advancement and targeted correction. We introduce Goal2Skill , a dual-system framework that couples adaptive planning , proactive memory , and execution verification for long-horizon manipulation. A high-level VLM brain decomposes the goal into sub-tasks, while a low-level VLA executes them through continuous visuomotor control. Periodic verification assesses task progress and determines whether to continue execution, advance to the next sub-task, or trigger reflection for local correction and replanning. Task memory supports these decisions by preserving information needed by upcoming steps and updating task context from execution outcomes. Together, these mechanisms enable the brain to adapt its plan while retaining valid progress and accounting for previous attempts. Central to this coordination is a self-evolving verifier that governs task progression by determining when to continue execution, advance to the next step, or trigger correction. It combines lightweight tools, rule-based checks, and VLM reasoning to balance reliability and computational cost. Execution feedback informs iterative refinements to its assessment criteria and checking procedures, with candidate updates validated before adoption. Operating between frequent VLA execution and sparse planning, the verifier provides timely feedback for reflection and plan revision while adapting progress assessment to different tasks and scenes. We evaluate Goal2Skill on RoboMME and RMBench tasks spanning diverse manipulation settings. It achieves the highest average success rate with particularly strong gains on tasks requiring persistent memory and multi-stage coordination. Qualitative analyses illustrate how execution feedback refines verification procedures and how reflection guides replanning when execution invalidates the initial plan. Our contributions are threefold: • We introduce an adaptive planning mechanism that combines initial task decomposition with evidence-guided retries, local adjustments, and revision of the remaining plan. • We develop proactive task memory with a self-evolving verifier to guide task progression and refine verification through execution feedback. • Experiments on RoboMME and RMBench show that Goal2Skill achieves the highest average success rate with strong gains on tasks requiring persistent memory and multi-stage coordination.
