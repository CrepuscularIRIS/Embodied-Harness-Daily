# DexHoldem: An Agentic Robotics Benchmark for Dexterous Manipulation in Texas Hold'em

## Abstract
Evaluating embodied systems with real dexterous hardware requires more than isolated motor-skill tests: an agent must perceive a changing scene (e.g. a tabletop), choose a context-appropriate action, execute it with a dexterous hand, and leave the scene usable for later decisions. We introduce DexHoldem, a comprehensive real-world benchmark evaluating Texas Hold'em related dexterous manipulations with a ShadowHand. DexHoldem provides 1,470 teleoperated demonstrations across 14 Texas Hold'em manipulation primitives, a standardized physical policy benchmark, and an agentic perception benchmark that tests whether agents can recover the structured game state needed for embodied decision making. On primitive execution, $π_{0.5}$ obtains the highest task completion rate ($61.2\%$), while $π_{0.5}$ and $π_0$ tie on scene-preserving success rate ($47.5\%$). On agentic perception, Opus 5.5 narrowly leads on both strict problem-level accuracy ($49.1\%$) and average field-wise accuracy ($80.6\%$); the gap between the two exposes the distance between isolated visual sub-capabilities and complete routing-relevant state recovery. Finally, we instantiate the full embodied-agent loop with one agent--policy pairing over 33 closed-loop hand-level rollouts, in which only $12.1\%$ of hands complete; retries restore the failed primitive in 12 of 34 dispatches and resolve prolonged execution stalls in three of the four completed hands, which would otherwise have required manual termination. Only one hand completes with neither a retry nor a human-help request. DexHoldem therefore evaluates dexterous tabletop execution, agentic perception, and embodied decision routing in a shared physical setting. Project website: https://dexholdem.github.io/Dexholdem/

## 1 Introduction
Figure 1: Overview of DexHoldem , a real-world Texas Hold’em benchmark for dexterous manipulation. (a) The policy uses top-down, third-person, and wrist-mounted views; a separate dedicated camera supplies the agent view. (b) The system closes the loop by parsing observations into game state, routing instructions, and executing policies. (c,d) Policy and agent benchmarks (the latter for a subset of perceivers) show that current models still struggle with contact-rich manipulation and fine-grained visual-state grounding. Recent advances in robotics and embodied agents have expanded the range of behaviors that can be learned and evaluated, including instruction following and long-horizon task composition ( Zitkovich et al., 2023 ; Chi et al., 2023 ; Intelligence et al., 2025 ; Driess et al., 2023 ; Huang et al., 2022 ; Huang et al., 2023 ; Liang et al., 2023 ; Liu et al., 2023 ; Chen et al., 2025 ; Zhang et al., 2024b ; Mees et al., 2022 ) . Yet evaluating these systems in realistic physical environments remains difficult. Existing embodied-agent benchmarks ( Srivastava et al., 2021 ; James et al., 2019 ; Yu et al., 2020 ; Nasiriany et al., 2024 ) have advanced evaluation of language grounding ( Liu et al., 2023 ; Mu et al., 2025 ; Chen et al., 2025 ) and planning ( Zhang et al., 2024b ; Mees et al., 2022 ; Mu et al., 2025 ) , but many still rely on simulation, coarse action spaces, or gripper-centric manipulation ( Chen et al., 2025 ; Liu et al., 2023 ; Mu et al., 2025 ; Mu et al., 2021 ) . Consequently, these scores provide limited evidence for grounding instructions in physical scenes while executing precise, real-world multi-finger manipulation. Benchmarks for dexterous manipulation address a complementary aspect of this problem by advancing contact-rich manipulation ( Chen et al., 2022 ; Kumar et al., 2023 ; Fu et al., 2021 ; Bao et al., 2023 ; Tao et al., 2025 ) , grasping ( Chen et al., 2024 ; Wang et al., 2025b ; Turpin et al., 2023 ; Ye et al., 2025 ; Zhang et al., 2024a ) , and in-hand manipulation ( Charlesworth and Montana, 2021 ; Cruciani et al., 2020 ) . However, these benchmarks typically evaluate motor competence through isolated low-level skills rather than instruction-conditioned tasks that also require visual grounding, sequential state awareness, and progress verification ( Ye et al., 2025 ; Chen et al., 2024 ; Wang et al., 2025b ) . Consequently, existing evaluation paradigms remain incomplete in complementary ways: embodied-agent benchmarks ( Yang et al., 2025 ; Ju et al., 2026 ; Wang et al., 2025a ) often under-emphasize real-world dexterous execution, whereas dexterous manipulation benchmarks often lack the task structure needed to assess instruction-driven embodied behavior. To evaluate this coupled setting, we use Texas Hold’em tabletop interaction, where semantic grounding, state tracking, and dexterous control are jointly necessary. Cards and chips define structured targets: a policy must identify a specific card or chip denomination and manipulate it at a designated location. Thin cards ( \sim 0.3 mm) and chips require contact-rich control under friction and disturbance uncertainty. Each action changes the scene, allowing perception, routing, execution, and recovery errors to compound. The domain therefore provides a controlled test of instruction-conditioned dexterous manipulation rather than general poker intelligence. Based on this rationale, we introduce DexHoldem , a real-world ShadowHand ( Shadow Robot Company, 2025a ) benchmark for Texas Hold’em tabletop manipulation. As summarized in Figure 1 , it provides 1,470 demonstrations across 14 card and chip primitives, a shared physical policy evaluation, a contextual state-parsing benchmark, and closed-loop system evaluation. The policy benchmark measures execution conditioned on a fixed task vocabulary; it does not independently establish generalization to unseen language or disentangle visual grounding from learned spatial priors. The physical evaluation separates completion of the requested primitive from preservation of the surrounding scene. Its four outcome categories distinguish successful execution with or without disruption from failed execution with or without disruption. The scoring rules in Section B.2 specify playable-region, unintended-overlap, and non-target-displacement criteria. This separation reveals failures that task completion alone would conceal, while the perception and system evaluations examine state parsing and the composition of primitives in the same environment. The shared interfaces and scoring protocols allow future policies and perceivers to be compared at the component level and then evaluated together in closed-loop interaction. We evaluate nine policies on an 80-trial schedule each, 720 trials in total. The highest observed TCR is 61.2\% , whereas the best SPSR is 47.5\% . On 36 perception problems, the best exact match over applicable scored fields is 49.1\% and the best field-wise average is 80.6\% ; community-card accuracy peaks at 61.5\% . Over 33 closed-loop rollouts pairing GPT 5.5 with \pi_{0} , only 12.1\% of hands complete; retries restore the failed primitive in 12 of 34 dispatches. In three of the four completed hands, closed-loop retries resolve prolonged stalls that would otherwise require manual termination; only one hand completes with neither a retry nor a human-help request. In summary, DexHoldem makes the following contributions: 1. We collect a real-world Texas Hold’em dexterous manipulation dataset with 1,470 real-world teleoperated demonstrations, covering 14 Texas Hold’em manipulation primitives. 2. We introduce a real-world dexterous hand policy benchmark that trains and evaluates policy models on these demonstrations under a shared multi-view observation–action interface and a scene-preservation-aware physical scoring rubric. 3. We introduce an agentic perception benchmark that evaluates whether embodied agents can visually parse structured tabletop game state for downstream decision routing. 4. We provide a system-level evaluation over 33 closed-loop hand-level rollouts, with three case studies, and an empirical analysis of RDT fine-tuning dynamics, exposing failure modes in scene-preserving execution, chip-state perception, and long-horizon reliability.
