# JoyInside-VLM-Agent: Product-Conditioned Multimodal Policy Learning for Embodied Agents

## Abstract
Embodied agents must translate multimodal observations and user requests into executable actions while adapting to product constraints, changing goals, and execution feedback. We present JoyInside-VLM-Agent, a ReAct-style multimodal agent developed through product-conditioned trajectory construction and staged policy learning for robotic platforms and smart hardware. A closed-loop sandbox generates multimodal interaction trajectories covering goal revisions and failure recovery, which are checked against execution outcomes and semantic criteria. Supervised fine-tuning and multi-teacher on-policy distillation precede reinforcement learning with DPER (Dual-Source Privileged On-Policy Self-Distillation with Token-Level Evidence Routing). DPER routes token-level evidence between a frozen privileged teacher and a privileged student sharing the rollout policy's parameters, modulating reward-derived token advantages while preserving their signs. Training rewards combine generative process evaluation, multi-judge rankings, and task-queue consistency. JoyInside-VLM-Agent achieves a BFCL v4 overall score of 77.44, exceeding the evaluated baselines; results on the other public benchmarks are mixed. On JoyInside-Devices and JoyInside-Robotics, the evaluated system, which includes domain-specific skill refinement, leads the baselines on all reported metrics. Two real-world quadruped case studies illustrate visually grounded search, replanning after execution failure, and explicit failure reporting.

## 1 Introduction
Embodied agents deployed on robotic platforms and smart hardware must translate multimodal observations and user instructions into executable behavior. Across products ranging from bipedal, quadruped, and wheeled robots to AI-enabled plush toys, the appropriate response to a given request depends on the agent’s role, capabilities, and operating constraints. Changes in user goals, new visual evidence, and execution failures can further invalidate earlier plans. We study how to train a shared multimodal policy that adapts to product specifications and revises its decisions as interactions unfold. Multimodal training has demonstrated positive transfer across tasks and robotic embodiments [ 1 , 2 ] , while context-aware systems use user intent and environmental feedback to guide interaction [ 3 , 4 ] . However, reported failures include actions beyond device capabilities and descriptions that no longer match the user’s current scene or intent [ 3 , 4 ] . These observations motivate training policies that combine transferable capabilities with adherence to product constraints and sustained alignment with the user’s current goal. This objective imposes two requirements on training. The data must cover diverse product configurations, goal revisions, and failure recovery while preserving the correspondence between actions and observations. The learning signal must reflect reasoning quality, compliance with product constraints, and progress toward the current goal. Trajectory-level rewards summarize these criteria but do not specify how to distribute credit across tokens. Using additional evidence during training therefore requires selecting an appropriate source for each token and controlling how that evidence affects policy updates. We present JoyInside-VLM-Agent, a multimodal agent developed through product-conditioned data construction and staged policy learning. Our pipeline combines product roles, workflows, and constraints with available tools and skills to build executable environments. A closed-loop sandbox generates multimodal interaction trajectories, which undergo execution checks and semantic validation. Multi-source supervised fine-tuning establishes behavior consistent with product specifications, while multi-expert on-policy distillation integrates complementary language and visual capabilities. Reinforcement learning then refines the policy using interaction-specific rewards. To guide reinforcement learning, we combine process-quality scores from a Generative Process Reward Model (GPRM), multi-judge rankings of the same candidate trajectories, and task-queue assessments of goal alignment and cross-turn continuity. For token-level policy optimization, we introduce DPER (Dual-Source Privileged On-Policy Self-Distillation with Token-Level Evidence Routing), building on RLSD’s sign-preserving advantage modulation [ 5 ] and DOPD’s dual-source routing [ 6 ] . A frozen privileged teacher and a privileged student sharing the rollout policy’s parameters score tokens along the same student-generated trajectory. DPER selects evidence based on their disagreement and support for each sampled token. The selected evidence rescales token-level advantages while preserving their reward-derived signs. Privileged information is used only during training. We evaluate JoyInside-VLM-Agent on public tool-use benchmarks and the proprietary JoyInside-Devices and JoyInside-Robotics datasets. The model achieves a BFCL v4 overall score of 77.44, outperforming the evaluated baselines. Case studies on a physical quadruped robot provide qualitative evidence of visually grounded search, replanning in response to execution feedback, and honest failure reporting. Our contributions are as follows: • Product-conditioned trajectory construction. We compose executable product configurations and generate verified multimodal trajectories covering product variation, goal revisions, and failure recovery. • DPER for policy optimization. We route token-level evidence between two privileged sources to scale token advantages while preserving their reward-derived signs. • Multi-source interaction rewards. We combine process evaluation, multi-model rankings, and task-queue consistency into a learning signal for reasoning quality, adherence to product constraints, and alignment with evolving goals. • JoyInside-VLM-Agent and its evaluation. We integrate these components into a unified training framework and assess the resulting model through public benchmarks and proprietary evaluation sets and qualitative quadruped case studies.
