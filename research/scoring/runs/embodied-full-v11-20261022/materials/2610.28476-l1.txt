# CLOSER: Evidence-Aware Active Visual Verification for Embodied Object-Goal Navigation

## Abstract
Object-goal navigation (ObjectNav) requires an embodied agent to find an instance of a specified category in an unseen environment and stop near it. The agent typically relies on an object detector but must judge online from a limited egocentric video stream, sometimes a single frame, whether a detected candidate is the target. Detectors often confidently mistake similar objects, such as a sofa for a bed, and stopping at a false positive fails the episode. Prevailing systems also approach candidates greedily, leaving them occluded or partially observed. We present CLOSER, an evidence-aware active verification framework that treats stopping as closed-loop evidence acquisition, coupling vision-language model (VLM) verification with active perception. CLOSER fine-tunes an open-source VLM to label each view of a candidate tracked across frames as target, non-target, or uncertain, selects complementary views with an embedding-based diversity gate, and fuses their per-view probabilities with a lightweight network. If the fused judgment remains uncertain, a diffusion policy drives the agent toward the candidate using only the tracked mask and depth until the decision becomes decisive. CLOSER runs locally and plugs into existing ObjectNav systems without retraining them. On Gibson and Habitat-Matterport 3D, it raises average success rate by 3.6 points and success weighted by path length by 2.0 points, showing that verifying evidence before committing improves embodied navigation.



## I Introduction

Object-goal navigation (ObjectNav) is a fundamental task in embodied AI [ 1 ] in which an agent searches an unseen environment for an object of a specified target category and stops near it [ 2 , 3 ] . To find the target, the agent must explore the environment efficiently and infer where the target is likely to be located. When an object resembling the target comes into view, the agent must further judge whether it is the target. ObjectNav therefore demands both efficient exploration and reliable perception from its online egocentric video stream, and remains a challenging problem despite extensive study [ 4 ] . To meet these demands, prevailing modular systems adopt an exploration strategy to search promising regions and an off-the-shelf object detector to recognize the observed objects [ 2 , 5 , 6 ] . Whenever a candidate for the target category is detected, the agent must decide from limited local observations whether the candidate is the target and hence whether to stop; in the worst case, only a single partial view is available. Since the episode ends once the agent stops, a wrong stop cannot be corrected, and this stopping decision has been identified as a major bottleneck of modular systems [ 7 , 8 ] . Although modern open-vocabulary detectors such as YOLO-World and Grounded-SAM [ 9 , 10 ] achieve high recall on common indoor categories, they frequently mistake semantically similar objects for the target. For example, a sofa observed from a distance may be detected as a bed (Fig. 1 ), and a stool partially occluded by a table may be detected as a chair. Such confusers are often detected with tight bounding boxes and high confidence, so raising the confidence threshold removes true targets before it removes these errors. Therefore, each detected candidate needs to be further verified before the agent commits to a stop. To this end, recent systems introduce a closed-source vision–language model (VLM) such as GPT-4o-mini as a verifier that confirms or rejects each detected candidate [ 11 ] . Such a model provides open-world visual reasoning that the detector lacks, and can reject confusers even when the detection confidence is high. However, the verifier is accessible only through a remote API, which incurs per-query cost and latency and leaves its weights unavailable for local adaptation. A smaller open-source VLM avoids these constraints, yet its verification accuracy drops markedly on hard cases such as distant or partially occluded confusers. Moreover, existing verifiers are binary and must decide even when the current view supports neither confirmation nor rejection. Such a forced decision may stop the agent at a non-target or reject a candidate that a closer view could confirm. Recent work has proposed ternary uncertainty scores and explicit not-found outcomes [ 12 , 13 ] , but neither mechanism moves the agent to acquire more views of the candidate for verification. In this paper, we propose CLOSER (Closed-Loop Observation Sensing for Embodied veRification), an evidence-aware active verification framework in which the agent decides whether to stop by reasoning over the visual evidence on a candidate and acting to improve it. Our key insight is that a reliable stopping decision depends not only on what the candidate appears to be but also on whether the available views suffice to identify it, and this sufficiency depends on the spatial relationship between the agent and the candidate, including its distance, viewing angle, and degree of occlusion. The agent therefore commits to a decision only when the evidence suffices and otherwise approaches the candidate to acquire more informative views (Fig. 1 ). Realizing this closed loop poses two challenges. First, the agent must learn when its evidence is insufficient, which confidence cannot reveal since distant or occluded views often yield confident predictions. Second, it must learn how to reach a more informative view without a complete map of the unseen environment. To learn when the evidence is insufficient, CLOSER fine-tunes an open-source VLM with LoRA [ 14 ] to assess both the identity of the candidate and the sufficiency of each view. During navigation, CLOSER tracks the candidate across egocentric frames with ByteTrack [ 15 ] and caches complementary views of it, and a lightweight MLP fuses the per-view assessments of the VLM to decide whether the evidence suffices. To learn how to reach a more informative view, CLOSER imitates an expert that plans maximum-clearance paths along the Voronoi medial axis of the complete map and smooths them into Bézier trajectories, so that walls are less likely to occlude the candidate along the way. To eliminate this dependence on the map, we distill the expert trajectories into a Diffusion Transformer policy [ 16 , 17 ] that conditions only on the tracked candidate mask and depth, which encode the distance and visibility of the candidate relative to the agent. As the agent follows this policy, verification and active perception form a closed loop, in which every view cached along the trajectory refines the assessment and the approach phase ends as soon as the evidence supports a decision. CLOSER is invoked only when a candidate for the target category is detected and leaves the exploration and mapping components of existing ObjectNav systems unchanged. It can therefore be inserted into these systems as a plug-in verification layer without retraining them. The main contributions of this paper are summarized as follows: • We propose CLOSER, a plug-in layer for existing ObjectNav systems that formulates the stopping decision as an active verification problem. Closer judges not only whether a candidate is the target but also whether its views suffice for that judgment, and it drives the agent to acquire more views by approaching the candidate when the current views are insufficient. • Since a confident prediction on a distant or occluded view can be wrong, we design the verifier in Closer to assess the sufficiency of each view in addition to the identity of the candidate. We fine-tune an open-source VLM for this assessment under a closed-source VLM teacher and train a lightweight MLP that fuses the assessments of complementary views into a single decision. • We train a map-free approach policy by distilling an expert into a Diffusion Transformer that generates a trajectory toward the candidate from its tracked mask and depth. To keep the candidate in view throughout the approach phase, the expert plans each path along the Voronoi medial axis of the free space for maximum clearance from walls and smooths the path into a Bézier trajectory. • Simulation experiments confirm that CLOSER improves ObjectNav performance. Across diverse ObjectNav systems on Gibson, HM3D v1, and HM3D v2, CLOSER improves success rate (SR), with an average gain of 3.6 points and a maximum gain of 4.9 points. CLOSER also improves success weighted by path length (SPL) by 2.0 points on average.
