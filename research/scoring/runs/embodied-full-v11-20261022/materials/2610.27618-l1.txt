# JARVIS: From Visual Manuals to Autonomous Robotic Assembly

## Abstract
Visual manuals communicate procedural knowledge through stepwise diagrams, but translating these instructions into reliable long-horizon robotic execution remains challenging. We introduce JARVIS, a method-agnostic benchmark for autonomous visual-manual-guided execution without additional language instructions. JARVIS comprises 236,679 LEGO assembly tasks across 8,987 scenes, with a carefully curated JARVIS-v1 suite of 50 tasks for interactive robotic evaluation. Tasks span varying initial structural complexities and assembly horizons, requiring agents to interpret manuals, ground actions in an evolving workspace, and execute the intended procedure. A structure-aware evaluation protocol captures geometric accuracy, structural relations, construction order, partial progress, and completion. We further introduce JARVIS-agentic, a tool-grounded embodied agent that composes robot primitives into executable programs and uses execution feedback for verification and recovery, without task-specific action trajectories or model parameter updates. Across nine multimodal and reasoning models, task-oriented primitives increase the average assembly score. Performance nevertheless declines sharply with assembly horizon, and execution errors remain difficult to correct. Prior-task experience improves initial performance, while repeated reflection provides inconsistent benefits, highlighting persistent limitations in connecting visual procedural reasoning with reliable physical execution.



## 1 Introduction
Across many everyday activities, people learn how to build, assemble, and operate things by following visual manuals. These manuals organize tasks into standardized step-by-step diagrams, providing a compact and transferable representation of procedural knowledge that conveys both the desired outcome and the process required to achieve it [ 31 ] . Following such manuals requires humans to interpret the visual instructions, align each illustrated step with the current physical state, and translate the inferred procedure into concrete actions. This ability goes beyond visual recognition alone, requiring visual reasoning to be tightly coupled with sequential decision-making and physical execution [ 32 ] . As multimodal models increasingly move beyond passive perception toward embodied interaction [ 16 , 12 , 17 ] , a fundamental question is whether machines can interpret procedural knowledge from visual manuals and autonomously translate it into action. Recent studies have taken initial steps toward this capability, particularly in manual-guided robotic assembly [ 31 , 39 , 9 , 15 ] . Existing approaches have primarily developed task-specific solutions for extracting assembly structures, predicting part poses, and generating assembly plans [ 31 , 28 , 18 , 32 , 36 , 22 ] . Many therefore stop before physical execution; when execution is included, it is often realised through predefined or manually engineered control pipelines. As a result, they do not directly evaluate whether a model can autonomously close the loop from visual manual understanding to physical construction. Benchmarking this capability further requires end-to-end execution tasks and metrics that capture partial progress and structural correctness beyond binary task success [ 11 , 33 ] . Consequently, it remains unclear how well current multimodal models can use a visual manual as the task specification and autonomously carry out a complete procedure over multiple steps. To systematically evaluate this capability, we introduce JARVIS, a method-agnostic benchmark for autonomous visual-manual-guided execution. We construct a large-scale task collection comprising 236{,}679 assembly tasks across 8{,}987 LEGO scenes, spanning diverse initial structures and assembly horizons. From this collection, we curate JARVIS-v1, a suite of 50 tasks across three difficulty levels for interactive robotic evaluation. As shown in Fig. 1 , an agent receives a visual assembly manual and workspace observations, infers the intended procedure, and executes it while adapting to the evolving physical state. Each manipulation affects subsequent steps, requiring coherent planning and execution throughout assembly. We further introduce a structure-aware evaluation protocol that measures relative geometry, structural relations, and completion, capturing partial progress and accumulated errors beyond binary task success. Learned control policies typically require task-specific robot action supervision, as low-level trajectories are tied to the robot’s kinematics and control interface. Collecting dedicated demonstrations or trajectories for each new construction task is therefore costly. We introduce JARVIS-agentic, a tool-grounded embodied agent that bridges manual-derived reasoning and physical execution without task-specific action trajectories. Equipped with a compact library of verified robot primitives, the agent grounds the manual, composes executable behaviours, and uses execution reflection for verification, recovery, and replanning. Using JARVIS, we evaluate 9 multimodal and reasoning models on manual-guided assembly tasks spanning different structural complexities and assembly horizons. Task-oriented robot skills raise the average assembly score from 7.88 to 14.71 without task-specific action trajectories, demonstrating the value of tool-grounded execution. Nevertheless, performance declines sharply with assembly horizon, and improvements in intermediate grounding do not reliably translate into successful assembly. Prior-task experience improves first-round performance, whereas repeated reflection and recovery from execution errors remain unreliable. These findings expose the difficulty of connecting procedural reasoning, physical execution, and reflection-based correction in long-horizon assembly. Our main contributions are summarized as follows: • We formulate autonomous visual-manual-guided execution for long-horizon robotic assembly and introduce JARVIS, comprising 236{,}679 tasks across 8{,}987 scenes, with a curated JARVIS-v1 suite for interactive evaluation. A structure-aware protocol measures geometric and relational correctness, partial progress, and completion. • We introduce JARVIS-agentic , a tool-grounded embodied agent that translates visual assembly instructions into executable programs through reusable robot primitives. The framework integrates planning, execution verification, and recovery without task-specific action trajectories or model parameter updates. • We evaluate nine multimodal and reasoning models , revealing persistent difficulties in translating manual understanding into reliable long-horizon execution. Our analysis shows that task-oriented tools and prior-task experience improve performance, while online reflection provides inconsistent benefits and error recovery remains limited.
