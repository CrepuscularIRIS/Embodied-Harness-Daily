# Replan for Error Recovery: Training Embodied VLMs through Verifiable Interaction with Action-Grouped Turn-GSPO

## Abstract
Embodied VLMs can execute increasingly complex manipulation tasks, yet execution errors can leave the environment in states that require replanning for recovery. Existing recovery methods either rely on inference-time mechanisms with fixed policies or learn from failure examples and reference corrections rather than verified action outcomes. To enable recovery learning through verifiable interaction, we introduce the Embodied Replanning for Recovery Environment (ERR-Env), which provides feedback on task progress and execution errors. We further construct ERR-Bench, a benchmark with held-out tasks spanning nominal, intermediate, and error states for evaluating recovery capability and execution reliability. We then propose Action-Grouped Turn-GSPO (AGT-GSPO), an online reinforcement learning method combining turn-level rewards for task progress and error states with action-grouped advantage estimation, and use it to train ERR-VLM, a compact 2B embodied VLM. Evaluation on ERR-Bench reveals a mismatch between task progress and execution reliability across diverse VLMs. AGT-GSPO improves ERR-VLM by 10.25 percentage points in Strict Success Rate for error-free completion and 22.95 percentage points in Recovery Rate, while ablations and zero-shot transfer further validate the effectiveness of our method.

## 1 Introduction
Vision-language models (VLMs) are increasingly used as high-level decision makers for embodied agents, enabling multi-step manipulation through visual observations, language goals, and semantic action interfaces ( Driess et al., 2023 ; Tan et al., 2026 ; Wang et al., 2026 ) . During execution, errors, limited visibility, or unexpected scene conditions can leave the task in a state for which the original plan is no longer appropriate. Reliable task completion therefore requires the model to recognize these deviations and replan from the resulting state to recover and continue execution. To support recovery from execution errors, inference-time approaches equip VLMs with prompting strategies, external monitors, or specialized mechanisms to detect errors and trigger replanning ( Chen et al., 2025b ; Zhou et al., 2025 ; Ahmad et al., 2025 ) . But with the policy fixed, recovery is limited to existing model capabilities. Recovery-oriented post-training instead uses error cases and reference corrections to improve error recognition and corrective action selection ( Ye et al., 2026 ; Pacaud et al., 2026 ; Yuan et al., 2026 ) . However, reference-based supervision does not optimize the policy against verified outcomes of recovery actions, leaving a gap between imitating corrections and resolving execution errors. This motivates training through environment interaction, where action outcomes are verified for error correction and task progress to improve recovery decisions. In this paper, we aim to train embodied VLMs to recover from execution errors through verifiable interaction and evaluate their recovery capability and execution reliability. Interaction-based recovery learning requires explicit feedback on errors and task progress, which existing embodied benchmarks typically lack ( Yang et al., 2025 ; Chen et al., 2026a ) . To address this gap, we develop the E mbodied R eplanning for R ecovery Environment (ERR-Env), an executable and verifiable interaction environment built on RoboTwin 2.0 ( Chen et al., 2025c ) . ERR-Env uses task-specific checkers to verify task progress, completion, execution errors, and visibility, supporting recovery-oriented data generation, online reinforcement learning, and evaluation. To assess recovery capability and execution reliability beyond task progress alone, we construct ERR-Bench based on ERR-Env, a controlled benchmark with held-out tasks spanning nominal, intermediate, and error states. To learn recovery from verifiable interaction in ERR-Env, verified action outcomes need to be translated into signals that reinforce effective corrections and discourage error-inducing actions. However, a trajectory-level signal cannot assign distinct feedback to error-inducing and corrective actions. Therefore, we propose Action-Grouped Turn-GSPO (AGT-GSPO), an online reinforcement learning method that uses turn-level rewards capturing task progress and error states after each executed action. AGT-GSPO further estimates advantages within action groups, avoiding comparisons across action types. Using AGT-GSPO, we train ERR-VLM, a compact 2B embodied VLM that learns to recognize execution errors and select corrective actions through replanning. Evaluation of diverse VLMs on ERR-Bench shows that high task progress does not guarantee reliable execution. Against this backdrop, training ERR-VLM with AGT-GSPO improves both task completion and recovery, raising Strict Success Rate for error-free task completion by 10.25 percentage points and Recovery Rate by 22.95 percentage points over its supervised initialization. Ablations and zero-shot transfer to external simulators and a physical robot further validate our approach. In summary, our principal contributions comprise: • We introduce ERR-Env, an executable and verifiable interaction environment with feedback on task progress and execution errors, supporting recovery data generation, online RL, and evaluation. We further construct ERR-Bench to evaluate recovery capability and execution reliability. • We propose AGT-GSPO, an online RL method that learns from verifiable interaction by combining turn-level rewards for task progress and error states with action-grouped advantage estimation. Using AGT-GSPO, we train ERR-VLM, a compact 2B embodied VLM for error recovery. • Evaluation on ERR-Bench across diverse VLMs reveals a clear mismatch between task progress and reliable execution, while ERR-VLM, trained with AGT-GSPO, substantially improves both recovery capability and execution reliability.
