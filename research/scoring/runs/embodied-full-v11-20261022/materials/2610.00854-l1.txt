# Are Frontier VLM Agents Ready to Be Robot Generalists? An Empirical Study with the Embodied Agent Arena

## Abstract
Frontier vision-language models (VLMs) increasingly estimate scenes, ground interactions, and generate executable actions. How far these native capabilities support embodied generalism across diverse tasks remains unclear. We introduce Embodied Agent Arena to assess seven VLM agents across Geometry, Spatial Reasoning, Affordance, Task Planning, and Manipulation. The arena contains 1,000 cases drawn from 32 established sources and GeoProbe, our new benchmark for geometric estimation on Blender renders and real-scene images. A minimal harness preserves source observations and operations while leaving perception, reasoning, and action selection to the model. Separate measures of metric precision, functional grounding, and native goal completion connect local competence to complete task outcomes. Astra's strengths in precise estimation and usable-contact localization coexist with endpoint errors in tracing and low household-task completion. Supplementary comparisons of richer observations and multi-round review show model- and task-dependent effects. Current VLM agents thus fall short of embodied generalism: they often make partial progress without satisfying all task goals within allotted time and interaction budgets.

## 1 Introduction
A robot generalist must turn scene understanding into actions that satisfy a complete goal. Locating an object, selecting a usable contact, and executing a grasp do not establish that the object reaches its destination. Vision-language models (VLMs) increasingly address spatial relations ( Ma et al., 2024 , Yang et al., 2024 ) and action-relevant grounding ( Wang et al., 2025a , Song et al., 2024 ) . Learned visuomotor policies connect observations to robot actions ( Kim et al., 2024 , Black et al., 2024 ) , while language-model controllers select skills or generate executable programs ( Ahn et al., 2022 , Liang et al., 2022 ) . Frontier agents increasingly combine these mechanisms with perception support and visual action rehearsal ( Fu et al., 2026 , Guo et al., 2026 , Li et al., 2026b , Zhang et al., 2026a ) . This progress raises a central issue: which robotic requirements can the VLM’s own perception, reasoning, and action selection satisfy? Assessing generalism requires connecting individual capabilities to complete task performance. Aggregate scores can hide errors in metric scale, reference frames, or placement conditions; terminal failure can conceal useful progress. This distinction motivates our central question: Are frontier VLM agents ready to be robot generalists? We introduce Embodied Agent Arena to evaluate seven VLM agents across Geometry, Spatial Reasoning, Affordance, Task Planning, and Manipulation (Figure 1 ). Its 1,000 cases combine 32 established sources with GeoProbe, which varies camera motion, object motion, and scale references to diagnose geometric estimation. A minimal harness retains each source’s observations and operations while leaving task decisions to the model. Separate measures of geometric precision, functional grounding, and native task completion reveal both cross-capability strengths and the requirements left unsatisfied within a task. The results expose a gap between local precision and complete execution: Astra produces the closest paths yet often misses their endpoints, and its local control strengths coexist with low household-goal completion. Supplementary experiments examine richer observations and multi-round review, showing that additional evidence has model- and task-dependent effects. Our contributions are: • An evaluation perspective centered on task requirements. We distinguish local estimates and interaction evidence from complete goal satisfaction across five domains. • A unified arena with a new geometric benchmark, GeoProbe. We combine 32 established sources with 168 GeoProbe cases, using controlled Blender scenes and real images to diagnose camera, motion, depth, and scale estimation. • A diagnosis of the gap from local competence to complete tasks. We compare seven agents and identify the task requirements that remain unsatisfied despite Astra’s leading precision and contact localization.
