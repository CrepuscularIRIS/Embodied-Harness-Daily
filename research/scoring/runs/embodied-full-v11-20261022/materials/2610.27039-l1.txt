# Embodied Playground: A Long-Horizon Agentic Robotics Benchmark for Physical, Strategic, and Spatial Reasoning

## Abstract
Foundation models offer a promising basis for robotic agents, but long-horizon tasks require them to sustain coherent reasoning and reliable physical execution across interdependent stages. We introduce Embodied Playground, a benchmark for evaluating how foundation-model agents coordinate physical, strategic, and spatial reasoning throughout such tasks. Agents must track task progress, satisfy intermediate constraints, and revise their plans using execution feedback as earlier actions shape subsequent decisions. The playground comprises 410 task environments spanning basic manipulation, sequential puzzles, and multi-stage household activities, with variations in planning depth, geometric constraints, and physical execution demands. We curate EP-100, a representative selection of 100 tasks across five suites and 16 task types, together with the EP-Easy and EP-Hard difficulty splits and the compact EP-20 selection. A shared protocol specifies observation and tool access, resource budgets, repeated trials, and independent checks of goal completion, task-rule compliance, and physical validity. Embodied Playground provides a unified framework for studying whether foundation-model agents can sustain effective reasoning and action over extended task horizons. https://winniechen2002.github.io/Agent-bench-website/



## 1 Introduction
Foundation models have shown promising capabilities in robotic tasks, supported by their ability to interpret visual observations, reason about spatial relationships and physical interactions, and formulate strategies. [ 1 , 2 , 3 , 4 ] Existing robotics benchmarks largely emphasize the performance and generalization of control policies. [ 5 , 6 , 7 , 8 ] This leaves a complementary question: can a foundation-model agent sustain these capabilities over long-horizon robotic tasks, where actions must establish the conditions for later stages and execution errors can affect subsequent decisions? Addressing this question requires evaluating how an agent tracks task progress, satisfies intermediate constraints, and adapts its plan throughout physical execution. We introduce Embodied Playground , a benchmark for studying long-horizon robotic problem solving by foundation-model agents. Tasks couple physical, strategic, and spatial reasoning across dependent stages: an agent must determine what to do next, execute it through the robot interface, and use the resulting observations to update its understanding of the remaining task. Earlier actions can change object configurations, consume resources, or constrain later choices, making sustained coordination between planning and execution central to successful completion. Given a task description, permitted observations, and a specified set of tools, the agent must ground its decisions in the observed scene and revise its plan using execution feedback. Success requires achieving the final goal while respecting task rules and physical constraints throughout the episode. Embodied Playground comprises 410 robotic task environments, ranging from basic object manipulation to long-horizon planning and dexterous coordination. These tasks vary in planning depth, geometric constraints, and physical execution demands. Tower of Hanoi requires executing legal move sequences while preserving valid intermediate configurations; cube puzzles require keeping track of puzzle states and object orientations through successive physical turns; and Kongming locks couple assembly or disassembly order with geometric feasibility and contact-rich manipulation. Multi-stage household tasks combine ordered interactions with object-placement and final-scene requirements. Launcher tasks connect trajectory prediction and causal effects with finite ammunition and, in selected scenes, target-order constraints. Shorter manipulation, precision, and dynamics tasks complement these extended sequences by probing constituent capabilities across multiple robot embodiments. To facilitate evaluation, we curate EP-100 , a representative selection of 100 tasks organized into five subsets: Launch , Tower , Lock , Cube , and Home . Together, these subsets cover 16 task types and provide focused evaluations alongside the full playground. EP-100 is divided into EP-Easy and EP-Hard by ranking the tasks of each type according to their workload, and EP-20 offers a compact selection that spans both splits. All tasks follow a common interaction interface and evaluation protocol, with specified observation and tool access, execution budgets, repeated trials, and task-specific physical success criteria. Our contributions are threefold: • An agent-centered perspective on long-horizon robotic problem solving. We evaluate a foundation-model agent’s ability to sustain coordinated perception, reasoning, tool use, and action across interdependent task stages, using execution feedback to track progress, revise plans, and satisfy task constraints throughout an episode. • A diverse playground spanning task horizons and execution demands. We introduce 410 robotic task environments spanning basic manipulation and multi-stage problem solving, with variations in planning depth, geometry, and physical execution requirements. EP-100 provides a representative selection across five suites and 16 task types, complemented by two difficulty splits and the compact EP-20 selection. • An episode-level protocol for evaluating completion and validity. We specify observation and tool-access levels, resource budgets, and repeated-trial conditions, with independent checks of final goal completion, task-rule compliance, and physical validity over the executed trajectory.
