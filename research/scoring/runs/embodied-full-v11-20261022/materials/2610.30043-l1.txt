# TARSphere: A Robot-Oriented Full-Stack Framework for Long-Horizon Embodied Agents in Evolving Physical and Social Environments

## Abstract
Robots must act under physical and social constraints while adapting to changes in their environment and human needs. Existing embodied-agent systems often focus on specified goals or abstract execution into symbolic actions, limiting evaluation in an evolving world. We introduce TARSphere, a robot-oriented full-stack framework for physically and socially grounded long-horizon embodied agents. TARSphere-Infra runs robot policies in a physics simulator with embodied NPCs and constrained dialogue. An event-driven finite-state machine (FSM) uses completed actions and delivered information to update task states and evaluate outcomes. TARSphere-Harness, its embodied brain harness, lets a frozen multimodal large language model (MLLM) act through multi-level executable skills, using observations, hierarchical memory, and execution feedback to revise its decisions. On this foundation, agent-in-the-loop curation aligns task semantics, causal dependencies, and scene geometry to construct TARSphere-Bench: 360 episodes across 30 indoor scenes. Five paired conditions assess perceptual robustness, disruption adaptation, task update, human coordination, and intent understanding for navigate-and-report and fetch-and-deliver tasks. Our evaluation of 8 MLLMs shows that general-purpose models can outperform embodied models, while task updates and human interaction remain difficult. Frequent collisions and falls further motivate evaluating execution safety alongside task success, even with a shared action interface and decision budget.



## 1 Introduction
Robots operating in everyday environments must account for physical constraints [ 11 ] , human safety, appropriate interaction, and social norms [ 19 , 23 ] . As the world evolves, sensory conditions, physical access, and people’s actions and needs can change during execution [ 9 , 23 , 8 ] . Long-horizon tasks therefore require tracking world states, interpreting human intentions, and adjusting plans using execution feedback [ 34 , 4 , 9 ] ( Figure 1 ). Vision-language-action (VLA) models [ 3 , 10 ] and world-action models (WAMs) [ 38 ] have advanced robot control, yet they often lack the ability to adapt to dynamic and uncertain environments. Large language models (LLMs) provide broad knowledge for reasoning yet lack the ability to perform complex, real-world tasks [ 1 , 9 , 8 ] . This complementarity motivates embodied agents that connect reasoning to action [ 1 , 9 , 37 , 14 ] . However, Harness VLA [ 39 ] , RoboClaw [ 13 ] , and HumanCLAW [ 14 ] focus on tasks with specified goals, leaving an evolving world with human involvement largely unexamined. Other settings, including EmbodiedBench’s high-level tasks [ 37 ] , LoTa-Bench [ 5 ] , ALFWorld’s text environments [ 28 ] , and Embodied Agent Interface [ 12 ] , abstract physical execution into discrete symbolic actions. GRBench [ 35 ] combines robot execution with virtual NPC dialogue, while PARTNR [ 4 ] evaluates embodied human-robot collaboration with temporal task constraints. These gaps motivate a physically and socially grounded foundation for benchmarking robotic agents in an evolving world, with task outcomes tied to execution and human interaction. Insight. Studying embodied intelligence requires physically and socially grounded execution and tasks revealing capabilities across varied situations in a changing world ( Sections 4.2 , 4.3.2 and 4.3.4 ). Challenge 1: Embodied execution is not symbolic [ Sections 4.2 and 4.3.4 ]. A valid action call does not guarantee successful execution: the robot must contend with contact, friction, balance, and human responses. The agent must therefore revise decisions using physical execution feedback. Challenge 2: The world changes during execution [ Sections 4.3.2 and 4.3.3 ]. Physical conditions, human activity, and task requirements can change while a plan unfolds. The agent must interpret the current situation and reconsider what to do next. Challenge 3: Evaluation must assess diverse capabilities [ Section 4.2 ]. Within this setting, evaluation must reveal how agents handle varied situations across multiple capability dimensions. Aggregate success alone can mask different capability gaps across task families ( Section 4.2 ). We introduce TARSphere, a robot-oriented full-stack framework for embodied agents under physical and social constraints. TARSphere-Infra couples robot controllers with embodied NPCs in a physics simulator ( Challenge 1 ). An event-driven FSM maintains world and task states, while TARSphere-Harness combines observations, memory, skills, and execution feedback for replanning ( Challenge 2 ). We build TARSphere-Bench through agent-in-the-loop curation, pairing navigate-and-report and fetch-and-deliver tasks with five capability conditions: perceptual robustness, disruption adaptation, task update, human coordination, and intent understanding ( Challenge 3 ). Paired variants retain their Base scene and task family. Across 8 MLLMs, general-purpose models can outperform embodied models, with capability gaps varying by task family ( Section 4.2 ). Planning credit does not ensure successful execution, and collisions and falls remain common ( Sections 4.2 and 4.3.4 ). These findings call for agents that combine general reasoning, adaptation to world changes, and safe execution ( Sections 4.2 and 4.3.2 ). Our contributions are three-fold. • We formulate long-horizon embodied tasks under physical and social constraints, where execution outcomes and world changes continually shape the agent’s decisions. • We develop TARSphere Framework, where physical execution and interaction with embodied humans update task states and supply evaluation evidence; an embodied brain harness uses observations, memory, executable skills, and world feedback to guide model decisions. • We introduce agent-in-the-loop curation for 360 TARSphere-Bench episodes in 30 scenes and five paired capability conditions, aligning task semantics, geometry, and execution.
