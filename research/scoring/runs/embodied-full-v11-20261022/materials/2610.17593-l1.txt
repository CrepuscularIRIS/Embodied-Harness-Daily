# Training Generalist Navigation Policies via Action Self-Distillation

## Abstract
Language provides a natural interface for humans to specify and reason about goals, but training language-conditioned robotic policies and reasoning typically requires expensive manual annotations. We propose Action Self-Distillation (ASD), a method for learning language-conditioned reasoning for robotic navigation without human-annotated language or reasoning labels. ASD uses an open-source VLM, which contains broad world knowledge and reasoning capabilities, as both a teacher network and a student policy. During training, the self-teacher receives privileged trajectory feedback and infers the underlying navigation goal together with goal-oriented reasoning. The student is then trained to condition on the inferred navigation goal, predict future ego-frame trajectory, and provide a description of the scene semantics, plan, and reasoning, from onboard observations alone. Across 13 challenging indoor and outdoor real-world navigation tasks, our method improves success rate over the strongest baseline by $30\%$ in indoor settings, $30\%$ on multi-stage tasks, and achieves $95\%$ success rate on outdoor tasks, validating that privileged trajectory feedback can enable self-distillation for scalable generalist navigation policies without human annotations.

## 1 Introduction
Vision-language-action (VLA) models have emerged as a successful approach to robot learning, enabling policies to condition on natural-language commands while grounding them in visual observations [ 1 , 2 ] . For navigation, this paradigm is especially appealing because language provides a deployable interface for specifying goals in human-centered environments, without requiring privileged map coordinates or goal images [ 3 , 4 , 5 , 6 ] . However, training VLMs to end-to-end navigation typically requires datasets with goal descriptions, scene semantics or reasoning traces, followed by supervised fine-tuning [ 7 , 4 ] . These annotations are expensive to obtain at scale, and many widely used robot navigation datasets contain only sensor observations and trajectories, with no language supervision [ 8 , 9 , 4 , 10 , 11 , 12 ] . The student observes only deployable inputs and learns to align its reasoning and trajectory tokens with the self-teacher, removing the need for external goal or reasoning annotations. Prior approaches address this gap either by relying on human annotators for high-quality language labels [ 7 , 4 , 13 , 14 ] , or by orchestrating multiple external models to generate goals, semantic descriptions, or reasoning supervision. Departing from these approaches, we investigate whether a single pretrained VLM can supervise itself from trajectory-only navigation data. This motivates our research question: How can we learn language goal-conditioned end-to-end navigation policies from trajectory-only robot datasets, without relying on dataset language annotations or multi-model annotation pipelines? In human interaction with frontier VLMs, feedback plays a central role in refining model behavior when initial responses do not match the user’s intent. A key observation is that when a model is conditioned on corrective or informative feedback about its own outputs, it can recover coherent goal-aligned predictions even when it could not do so from the input alone [ 15 , 16 , 17 ] . Inspired by this feedback-driven reasoning capability, we treat trajectory feedback as privileged information during training. Given such feedback, a VLM can infer the underlying goal, extract relevant scene semantics, and produce planning-oriented reasoning during training. To this end, we propose Training Generalist Navigation Policies via Action Self-Distillation ( ASD ), a framework in which a single model extracts language goals from trajectory-conditioned scene reasoning as self-teacher, and then learns to generate goal-conditioned trajectories together with corresponding reasoning traces. ASD instantiates this idea through a self-distillation procedure with two passes. In the teacher pass, the VLM receives the current observation together with ground-truth future trajectory, projected onto an egocentric observation image. This ground truth trajectory as privileged feedback information guides the model to produce structured perception-prediction-planning reasoning and to extract a natural language goal. In the student pass, the model is trained to condition on the extracted goal and predict both future actions and structured reasoning from onboard observations alone. The self-teacher is implemented as an EMA-updated copy of the student [ 18 ] , allowing language-goal and reasoning supervision to be generated within the training loop. Critically, ASD operates directly on raw image-trajectory pairs, requiring no human-provided language goals or reasoning annotations. To our knowledge, this is the first application of feedback-conditioned self-distillation on robot control, converting existing unannotated navigation datasets into goal-conditioned reasoning supervision without external labeling. We deploy ASD on a FrodoBot rover in zero-shot manner across a range of out-of-distribution natural language goals in both indoor and outdoor environments. The policy is trained on a mixture of RECON [ 8 ] and FrodoBot-2K [ 9 ] , without any language goal or reasoning annotations. Across 9 indoor and 4 outdoor language-conditioned real-world experiments, we compare ASD against competitive VLA navigation baselines, including OmniVLA [ 7 ] and NaVILA [ 19 ] . ASD demonstrates language goal steerability across directional, color-based, and object-centric commands, and further generalizes to both multi-stage and indoor navigation tasks that are unseen during training. Across all 13 real-world tasks, ASD achieves the highest overall success rate, outperforming the strongest baseline, OmniVLA, by 30\% in the indoor setting and reaching a 95\% success rate in the outdoor setting. On multi-stage tasks, ASD further improves over the leading baseline, NaVILA, by 30\% .
