# Frontier LLMs Enter the World of Robotics: A Cross-Model Evaluation on RoboColiseum

## Abstract
Recent studies have explored the potential of multimodal large language models for robot control, yet the models' embodied capabilities remain insufficiently characterized across models, tasks, and execution settings. We conduct a multidimensional comparative evaluation on RoboColiseum across four tracks: Instruction Following, Spatial Reasoning, General Manipulation, and Robustness. We implement Agent2Act, a control harness that integrates GPT-6 Astra, GPT-6.1 Sol, Claude Opus 5.5, and Gemini 4 Argon into the evaluation environment through shared observation, action execution, and feedback interfaces. Under common task settings and scoring criteria, all four models exhibit a consistent capability profile: high instruction-following scores, with three approaching perfect performance; strong spatial reasoning; and general manipulation scores below those of the other three tracks. Within the evaluated configurations, Astra leads instruction following and robustness, while Opus leads spatial reasoning, general manipulation, and overall performance. These results reveal complementary strengths across models and a persistent gap between task understanding and reliable physical execution. Component comparisons on selected tasks further show that harness design affects task success, planning cost, and execution time. Additionally, case studies with Astra examine whole-body coordination, manipulation across robot embodiments, precision manipulation, dexterous-hand manipulation, and real-robot tasks, providing further insight into the model's capabilities and limitations across embodied tasks.

## I Introduction
I-A Motivation The release of GPT-6 Astra [ 1 ] raises a practical question: what can a multimodal large language model achieve when it directly controls a robot? A growing body of work explores how large language models (LLMs) can use observations to control robots. In simulation, W. Zhang et al. evaluate LLMs as manipulation policies and identify a gap between semantic task understanding and precise physical control [ 2 ] . GPT-as-Policy compares Astra-generated end-effector actions with a hybrid controller in which Astra reviews and corrects \pi_{0.5} action proposals [ 3 ] . Galbot’s study examines direct and hybrid control across simulated manipulation and other embodied domains, distinguishing model-generated commands from actions provided by learned policies or whole-body controllers [ 4 ] . Y. Yao et al. evaluate tabletop and mobile manipulation in Ebench, where Astra generates end-effector waypoints, gripper targets, and base targets through a fixed execution interface [ 5 ] . On real hardware, RoboCurve compares Astra with a VLA policy on bimanual tabletop tasks using YAM robot arms. Astra issues absolute end-effector poses through action tools [ 6 ] . RoboICL evaluates both simulated tasks and three real-robot tasks on a Franka Research 3 arm. Its frozen Astra model uses demonstrations and interaction memory to generate numerical action chunks through a fixed tool schema [ 7 ] . These studies provide initial evidence of LLM-driven robot control across different tasks and control configurations. Building on this work, we compare four frontier multimodal large language models across RoboColiseum’s four tracks, using shared sensing and execution interfaces to examine their strengths and limitations. Our evaluation covers GPT-6 Astra [ 1 ] , GPT-6.1 Sol [ 8 ] , Claude Opus 5.5 [ 9 ] , and Gemini 4 Argon [ 10 ] . To integrate these models into RoboColiseum, we implement Agent2Act, a harness that provides a unified interface for interaction with robotic environments and action execution. RoboColiseum’s consistent task settings and scoring criteria allow us to systematically examine the models’ relative strengths, weaknesses, and capability boundaries in embodied tasks. I-B Benchmark Overview and Key Findings RoboColiseum is a multidimensional benchmark for embodied model capabilities. It supports vision-language-action (VLA) models, world/action models (WAM), and agent-based systems. Its four tracks assess complementary dimensions of embodied performance: • Instruction Following: evaluates instruction understanding across ten dimensions, including color, size, quantity, semantics, and logical conditions. • Spatial Reasoning: evaluates understanding of spatial relations, including relative and absolute positions, sorting, and stacking. • General Manipulation: evaluates a range of manipulation skills, including grasping, placing, insertion, twisting, pouring, scooping, and bimanual coordination. • Robustness: evaluates performance under changes in robot state, camera mounting position, image quality, instruction wording, environmental conditions, and lighting. Together, these tracks reveal model strengths, limitations, and challenges in physical task execution. Our main findings are as follows: • Strong instruction following, spatial reasoning, and robustness, but weaker manipulation. All four models exhibit a common capability profile: their instruction following, spatial reasoning, and robustness scores exceed their general manipulation scores. Strong zero-shot instruction following has not yet translated into reliable complex physical manipulation. • Different models have different strengths. Among the four multimodal models, Astra leads instruction following and robustness, while Opus leads spatial reasoning, general manipulation, and Overall. No single model leads every dimension. These complementary strengths show why Overall scores alone cannot fully characterize model capabilities. • Cost and latency remain bottlenecks. Despite strong benchmark performance, high inference costs and long waits for model responses continue to limit the practical use of multimodal models for embodied robot control. • Harness design matters. A well-designed harness helps models make full use of their capabilities in robot control. Agent2Act provides a shared control harness for evaluation across four capability dimensions. Comparisons on a limited test set illustrate how observation inputs, feedback, history, and control interfaces affect task completion and execution efficiency. • Case studies reveal the scope and limits of model capabilities. Case studies with GPT-6 Astra demonstrate that multimodal large language models can control whole-body motion, manipulate objects across robot embodiments, combine mobile navigation with interaction, and perform selected tasks on real robots.
