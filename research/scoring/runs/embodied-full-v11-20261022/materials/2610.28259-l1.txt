# Large Multimodal Models for Direct Robot Control: GPT-6 as a Beginning

## Abstract
Recent advances in visuospatial understanding and agentic reasoning enable direct robotic manipulation with general-purpose multimodal models, but their embodied capabilities remain poorly understood. This paper systematically evaluate GPT-6 Astra for closed-loop robotic manipulation using a lightweight agent execution framework. We first probe three fundamental embodied capabilities-depth perception, embodied in-context learning, and tactile understanding-and demonstrate that the model can exploit depth information, acquire simple manipulation strategies from contextual demonstrations, and use tactile feedback to correct its actions. We then evaluate GPT-6 Astra across five established manipulation benchmarks along six capability dimensions. Despite competitive performance on several benchmarks-26.92\% success on RoboDojo and 76.0\% on RoboTwin 2.0-our analysis reveals a pronounced gap between semantic reasoning and physical execution, particularly in consequence-aware action planning, precision, and complex motion coordination. To further examine these limitations, we introduce LIBERO-GeoMotion, a new benchmark of geometry-constrained tasks that require precise pose alignment, coordinated translation and rotation, and anticipation of physical action consequences. GPT-6 Astra achieves only 11.1\% zero-shot success, exposing the difficulty of translating spatial understanding into physically feasible motion. Real-world experiments corroborate these findings: zero-shot execution is feasible for simple tasks, yet persistent deficits remain in motion feasibility, precise control, and physical safety, while substantial inference latency further hinders practical deployment. Overall, our results characterize the emerging embodied capabilities of general-purpose multimodal models and delineate the key challenges toward safe, efficient, and reliable robotic manipulation.



## 1 Introduction
Robot manipulation requires a tightly coupled set of capabilities: understanding language and visual scenes, reasoning about spatial relationships, generating physically grounded actions, and adapting to environmental feedback [ 2 , 52 ] . Although general-purpose LLMs and VLMs excel at semantic understanding and high-level planning [ 1 , 16 ] , their limited spatial grounding [ 9 ] and closed-loop control preclude reliable action generation [ 26 , 35 , 32 , 14 ] . LLM/VLM-based coding agents have likewise been explored for robotic manipulation [ 35 ] ; however, they specify only high-level action logic, whereas precise manipulation points and target poses are still delegated to hand-engineered geometric computations and control primitives, which limits their flexibility. In contrast, specialized action models, such as Vision-Language-Action (VLA) [ 6 , 5 , 30 , 4 , 20 , 3 , 55 , 57 ] models and World Action Models (WAMs) [ 7 , 51 , 23 ] , directly generate actions, but remain constrained by their embodied training distributions [ 56 ] , which limits their generalization to unseen tasks and environments [ 17 ] . Agentic hybrid architectures [ 48 , 63 , 46 ] aim to combine the strengths of both families, using general models for planning and specialized action models for execution. However, these systems still rely on post-trained action policies, even for relatively simple manipulation tasks, and therefore inherit the same generalization and flexibility bottlenecks. Recently, GPT-6 Astra [ 45 ] has emerged as a promising candidate, with strong visuospatial understanding, 3D reasoning, and agentic reasoning. These capabilities suggest that a general-purpose model could directly generate executable actions through spatial reasoning and sustain closed-loop control through agentic reasoning—without the hand-engineered primitives or post-trained action policies required by existing hierarchical architectures. Preliminary robotics studies [ 50 , 62 , 13 , 37 ] offer initial evidence for this potential, reporting promising manipulation performance without specialized VLA or WAM policies. Yet the boundaries of GPT-6 Astra’s embodied capabilities remain insufficiently characterized. Existing studies focus on benchmark performance and selected failure cases, without establishing which capabilities the model executes reliably and where its limitations emerge across diverse physical demands; in particular, real-world evaluations have not systematically probed precise geometric constraints, complex motion coordination, or safety-critical interactions. These gaps raise a fundamental question: What can GPT-6 Astra reliably achieve in manipulation, and where do its capabilities break down, particularly in the real world? To answer this question, we conduct a comprehensive, capability-driven investigation of GPT-6 Astra spanning more than 2,000 trials across simulation and real-world environments. We first introduce GPT-Generalist End-effector Action Reasoning ( GPT-GEAR ), an agent execution framework that equips GPT-6 Astra with minimally engineered general-purpose tools for direct closed-loop robotic manipulation (Figure 1 (a)). Using GPT-GEAR, we isolate three capabilities that underlie successful manipulation—depth perception [ 25 , 24 ] , embodied in-context learning [ 13 , 37 ] , and tactile understanding [ 33 , 8 , 39 , 18 ] (Figure 1 (b))—and show that the model can estimate and exploit depth, acquire simple motor skills from in-context demonstrations, and use tactile feedback to correct its actions during execution. We then evaluate GPT-6 Astra on five established benchmarks—LIBERO-Plus [ 17 ] , RoboTwin 2.0 [ 11 ] , RoboDojo [ 10 ] , RoboCasa GR1 [ 43 , 3 ] , and UniVTAC [ 8 ] (Figure 1 (c))—providing a broad empirical baseline for general-purpose models in robotic manipulation. Characterizing performance along six capability dimensions reveals an uneven profile: without task-specific policy training, GPT-6 Astra exhibits strong task understanding, promising generalization, and superior memory and attains competitive results on several benchmarks, yet these strengths do not consistently translate into reliable physical execution—particularly in long-horizon manipulation, precision-sensitive tasks, and complex motion coordination—while substantial inference overhead further underscores the gap between general-purpose reasoning and efficient robotic control [ 31 ] . Geometry-constrained manipulation exposes this gap most clearly, as it requires precise pose control, coordinated translation and rotation, and anticipation of physical interactions. To probe these demands, we introduce LIBERO-GeoMotion , a targeted benchmark built on LIBERO [ 36 ] assets that emphasizes complex motion execution while minimizing semantic complexity (Figure 1 (d)). On LIBERO-GeoMotion, GPT-6 Astra exhibits substantial difficulties in satisfying geometric and sequential constraints, exposing a critical disconnect between understanding desired spatial configurations and generating physically feasible action sequences. We finally extend the evaluation to real-world zero-shot deployment: GPT-6 Astra reliably completes basic pick-and-place tasks, but its overall zero-shot success rate reaches only 40%, with some constrained tasks failing entirely, and persistent failures involving unsafe grasp configurations, motion feasibility, and precise alignment reveal substantial challenges in safety-aware planning and reliable physical execution. General-purpose models thus already exhibit meaningful zero-shot manipulation capabilities, but remain far from delivering consistently safe and reliable robotic control. In summary, our contributions are as follows: • We introduce GPT-GEAR , a lightweight agent execution framework that equips GPT-6 Astra with minimally engineered general-purpose tools, enabling direct closed-loop robotic manipulation with no specialized action policies . • We systematically investigate three fundamental embodied capabilities—depth perception, embodied in-context learning, and tactile understanding—revealing how diverse forms of embodied information support action generation and execution. • We conduct comprehensive evaluations across five established manipulation benchmarks and characterize performance along six capability dimensions, establishing a broad empirical baseline and identifying the strengths and limitations of general-purpose models in robotic manipulation. • We introduce LIBERO-GeoMotion , a targeted benchmark for geometry-constrained manipulation that probes precise pose control, coordinated rotational and translational motion, and consequence-aware action planning, revealing critical limitations that conventional pick-and-place benchmarks cannot reveal. • We validate GPT-6 Astra on real robotic hardware, demonstrating its potential for zero-shot execution of simple manipulation tasks while identifying critical limitations in safety-aware planning, motion feasibility, and reliable physical control.
