# CoEvolve: Self-Evolving Code Policies from Skills to Strategies for Humanoid Robots

## Abstract
Recent advances in Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models and World Action Models (WAMs) have expanded the manipulation and whole body capabilities of individual robots. Yet a single robot remains limited by its embodiment, and many real world tasks require capabilities that can only emerge through cooperation. Existing multi robot frameworks largely emphasize task decomposition, allocation, and replanning, while providing limited support for adapting executable coordination when physical execution deviates from the intended behavior. We present CoEvolve, a framework that represents team coordination as executable code over fixed robot motor skills. CoEvolve introduces Verifier Guided Coordination Evolution, where a multimodal foundation model instantiates coordination strategies through motion primitives, semantic objectives, temporary robot roles, and bounded execution parameters. Physical verification evaluates each executed transition, rejected behaviors trigger structured coordination revision from an accepted checkpoint, and verified experience is retained to guide subsequent decisions. We evaluate CoEvolve with two simulated Unitree G1 humanoids transporting a freely supported rigid payload through constrained corridors. Across 15 base task runs with three multimodal backbone families, CoEvolve completes 8 tasks, with every backbone achieving end to end success. Additional evaluations yield verified placement on two additional corridor layouts and after both same-backbone and cross-backbone experience initialization, while exposing persistent limitations in motion precision and contact stability. These results support executable coordination as a practical interface for adapting long horizon multi robot collaboration under shared physical constraints.



## 1 Introduction
Recent advances in robot foundation models have equipped individual robots with increasingly general visuomotor capabilities, enabling them to follow language instructions and perform diverse manipulation tasks ( Brohan et al., 2022 ; Brohan et al., 2023 ; Kim et al., 2024 ; Black et al., 2024 ) . Yet some physical tasks require capabilities that cannot be provided by one robot alone. Transporting an extended object through a constrained environment, for example, requires coordinated support, relative motion, corner negotiation, and placement ( De Vincenti and Coros, 2023 ; Pandit et al., 2025 ) . Collaboration is therefore not simply the aggregation of individual skills. Robots that can independently walk, turn, and move laterally must still determine how to combine these capabilities while carrying a shared object. We view this formation of team behavior as a form of co-becoming : effective cooperation is realized through the changing relationship between individual capabilities and the shared physical state. CoEvolve combines executable team policies, physical verification, and reusable experience to revise coordination during execution. This motivates our central question: given fixed individual motor skills, how can an agent instantiate and revise executable team coordination as a shared physical task unfolds? Multi-robot planning methods address task decomposition, allocation, and collaboration through structured and language-model planning ( Cai et al., 2025 ; Mandi et al., 2024 ; Kannan et al., 2024 ; Liu et al., 2025a ; Yuan et al., 2026 ) , while learned coordination methods optimize joint or decentralized policies through training ( Zhang et al., 2026a ; Doshi et al., 2026 ) . In parallel, agents improve textual skills, memory systems, and executable implementations through feedback and experience sharing ( Yang et al., 2026 ; Zhang et al., 2025 ; Weng et al., 2026 ) . Code as Policies and subsequent robot programming systems also generate and revise executable behaviors through interaction ( Liang et al., 2023 ; Fu et al., 2026 ; Lu et al., 2026 ; Xiao et al., 2026 ) . We study a complementary challenge: revising coordination when robots are physically coupled through a shared payload. In this setting, an individual action can alter both a teammate’s support conditions and the feasibility of subsequent joint motion. As a result, a valid task plan does not guarantee that the current coordination pattern remains executable. The key challenge is therefore to instantiate joint behavior from fixed motor skills and revise the relevant coordination relation when physical execution deviates, for example when unequal displacement disrupts formation or a constrained turn requires temporary support and mover roles. We introduce CoEvolve , a framework for adapting executable team coordination during shared physical tasks. Figure 1 summarizes the motivation and overall interaction loop. CoEvolve separates fixed individual motor capabilities from the coordination strategy that composes them for the current task phase. Rather than selecting from a library of complete strategies, the agent instantiates coordination through motion primitives, semantic objectives, temporary roles, coordination modes, and bounded execution parameters. The evaluated setting provides simulator measurements and route references, while the model determines how these capabilities should be combined within the resulting physical constraints. Our Verifier Guided Coordination Evolution mechanism uses physical execution evidence to determine which aspects of coordination should change. A rejected transition triggers a revision to the maneuver, the relationship between robot roles, or the execution parameters. Revised policies are evaluated after restoration of the latest accepted simulator checkpoint. Verified outcomes also contribute complementary forms of experience: abstract coordination records guide subsequent decisions, while accepted executable examples inform code generation. Foundation model and motor policy weights remain fixed. The adaptation concerns the task plan, instantiated coordination strategies, and stored experience rather than the acquisition of new motor skills. We evaluate CoEvolve with two simulated Unitree G1 humanoids carrying a freely supported rigid payload through constrained corridors. The base evaluation contains eight complete tasks across fifteen runs and three foundation model families. We further compare against four adapted external baselines and two component variants, evaluate additional corridor layouts, and study reuse of verified cooperative experience across foundation model backbones. These experiments distinguish demonstrated execution capability from causal component effects: some comparison, scene, and transfer records differ in runtime configuration, interaction budget, or continuation conditions. Our contributions are: • We introduce an executable representation of team coordination that separates fixed individual motor capabilities from the cooperative strategy instantiated for the current physical situation. • We propose Verifier Guided Coordination Evolution, which combines physical verification, structured strategy revision, and reusable cooperative experience to adapt coordination during execution. • We provide an extensive evaluation across three foundation model families, adapted multi robot and code policy baselines, additional corridor layouts, and cross-model experience reuse, while analyzing the execution and contact limitations that remain.
