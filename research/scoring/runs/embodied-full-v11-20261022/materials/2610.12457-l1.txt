# SpatialHarness: Test-Time Spatial Scaffolding for Fine Robotic Manipulation

## Abstract
Frontier multimodal foundation models (e.g., GPT-6 Astra) have recently shown strong potential for direct robotic control, yet their performance on fine manipulation remains limited. We argue that an important source of failure is not necessarily insufficient policy capability, but insufficient spatial observability, where task-critical spatial relationships may be poorly revealed by the existing physical camera setup. We introduce SpatialHarness, a test-time embodied harness that provides test-time spatial scaffolding for fine robotic manipulation without policy fine-tuning or changes to the physical sensing setup. SpatialHarness maintains an online simulated scene synchronized with real-world execution, identifies task-critical spatial relationships, and renders complementary virtual views that expose them to a frozen multimodal policy. To keep the simulated scene aligned during interaction, we develop interaction-aware scene synchronization that distinguishes static, held, and transition modes. We evaluate SpatialHarness on four real-robot manipulation tasks spanning precise geometric alignment, object-relative placement, and articulated-object interaction. Using the same frozen GPT-6 Astra policy, SpatialHarness substantially improves task success, including from 26.7% to 66.7% on plug insertion and from 0% to 100% on Tower of Hanoi. These results indicate that improving spatial observability at test time can unlock fine-manipulation capabilities already present in strong multimodal foundation models. Project website: https://emilia113.github.io/SpatialHarness/.

## 1 Introduction

Recent frontier multimodal foundation models, such as GPT-6 Astra [ 27 ] , have begun to exhibit surprisingly strong capabilities for robotic manipulation. Given visual observations and language instructions, these models can directly generate robot actions and refine subsequent decisions through closed-loop interaction, without task-specific policy training. This emerging paradigm suggests a compelling alternative to robotic foundation models [ 32 , 19 , 4 , 9 , 15 ] . Instead of adapting the model to each robotic setting, an embodied harness can instead structure how the model observes, reasons about, and interacts with the physical world. Existing harnesses have explored action interfaces, demonstrations, interaction histories, and execution feedback to better expose the capabilities already present in general-purpose multimodal foundation models [ 7 , 12 , 23 ] . Nevertheless, strong high-level reasoning does not necessarily translate into reliable fine robotic manipulation [ 21 , 31 ] . Tasks such as insertion, stacking, or placement onto narrow structures depend on task- critical spatial relationships, whether a plug is aligned with its socket, whether a hole is centered over a peg, or whether a grasped object is correctly positioned relative to a supporting surface. These relationships can be difficult to infer from the fixed cameras available in a real deployment, particularly near contact, where the gripper and manipulated objects frequently occlude the most relevant geometry [ 25 ] . In such cases, failure may arise not because the policy lacks the necessary manipulation capability, but because the spatial evidence required to exercise that capability is poorly observable. This observation motivates us to ask a critical question: How can we improve the spatial observability of a robot’s environment at test time, without retraining the policy or modifying the physical sensing setup? To address this question, we introduce SpatialHarness , a test-time embodied harness that provides test-time spatial scaffolding for fine robotic manipulation. SpatialHarness maintains an online simulated scene synchronized with real-world execution and uses it to construct task-relevant spatial context, without the need to modify the policy or add extra physical cameras, as illustrated in Figure 1 . Before execution, the multimodal foundation model analyzes the task goal and identifies task-critical spatial relationships. SpatialHarness then configures virtual viewpoints that expose these relationships, complementing the observations from the robot’s physical cameras. During execution, the harness renders the complementary virtual views from the continually updated simulated scene and supplies them to the policy together with structured spatial information, including object 6-DoF poses and estimates of rendering reliability. A key challenge is maintaining sufficiently accurate spatial context as physical interaction changes the scene. Pure physics rollout is unreliable during manipulation because errors in physical parameters and contact modeling can cause simulated object states to diverge from real execution, particularly during grasping and release [ 5 , 20 ] . SpatialHarness therefore performs interaction-aware online scene synchronization, combining visual correction grounded in real observations with programmatic correction. We distinguish three object–gripper interaction modes, including static, held, and transition, and exploit different physical and observational priors in each case. Static objects preserve or visually update their aligned states, while objects under sustained held are propagated using robot action and programmatically validated against real observations and gripper geometry. In transition mode, small pre-contact errors can substantially alter simulated outcomes. SpatialHarness performs hypothesis replay from a pre-interaction checkpoint using candidate poses and measured robot motion, then selects a corrected state through interaction consistency filtering and contour matching. Consequently, SpatialHarness uses simulation as a runtime spatial interface: real observations ground the scene, while complementary virtual views expose task-critical spatial relationships that may be hidden, ambiguous, or poorly conditioned in the physical camera configuration. physical camera observations. We evaluate SpatialHarness on four real-robot manipulation tasks spanning local geometric alignment, object-relative placement, and articulated-object interaction. Using the same frozen GPT-6 Astra policy for both the baseline and SpatialHarness, we observe substantial improvements in task success, for instance, the success rate for plug insertion increases from 26.7% to 66.7% , and 0% to 100% on Tower of Hanoi, as shown in Fig. 1 . These improvements are achieved without policy fine-tuning or changes to the physical camera setup, highlighting the importance of spatial observability as a test-time factor in fine robotic manipulation. Our contributions are as follows: • We introduce SpatialHarness and formulate test-time spatial scaffolding as a mechanism for improving fine manipulation with frozen multimodal policies. SpatialHarness augments runtime observations with task-relevant virtual views and structured spatial context, without modifying the underlying policy or physical sensing setup. • We develop an interaction-aware online scene synchronization framework that combines visual and programmatic correction across static, held, and transition modes to maintain alignment between the simulated scene and the real environment during manipulation. • We demonstrate on real-robot fine-manipulation tasks that improving spatial observability can substantially increase the success rate for a strong multimodal foundation model used as a robot policy.
