# RoboHarness: Memory-Driven Orchestration of Heterogeneous Robot Policies for Long-Horizon Planning

## Abstract
Long-horizon robotic tasks require a breadth of capabilities beyond what any single existing robot control policy can reliably provide. Combining heterogeneous policies with complementary strengths offers a promising solution, but introduces two key challenges: uncertain capability boundaries and distribution mismatches during policy handoffs. These challenges remain largely unaddressed by existing planning methods, which typically assume homogeneous, predefined skills with fixed applicability. We propose RoboHarness, a unified framework that encapsulates independently developed heterogeneous policies, including vision-language-action models (VLAs), world-action models (WAMs), reinforcement learning (RL) policies, and task and motion planners (TAMP), as reusable agentic skills. RoboHarness integrates understanding, memory, and evolution skills to reason about policy capabilities and support capability-aware task decomposition and policy routing. To mitigate distribution mismatches during policy handoffs, we introduce Memory Bridge, a plug-in policy-chaining mechanism that enables reliable transitions between heterogeneous policies without joint retraining. Extensive experiments across five public benchmarks, 500 customized tasks across 10 classes, and 135 real-robot trials demonstrate substantial gains in long-horizon and memory-dependent tasks, as well as robustness to out-of-distribution conditions.

## 1 Introduction
Long-horizon robotic tasks require instruction alignment, generalization across environmental variations, logical consistency, and noise robustness ( Garrett et al., 2020 ; Yang et al., 2025 ) . Despite substantial progress ( Huang et al., 2026b ; Black et al., 2025b ; Silver et al., 2024 ; Ye et al., 2025 ) , no single policy reliably meets all these requirements. Vision-language-action models (VLAs) provide semantic grounding but struggle with long-horizon consistency ( Black et al., 2025b ; Kim et al., 2025 ; Zheng et al., 2025 ) . World-action models (WAMs) support predictive control but face prediction errors and inference latency ( Zhang et al., 2026b ) . Reinforcement learning (RL) post-training improves closed-loop control but depends on reward design and training coverage ( Zang et al., 2026 ; Physical Intelligence et al., 2025 ) . Task and motion planning (TAMP) offers logical and geometric reasoning but relies on predefined abstractions and skills ( Silver et al., 2023 ; Kumar et al., 2023 ; Liu et al., 2025 ; Huang et al., 2026c ) . These complementary strengths motivate coordinating heterogeneous policies for long-horizon execution. Such coordination presents two challenges. First, task decomposition and policy selection must account for uncertain, context-dependent capability boundaries. Conventional planners typically assume predefined skills with fixed applicability ( Liang et al., 2025 ; Han et al., 2024 ; Kaelbling and Lozano-Pérez, 2011 ) , whereas independently developed policies differ in architecture, interfaces, and assumptions ( Zhang et al., 2026b ; Garrett et al., 2021 ) . Their applicability may overlap, and relative performance varies with object configurations, observations, instructions, and execution history. Planners must therefore jointly determine task decomposition and policy assignment under current conditions. Second, successful policy selection does not ensure compatible handoffs: one policy’s terminal state may fall outside the next policy’s feasible execution region or input distribution ( Lee et al., 2022 ; Lee et al., 2019 ) , causing failures and cascading errors. Effective orchestration thus requires both capability-aware planning and reliable policy handoffs. We propose RoboHarness, a unified framework that exposes independently trained or hand-designed policies as reusable agentic skills through a common interface accommodating policy-specific inputs and outputs. It uses contextual and historical execution evidence to guide policy assignment, and the incoming policy’s retrieved experience to construct compatible handoffs. Our main contributions are: (1) We introduce RoboHarness, a unified framework that orchestrates heterogeneous robot policies as reusable agentic skills for long-horizon tasks. (2) We integrate understanding, memory, and self-evolution skills to characterize and adapt context-dependent policy capabilities, supporting capability-aware task decomposition and routing. (3) We introduce Memory Bridge, a policy-chaining algorithm that learns a local progress ranking from retrieved trajectories and selects reachable, memory-supported handoff configurations without joint policy retraining. (4) We evaluate RoboHarness across five public benchmarks, 500 customized tasks, and 135 real-robot trials, demonstrating substantial improvements in long-horizon and memory-dependent task success and out-of-distribution robustness.
