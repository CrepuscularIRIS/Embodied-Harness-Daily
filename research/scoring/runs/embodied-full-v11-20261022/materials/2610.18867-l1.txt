# EmbodiedEvo: Self-Evolving Coding Agent For Physical Intelligence

## Abstract
Coding agents can construct executable programs and revise them through feedback, offering a foundation for embodied control without target-task fine-tuning. Through a programmatic robot interface, the agent observes the scene, executes control programs, and adapts them to the resulting physical state, serving as the closed-loop policy. However, this within-episode adaptation does not automatically preserve experience across scenes. Our trajectory analysis shows that agents spend interaction and inference budgets establishing effective operations and identifying their preconditions, while fresh agents cannot reuse these discoveries. This motivates EmbodiedEvo, a self-evolving coding agent that distills physical feedback into reusable code Skills and diagnostic Lessons. Experience is acquired on one Playground scene per task, then frozen for independent agents in unseen scenes, without updating model weights. On RoboDojo-Sim, EmbodiedEvo achieves an average score of 44.48 and a success rate of 37.82\%, surpassing all officially verified results. Paired tests show the largest gains on memory-dependent tasks and successful reuse of coordinated procedures, while fine contact control remains difficult. Experience from stronger models also improves weaker executors: Astra's experience raises Sol and Luna's success rates by 8.86 and 5.50 percentage points over no experience. Compared with raw interaction history, Skills and Lessons achieve comparable task performance at 53.0\% lower execution cost.

## 1 Introduction
Coding agents offer a promising foundation for embodied control by combining program construction, tool use, and feedback-driven debugging [ 1 , 2 , 3 , 4 , 5 ] . Through a programmatic robot interface, these capabilities allow an agent to translate task goals into executable control procedures, inspect their physical outcomes, and revise the procedures as execution unfolds. The coding agent thus serves as the closed-loop policy, deciding what to observe, which programs to execute, and how to respond to feedback. This approach already shows promising zero-shot performance, where frontier agentic models could achieve a success rate comparable to VLA or WAM systems trained on target-similar scenes but in a zero-shot manner [ 6 ] . This highlights the potential of general-purpose coding capabilities for physical task execution. These zero-shot results, however, represent only a starting point for what coding agents can achieve in embodied control. In the zero-shot setting, an agent begins operating an unfamiliar embodiment without an opportunity to first learn from physical interaction. Understanding the task and the robot interface does not establish how to reliably realize an intended action: a valid command may move the gripper to the desired pose without securing the object. The agent must therefore discover effective operations and their preconditions during execution, spending part of its task budget familiarizing itself with the embodiment. While VLA and WAM systems acquire relevant experience through training on target-similar scenes, zero-shot coding agents undertake this adaptation while attempting to complete the task. This raises a natural question: can a coding agent improve its performance in unseen scenes if it is first given an opportunity to explore and acquire experience with the embodiment? To this end, we introduce EmbodiedEvo , a self-evolving coding agent that acquires reusable physical experience through dedicated interaction with its embodiment. In a Playground containing one scene per task, the agent explores, inspects physical feedback, and refines two complementary forms of experience: executable Skills that capture how to perform operations, and diagnostic Lessons that record their applicability conditions, observed failure modes, and recovery guidance. The resulting experience is then frozen and provided to fresh agents executing the same task in unseen layouts, without updating model weights. These agents reuse the acquired experience while continuing to adapt their control programs through closed-loop interaction. Experiments on RoboDojo [ 6 ] simulation tasks show that inherited experience improves subsequent execution. With task descriptions and wiki recipes, Astra/xhigh reaches 37.82% Average success rate over 50 Test scenes per task, compared with the published Astra result of 22.48% . Paired tests hold the executor and scenes fixed, raising success from 8.50% to 30.17% with task descriptions alone. The largest gains occur on memory-dependent tasks, alongside successful reuse of coordinated procedures such as block stacking; fine insertion and liquid handling remain difficult. Experience also transfers across models: Astra’s Skills and Lessons raise Sol and Luna’s success rates by 8.86 and 5.50 percentage points over no experience. These results distinguish the ability to produce useful control knowledge from the ability to apply it. Our contributions are: • A self-evolution framework for physical coding agents. We introduce EmbodiedEvo, a self-evolution coding agent. The agent autonomously observes, writes, and executes control programs, and retains exploration experience to improve subsequent physical task solving while model weights and the robot interface remain fixed. • Transferable physical experience from a single scene. Skills preserve executable procedures, while Lessons retain operating preconditions, failure modes, and recovery guidance, capturing both how to act and when an operation applies. Experience acquired on one Playground scene per task is frozen for independent Test agents, enabling evaluation of its reuse across unseen layouts and different models. • A systematic analysis of the benefits and limits of evolution. On RoboDojo-Sim, EmbodiedEvo achieves 44.48 Average Score and 37.82% success. Further analyses show that unfinished exploration can yield useful knowledge and that weaker executors can use experience they struggle to acquire. Skills and Lessons retain comparable task performance to raw interaction history at 53.0% lower Test execution cost, while precise contact control remains challenging.
