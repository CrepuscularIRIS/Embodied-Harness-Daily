# TESS: LLM-guided Translation, Exploration, Simulation, and Search for Embodied Task Planning

## Abstract
In this work we focus on the problem of embodied task planning, which requires an agent to accomplish a complex task by separating it into atomic actions. Recent studies have developed LLM-based methods to address this problem. However, the executability and correctness of the generated plans remain suboptimal in complex environments and tasks. On the other hand, classical search algorithms have the potential to solve the planning problems perfectly, yet they require fully observable environments, known state transitions and goal state, which are often unattainable in general task domains. Therefore, we propose to implement an LLM-guided search algorithm that makes the search process possible and efficient by incorporating LLMs' commonsense knowledge. Our method leverages LLMs to (1) translate natural language instructions into formatted goal conditions, (2) facilitate efficient exploration in partially observable environments, (3) implement a rule-based world simulator, and (4) prune the space of candidate actions to improve search efficiency. Experimental results on VirtualHome and ALFWorld demonstrate its superior performance.



## 1 Introduction
Planning is a fundamental capability for general-purpose robots. In the problem of Embodied Task Planning, low-level robotic control is abstracted into a set of atomic skills, and the agent is expected to complete a user-specified task by performing an appropriate sequence of these skills in an interactive environment. This process requires in-depth reasoning over the environment state, user’s instruction, and the history of executed actions. As a crucial step toward achieving embodied intelligence for solving complex tasks, this problem has attracted growing attention, and a number of simulators and benchmarks have been built Puig et al. (2018) ; Shridhar et al. (2021) ; Li et al. (2023) ; Li et al. (2024) . With the advancement of large language models (LLMs) in reasoning and planning tasks, many recent studies have explored using LLMs as the core component for embodied task planning. Typically, an LLM is prompted or finetuned to generate a full plan or function as an action policy. Despite the notable progress achieved by previous works through improved training strategies Li et al. (2022) ; Xiang et al. (2023) ; Hao et al. (2023b) ; Brahman et al. (2024) ; Li et al. (2025) and test-time scaling Zhao et al. (2023) ; Wang et al. (2024) ; Hu et al. (2024) ; Qian et al. (2025) , ensuring the executability and correctness of the generated plans remains a major challenge. The limitation may stem from the fact that LLMs still struggle with reasoning in long contexts (containing the description of a large-scale environment and an extended action history) and planning for complex tasks (analyzing the intent of the user, the dependencies among subtasks, and the feasibility of actions). From a different perspective, search-based planning is a well-established approach to task planning. Classical algorithms such as breadth-first search (BFS) are capable of finding a path from the initial state to a goal state, where each step corresponds to an atomic action that the agent should take. In essence, a search-based agent is performing trial-and-error within its mental simulation space. It can explicitly reason about the sequence of actions and their consequences, thus leading to a more transparent and sensible decision-making process than the black-box inference of LLMs. Yet, applying search in embodied environments still comes with several challenges: how to obtain a complete and reliable representation of the environment (under partial observability); how to implement accurate state transitions; and how to determine whether a goal state has been reached. In this work, we aim at utilizing LLMs to implement several modules that assist the search process, addressing the aforementioned challenges while improving search efficiency. A high-level pipeline is presented in Figure 1 . Specifically, we propose: (1) a T ranslation module that converts the language instructions into a set of grounded predicates, enabling convenient termination condition checking and task progress monitoring; (2) an E xploration module that leverages the commonsense knowledge of LLMs to guide targeted exploration of the goal-relevant objects; (3) a S imulation module that constructs a rule-based world model with a set of Python functions, allowing for prediction of the state transitions resulting from actions; and (4) a filtering module that progressively prunes the action space to improve S earch efficiency. The resulting agent, referred to as TESS, effectively combines the performance advantages of search-based planning with the rich prior knowledge encoded in LLMs. Moreover, by breaking down the planning problem into a series of subproblems, TESS enables a modular decision process that is more interpretable and transparent, allowing us to take a closer look at the effectiveness and limitations of LLMs in different stages of embodied planning. In brief, the main contributions of this work include: (1) We propose TESS, an LLM-guided search-based pipeline for Embodied Task Planning. (2) We build a new dataset based on the VirtualHome Simulator. It encompasses tasks of varying difficulty, human-like instructions, and diverse environments, serving as a suitable benchmark for automated and accurate evaluation of planning. (3) Extensive experiments on the proposed benchmark and ALFWorld validate the effectiveness of TESS.
