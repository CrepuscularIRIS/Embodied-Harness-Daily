# EmbodiedSkills: World Action Model-augmented Cascaded Skills for Vision-Language-Action Agent

## Abstract
Vision-language-action (VLA) policies generate executable robot actions, but plausible proposals can have different consequences for the task. We present EmbodiedSkills, a skill-based agent framework that augments frozen VLA policies with world action models for consequence-aware action selection. The framework defines reusable skills for scene observation, context encoding, candidate generation, consequence prediction, outcome evaluation, candidate comparison, and action execution. Five stages organize their input-output dependencies in a shared feedback loop, accommodating both continuous action chunks and pick-and-place primitives. At each decision point, independently trained world action models predict the latent consequences of a common set of policy proposals. Selection combines their outcome scores and prediction disagreement with a lightweight comparator that estimates the benefit of an alternative from agreement in its predicted effects. The predictive and comparison modules learn from matched-state candidate branches, while the action policy and pretrained observation encoders remain frozen. On MetaWorld MT50 and CLIPort, EmbodiedSkills improves success rates from 76.28% to 78.64% and from 33.94% to 36.83%, respectively. An additional CALVIN evaluation with the frozen FLOWER policy increases five-task-chain success from 77.10% to 77.90%. These results demonstrate the applicability of consequence-aware selection across different policy backends and action representations.

## 1 Introduction

Vision–language–action (VLA) policies provide a reusable foundation for instruction-conditioned robot control, translating visual observations and language goals into executable actions Zitkovich et al. (2023) ; Kim et al. (2025) ; Black et al. (2025a) . Generative policies can represent multiple ways to act from the same observation, including alternative motion trajectories and action chunks Chi et al. (2025) ; Black et al. (2024) . This capability creates a decision problem at execution time: plausible actions can lead to different physical outcomes. When placing an object, for example, small differences in approach or release position can determine whether it reaches the intended location. Generating candidate actions alone does not establish which candidate is more likely to achieve the goal. This motivates a method that augments VLA policies with the ability to anticipate and compare candidate outcomes before execution. Action-conditioned world models offer a way to anticipate these outcomes. Visual foresight predicts future observations under proposed actions Finn and Levine (2017) , while latent world models such as DINO-WM and V-JEPA 2 support prediction and planning in learned representations Zhou et al. (2025) ; Assran et al. (2025) . Recent policy-steering methods, including GPC, FOREWARN, and DREAMSTEER, further demonstrate how predicted consequences can inform the selection of actions proposed by an existing policy Qi et al. (2026) ; Wu et al. (2025) ; Cui et al. (2026) . These advances motivate combining the action-generation capability of VLA policies with the predictive capability of world models. The resulting decisions also depend on the reliability of the forecasts: a high score from one predictor may reflect an inaccurate consequence estimate. Making this combination reusable requires a framework that accommodates different policy backends and world models while preserving a consistent decision and execution workflow as components change. Skill-based robotic agents offer a useful foundation for organizing such capabilities Ichter et al. (2023) . Representing observation, action proposal, prediction, selection, and execution as callable skills with shared interfaces allows their implementations to be adapted or replaced within a common feedback loop. Following this design, we introduce EmbodiedSkills , a skill-based agent framework that augments frozen VLA policies with world action models. An embodied skill is a reusable operation with defined inputs and outputs in the perception–decision–action loop. Five stages organize these operations: Observe uses scene observation and context encoding; Propose uses candidate generation; Predict uses consequence prediction and outcome evaluation; Select uses candidate comparison; and Execute uses action execution. Stages specify the information dependencies, while skills supply the capabilities that satisfy them. Their interfaces preserve the correspondence between a proposed action, its predicted effect, and the action sent to the environment. Policy-specific action representations and model-specific predictive features are handled within the corresponding skills, allowing implementations to be adapted or replaced while preserving the five-stage workflow. Within this loop, three independently trained world action models predict latent consequences and goal-conditioned outcome scores for the same policy proposals. Selection first aggregates their scores while accounting for disagreement, then applies a lightweight learned correction based on agreement in the predicted effects. The comparator estimates whether replacing the policy’s direct proposal is likely to improve or damage the outcome. Prediction and comparison are supervised by candidate branches executed from matched initial states: observed transitions provide consequence targets, and branch outcomes provide action-quality labels. During deployment, the models consume current observations and proposed actions; future observations and branch outcomes are reserved for supervision and evaluation. This design concentrates adaptation in additional predictive and comparison modules while keeping the base policy and pretrained observation encoders frozen. Latent prediction avoids pixel-level future reconstruction in the decision loop, and the skill abstraction accommodates both continuous control and manipulation primitives. We instantiate the framework with \pi_{0.5} on MetaWorld MT50 Black et al. (2025a) ; Yu et al. (2020) and with the language-conditioned CLIPort policy Shridhar et al. (2022) . The framework increases complete-task success from 76.28% to 78.64% on MT50 and from 33.94% to 36.83% on CLIPort. Ablations examine consequence supervision, joint predictive learning, language conditioning, and model disagreement, while alternative visual representations demonstrate component reuse within the same workflow. These evaluations cover repeated chunk selection on MT50 and initial-primitive selection on CLIPort. Additional comparisons examine inference-time policy augmentation methods, and a CALVIN five-task-chain study Mees et al. (2022) extends the framework to the frozen FLOWER backend Reuss et al. (2025) , improving complete-chain success from 77.10% to 77.90%. Our contributions are threefold: • A skill-based loop for predictive action selection. Five stages compose observation, encoding, generation, prediction, evaluation, comparison, and execution skills through explicit input–output relationships, preserving candidate identity from policy proposal to physical execution. • Branch-supervised prediction and consequence comparison. Independently trained world action models provide candidate outcomes and scores; a shared comparator uses predicted-effect agreement to refine disagreement-aware selection relative to the direct policy proposal. • Modular integration of policy backends and world models. Shared skill interfaces support the adaptation and replacement of action-policy backends and world action models, accommodating different action representations and predictive features within the same agent workflow.
