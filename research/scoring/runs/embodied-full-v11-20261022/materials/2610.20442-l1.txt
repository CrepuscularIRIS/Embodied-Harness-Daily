# Towards Agentic Robots: Model, Data, Environment, and Harness

## Abstract
Foundation models are driving a paradigm shift in robotic intelligence, moving beyond low-level action prediction to goal-directed orchestration of data collection, model training, task planning, and real-world execution. We characterize this emerging paradigm as an agentic robots, in which agents reason over human instructions, interact with robotic environments, rectify execution errors autonomously, and dynamically adjust operational strategies. Although this field develops fast, current studies are not well connected. Researchers have no consistent view of how to combine different agentic functions into a complete robot system. This paper provides a unified overview of existing research on agentic robots. We first trace the development from robot policies to agentic systems, covering vision-language-action models, world models, and world-action models. We then clarify the definition and scope of agentic robots and organize existing research into seventeen categories across four functional domains: Model, Data, Environment, and Harness. Across these domains, we examine how agents shape model learning and robot decisions, collect and curate robot data, construct worlds, tasks, and tests for learning and evaluation, and organize capabilities for robot use. Finally, we discuss open challenges and outline directions for future research. Looking forward, we hope this work contributes to a clearer understanding of agentic robots and supports new approaches to making them more capable, reliable, and adaptive.

## 1 Introduction
Over the past few years, foundation models including large language models (LLMs) and multimodal large language models (MLLMs) have greatly advanced robotic policy learning. They endow robots with novel capabilities that traditional task-specific models lack, especially in scene and task understanding and cross-scene generalization, by scaling robot datasets and model size [ 20 , 257 ] . As a dominant technical solution in this field, vision-language-action (VLA) models utilize either discrete action tokens or diffusion- or flow-matching-based continuous action prediction strategies to connect robotic action prediction and the semantic knowledge embedded in pre-trained LLMs [ 19 , 13 , 269 ] . Nevertheless, improved action prediction alone cannot guarantee reliable execution for long-horizon tasks. These tasks demand robots to track task progress, adjust plans amid environmental changes, and recover from failures. VLA policies by themselves cannot consistently deliver these capabilities. Why Agentic Robots Matter? Recently, agentic robots are attracting surging attention in the research community, which represent a new generation of embodied intelligent systems that integrate foundation model reasoning with embodied agency and environmental interaction capabilities. General-purpose models such as GPT-6 Astra [ 260 ] and Gemini 4 Argon [ 88 ] are opening new possibilities for such systems. For example, GPT-6 Astra demonstrates promising capabilities as a robot manipulation agent, using visual observations to generate end-effector commands and adjust its actions from execution feedback without task-specific fine-tuning [ 440 ] . On RoboDojo, it achieves a 22.48% average success rate across 42 simulation tasks, outperforming the trained robot policy DM0.5 (19.34%) [ 60 ] , GalaxeaVLA G0.5 (14.88%) [ 77 ] , Xiaomi-Robotics-1 (13.93%) [ 385 ] , OpenWAM- \alpha (11.92%) [ 364 ] and \pi_{0.5} (6.91%) [ 269 ] . Despite this progress, inference latency and resource costs highlight the importance of how foundation models are integrated into robotic systems. Beyond optimizing and scaling model parameters, agentic robots use foundation models to plan actions, monitor task progress, and revise decisions based on execution feedback. Their capabilities therefore depend on both the model and the tools, observations, and feedback available in the environment. The agentic robot paradigm addresses the “brain in a vat” limitation of previous robotic solutions, shifting robotic intelligence from static pattern matching toward adaptive autonomous operation. Agentic Robots Need a Taxonomy. Despite growing interest, research on agentic robots remains fragmented across model learning, data acquisition, environment construction, and runtime system design. These directions are often studied separately, although their contributions interact within a robotic system. Related terms such as embodied agents , autonomous robots , and self-improving robotic systems further blur the scope of the field by emphasizing different capabilities without consistently clarifying their boundaries. Together, these issues make it difficult to identify where agents make decisions and how those decisions contribute to the overall system. A unified taxonomy should therefore distinguish the roles of individual components while explaining how they work together. To this end, we organize agentic robotics into four functional domains: Model , Data , Environment , and Harness , tracing the agent’s decisions within each domain and their effects across the system. Key Questions 1 Model: What should the agent learn, understand, decide, and control? 2 Data: How should the agent collect, annotate, and refine robot data? 3 Environment: What worlds, tasks, and tests should the agent build? 4 Harness: How should robot capabilities be orchestrated, recovered, and evolved? The four questions above guide our discussion of how agents contribute to robotic systems. Section 4 examines how agents support model learning and robot decision-making, while Section 5 explains how they collect, annotate, and refine robot data. Section 6 considers the worlds, tasks, and tests agents construct for learning and evaluation. Section 7 examines how agents coordinate executable capabilities, monitor execution, recover from failures, and retain useful knowledge for subsequent tasks. Contributions. This paper makes three main contributions: • A Unified Illustration for Agentic Robotics. We trace the evolution from robot policies to agentic systems and clarify the role of agents in robot task execution and capability development. We introduce a taxonomy spanning Model , Data , Environment , and Harness , bringing these research directions into a common framework. • A Systematic Analysis of Agentic Methods. We synthesize the mechanisms through which agents acquire, coordinate, and improve robotic capabilities. By comparing representative approaches across the taxonomy, we explain how agent decisions depend on available tools, robot executors, and interaction feedback, and examine the strengths and limitations of these approaches. • A Research Agenda for Practical Agentic Robots. Drawing on this analysis, we outline research directions for establishing agents’ contributions and translating current advances into reliable, efficient robot operation. The agenda connects fair evaluation with the challenges of physical deployment and generalization across robotic platforms.
