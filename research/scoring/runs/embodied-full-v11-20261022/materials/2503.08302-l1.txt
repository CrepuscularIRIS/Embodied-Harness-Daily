# General-Purpose Aerial Intelligent Agents Empowered by Large Language Models

## Abstract
The emergence of large language models (LLMs) opens new frontiers for unmanned aerial vehicle (UAVs), yet existing systems remain confined to predefined tasks due to hardware-software co-design challenges. This paper presents the first aerial intelligent agent capable of open-world task execution through tight integration of LLM-based reasoning and robotic autonomy. Our hardware-software co-designed system addresses two fundamental limitations: (1) Onboard LLM operation via an edge-optimized computing platform, achieving 5-6 tokens/sec inference for 14B-parameter models at 220W peak power; (2) A bidirectional cognitive architecture that synergizes slow deliberative planning (LLM task planning) with fast reactive control (state estimation, mapping, obstacle avoidance, and motion planning). Validated through preliminary results using our prototype, the system demonstrates reliable task planning and scene understanding in communication-constrained environments, such as sugarcane monitoring, power grid inspection, mine tunnel exploration, and biological observation applications. This work establishes a novel framework for embodied aerial artificial intelligence, bridging the gap between task planning and robotic autonomy in open environments.

## I Introduction
Although UAVs have advanced significantly, they are still mostly limited to performing tasks that are specifically designed and pre-programmed. Typical applications of UAVs include precision agriculture, power grid inspection, mining industry, biological observations, etc. With the rise of LLMs and visual-language models (VLMs), an important question arises: Can UAVs carry out tasks based on open-set language instructions and interact with open-set objects? Is it possible in a few-shot even a zero-shot manner? We refer to such LLM-empowered UAVs that can perform general-purpose tasks in open-world as aerial intelligent agents (AIA) . To build an aerial intelligent agent, the integration of fast reactive behaviors and slow deliberative reasoning remains a critical challenge, particularly in open environments requiring real-time responsiveness and a long-horizon planning. Inspired by the dual-process theory of human cognition [ 1 ] , this paper proposes a hardware-software co-designed framework. It embeds fast thinking for agile reaction and slow reasoning for long-horizon task-planning into aerial intelligent agents. There are several challenges to implement an aerial intelligent agent. First, LLMs are characterized by their vast parameter sizes and training on internet-scale datasets. Due to limitations in weight, power consumption, and size of UAVs, designing an appropriate edge computing platform and selecting an proper LLM is crucial. Current UAVs designed for specific tasks do not have the computing power to run an LLM onboard. Although it is possible to run the LLM on a ground station and communicate with the UAV wirelessly, this approach is limited by bandwidth and introduces risks such as communication instability and latency. Moreover, it is unsuitable for general-purpose tasks in open world that might have poor communication conditions. Second, there is a lack of systematic guidelines for prompt design that integrating the slow deliberative reasoning ability of LLMs and fast reactive abilities. Common reaction includes state estimation [ 2 ] , perception, obstacle avoidance, motion planning [ 3 ] , and control. Recent efforts to integrate LLMs with UAVs predominantly focus on cloud-dependent architectures or offline task planning [ 4 ] , which fundamentally constrain real-time adaptability in open environments. While edge computing solutions have emerged for ground robots [ 5 ] , their direct adaptation to aerial platforms remains impractical due to stringent power-weight tradeoffs and vibration-induced computational instability. In this paper, to enable the implementation of aerial intelligent agents, we present a hardware platform and a prompt-design framework for LLM-empowered UAVs. The major contributions in this work are summarized as follows: • A novel hardware-software co-design framework featuring an edge computing for an UAV prototype. The embedded computing module enables efficient LLM inference (14B parameters DeepSeek-R1 LLM [ 6 ] or 11B parameters Meta Llama 3.2 Vision [ 7 ] at 5-6 tokens/second) while maintaining power efficiency ( 220 W peak consumption). • We propose a prompt design framework that integrates LLM-based reasoning with conventional UAV autonomy modules. Our architecture achieves bidirectional integration between high-level task planning (via LLMs) and low-level reaction pipelines (state estimation, motion planning, control, etc). • Using the prototype of our hardware platform, we validate the effectiveness of LLMs/VLMs running onboard. We test the performance through mission-critical applications, including sugarcane monitoring, power grid inspection, mine tunnel exploration, and biological observation, demonstrating the generalization ability of task planning and scene understanding in real-world scenarios.
