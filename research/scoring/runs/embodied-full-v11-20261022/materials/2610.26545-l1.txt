# ReInsVLN: Revisiting Historical Evidence for Training-Free Vision-and-Language Navigation

## Abstract
Vision-and-language navigation (VLN) relies on retaining and using relevant observations. Yet growing histories introduce redundancy, and retained information may remain underused. We introduce ReInsBench, a diagnostic benchmark that examines history utilization through controlled continuation trials. Its results show that history-review reinstruction can recover navigation failures using the existing task context. Motivated by this finding, we propose ReInsVLN, a training-free framework supporting memory construction and utilization in frozen navigators. Coverage- and Instruction-Aware Compression constructs compact visual context through temporal redundancy reduction, coverage-aware protection, and instruction-guided token selection. Forgetting-Inspired History Reminders guide navigators to revisit retained observations according to a predefined schedule, without additional model calls. On R2R Val-Unseen, ReInsVLN improves success rates by 8.48 and 3.37 percentage points over the native StreamVLN and InternVLA-N1, reaching 64.27% and 64.44%, respectively. Visual compression largely preserves navigation performance while reducing prefill latency and KV cache memory by up to 21.77% and 32.25%, respectively, on matched observation workloads. Real-world trials on humanoid and quadruped robots further support the applicability of ReInsVLN to sequential instruction following and ordinal navigation.



## I Introduction
Fig. 1: Navigation memory requires retaining and using relevant observations. Although the blue cabinet is no longer visible, its retained relation to the first armchair helps identify the next armchair as the stopping point. Vision-and-language navigation (VLN) requires an agent to follow natural-language instructions using a sequence of visual observations [ 1 , 2 ] . Partial observability makes decisions depend on previously encountered landmarks [ 3 ] . As illustrated in Fig. 1 , following the instruction “Pass the armchair beside the blue cabinet, then stop at the next armchair” requires the navigator to use the previously observed relation between the cabinet and the first armchair to identify the next armchair as the stopping point. Such dependencies make memory essential for retaining information from past observations and using it to guide subsequent actions [ 4 ] . Effective navigation memory involves two complementary capabilities: memory construction and memory utilization . Here, memory construction selects and organizes past observations into available context, while memory utilization uses that context to guide decisions. Spatial maps offer one way to organize navigation history [ 5 ] . As navigation proceeds, repeated views introduce redundancy into the growing observation history, increasing the cost of processing it [ 6 , 7 , 8 , 9 ] . This requires retaining useful historical information within limited context and computation budgets, while preserving cues for later decisions. However, retained information does not guarantee effective use, even in long-context language models [ 10 ] . In the example in Fig. 1, the cabinet observation may remain in the navigator’s history without guiding it to stop at the correct armchair. This raises a central question: even when relevant historical information is retained, can the navigator use it effectively? To examine this question, we introduce ReInsBench , a diagnostic benchmark constructed from selected R2R [ 1 ] navigation episodes. At selected decision points, a frozen navigator continues under different prompts from the same observation history and environment state. The benchmark assesses whether directing the navigator to revisit its existing history improves subsequent navigation, using neutral reminders to control for the effect of generic prompting. In a controlled subset of 198 instances, history review yields 64 successful continuations, compared with 53 for neutral reminders and 40 without an additional prompt. These findings suggest that navigators do not always make effective use of the historical information already available to them. Building on this diagnosis, we aim to improve memory construction and utilization within existing navigators. A compact visual context must preserve cues whose relevance may emerge only later [ 11 , 12 ] . Once retained, these cues need to inform subsequent decisions at appropriate moments, requiring guidance that encourages their use while limiting interference with otherwise correct navigation. To this end, we propose ReInsVLN , a training-free framework that combines visual context compression and history-review reminders within the existing context mechanisms of navigators. For memory construction, we adopt a two-stage visual compression strategy consisting of pre-LLM compression and in-LLM compression. The pre-LLM stage reduces temporal redundancy before visual tokens enter the language model, combining causal token reduction with dynamic-token protection to prioritize content poorly covered by previously retained tokens. The in-LLM stage builds a query from the original instruction and expands it with high-scoring visual seeds. It then combines semantic relevance with positional information to select a compact subset of visual tokens, restoring their original order after selection. For memory utilization, short reminders guide the navigator to revisit retained observations in relation to the original instruction. A predefined, forgetting-inspired schedule uses the number of decision calls elapsed since the previous reminder as a temporal prior for determining when to introduce history review. The prompts are inserted at existing decision calls, preserving the native action-execution pipeline. Experiments on R2R Val-Unseen evaluate ReInsVLN with StreamVLN and InternVLA-N1, representing streaming and non-streaming inference, respectively. With Coverage- and Instruction-Aware Compression and Forgetting-Inspired History Reminders applied together, ReInsVLN improves success rates by 8.48 and 3.37 points over the corresponding native navigators. These improvements exceed the sum of individual gains on both navigators, suggesting synergy between two components. Visual compression also reduces prefill latency and KV cache usage. Physical deployment is another focus of legged-robot VLN [ 13 ] . The main contributions are summarized as follows: • We introduce ReInsBench to diagnose history utilization through controlled prompting comparisons, showing that history-review reinstruction can recover navigation failures using the existing task context. • We develop Coverage- and Instruction-Aware Compression for memory construction in ReInsVLN, combining temporal redundancy reduction with instruction-guided visual token selection. • We introduce Forgetting-Inspired History Reminders to support memory utilization by guiding frozen navigators to revisit retained observations according to a predefined schedule, without additional model calls. • Simulation experiments show navigation gains on both navigators, together with reduced prefill latency and KV cache storage. Real-world trials on humanoid and quadruped robots further support the training-free generalization of ReInsVLN to challenging tasks requiring sequential landmark tracking.
