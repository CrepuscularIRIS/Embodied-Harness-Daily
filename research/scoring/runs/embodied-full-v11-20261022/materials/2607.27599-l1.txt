# World Action Planner: Generalizable Robot Decision-Making with Action-Conditioned World Models

## Abstract
Building generalizable robot agents for diverse applications remains a fundamental challenge. While imitation learning-based policies can perform well in familiar training environments, they often struggle to generalize to novel scenes, layouts, and task compositions. To this end, we present World Action Planner, an agentic robot planning system in which the agent searches for and composes executable action plans through imagination with an action-conditioned world model. The search proceeds in a coarse-to-fine manner. First, the agent performs global action optimization by reasoning over imagined world-model rollouts to identify potential failures and refine the proposed action plan. It then performs local action search, comparing the imagined future outcomes of neighboring candidates to select the best action for execution. Across compositional long-horizon tasks, novel object layouts, and real-robot planning on novel tasks without expert demonstrations, World Action Planner consistently outperforms state-of-the-art end-to-end generalist policy models and VLM planners, demonstrating the effectiveness of world-model-based action search for generalizable robot decision making. Qualitative results and videos are available at https://worldactionplanner.github.io/

## 1 Introduction
Developing generalizable robotic agents capable of performing diverse tasks across heterogeneous environments remains a fundamental challenge in AI. Recent progress has been driven by end-to-end (E2E) imitation learning-based policies that leverage pretrained Vision-Language Models (VLMs) ( Intelligence et al., 2025 ) or video generation models ( Ye et al., 2026 ) as backbones. However, these approaches are often fundamentally limited by the scope of the demonstration data available during training. For instance, a policy trained only on individual pick-and-place trajectories may fail to navigate between objects when executing composed, long-horizon tasks. Furthermore, E2E models can overfit to spurious motion patterns in demonstrations; if the training data consistently depicts grasping at a specific coordinate, the policy may continue targeting that position even after the object has moved. Consequently, these models struggle in novel scenarios that require systematic reasoning or flexible planning beyond the trajectory distributions observed during training. In this work, we depart from the end-to-end (E2E) imitation learning paradigm and instead propose an agentic planning framework centered on a generalizable world model that can faithfully imagine the outcomes of diverse robot actions. When facing a new task, the agent can synthesize novel action plans through search and composition within world-model imagination, enabling behaviors that remain challenging for end-to-end policies tightly coupled to previous expert trajectories. Instead of requiring demonstrations from the same task and environment, we only need to learn reusable dynamics with a world model, potentially from data collected across different tasks and environments, and then search directly for executable robot actions through imagination under the task objective in the current environment. This decouples decision making from the expert demonstrations seen during training and enables systematic generalization to novel states, tasks, and environments. To this end, we propose World Action Planner (Fig. 1 ), a world-model-based robot planning system designed to solve new tasks in novel scenes and layouts. We train a generalizable video world model conditioned on robot pose images (Fig. 2 ), enabling precise conditioning on robot actions and high-fidelity prediction of physical interactions. By imagining future action rollouts, the world model reveals their physical consequences before execution, allowing an out-of-the-box VLM to evaluate predicted outcomes and provide feedback for action optimization. To efficiently search the action space, we introduce a coarse-to-fine action search hierarchy that combines agent-driven global action optimization with physically grounded local action candidate search, both performed by reasoning over imagined world-model rollouts (Fig. 3 ). We demonstrate the generalization capability of our method across compositional tasks, novel layouts, and novel task scenarios without expert demonstration in both simulation and real-world environments. In compositional long-horizon tasks, our method successfully handles transitions between subtasks by proposing and refining maneuvers through world-model imagination, whereas end-to-end models such as VLAs often stagnate after completing the first subtask. In spatial generalization settings with modified object positions, our method identifies the updated target locations and completes the task, while imitation learning policies tend to reach toward object coordinates observed during training. By reasoning over imagined world-model rollouts, global optimization identifies and avoids physical risks such as collisions, significantly improving safety and success rates in real-robot settings. Local action search further evaluates neighboring candidates to identify precise actions for fine-grained manipulation tasks such as mug grasping and cube stacking. Together, these components form a coarse-to-fine action search strategy that achieves substantially greater planning efficiency than conventional action-sampling methods such as MPPI ( Hansen et al., 2023 ) and Best-of-N ( Cui et al., 2026 ) . We summarize our main contributions as follows: (i) we propose World Action Planner, an agentic planning system that proposes and refines robot actions through a physically grounded coarse-to-fine search over imagination from a generalizable robot pose-image conditioned world model; (ii) we provide theoretical insights into the multi-task generalization advantages of model-based planning over imitation learning; and (iii) we demonstrate that our method significantly outperforms SOTA policies and VLM planners across compositional tasks, novel layouts, and novel task generalization scenarios.
