# CAFE: Closed-Loop Autonomous Task Formulation and Execution without Instance-Specific Instructions

## Abstract
Scene-driven robot autonomy requires selecting tasks from observations as well as executing them. We study scene-driven task formulation and execution under predefined cleaning, safety, and storage/organization categories, without instance-specific goals or action sequences. CAFE maintains online memory of objects, functional zones, and execution states. A rule-guided Trigger-LLM admits formulation from accumulated evidence; Draft, Complete, and Audit propose goals, resolve execution details, and check dependencies. Outcomes update memory for correction and subsequent discovery. Evaluation in 12 AI2-THOR scenes compares three backbones and task-complexity tiers using coverage, constraint, and execution metrics. Tri-LLM records higher GCR scores than the evaluated baselines across three backbones and task-complexity tiers. Controls examine completion, auditing, zones, feedback, triggering, and prompt examples. LimoCobot traces illustrate storage, cleanup, and recovery from a failed grasp. The study examines how shared scene state connects task discovery to execution and outcome-driven revision.

## I Introduction
Language and vision-language models have extended robotic planning to goal-directed navigation, open-vocabulary manipulation, and long-horizon execution [ 1 , 2 , 3 , 4 ] . PaLM-E and SayCan connect supplied goals to observations and executable skills [ 5 , 6 ] . Exploration can recover missing information for an instruction [ 7 ] , while task-discovery methods identify opportunities from environmental and human cues [ 8 ] . A robot operating without scene-specific instructions must additionally decide which observed conditions call for action and when enough evidence is available to formulate a task. This setting requires a recurrent connection between exploration, formulation, and execution. An observation of a dirty bowl may suggest washing, but an executable plan also needs a sink, tools, preparation steps, and dependencies. Those bindings must remain available as the robot moves, and observed action outcomes must update the state used to select subsequent tasks. Persistent scene representations provide spatial and semantic context [ 9 , 10 ] ; observation filtering helps select useful evidence [ 11 ] . CAFE connects that evidence to concrete proposals, resolves their physical parameters and dependencies, and revises behavior after execution. Our objective is to connect scene-supported task discovery, executable-plan formation, and outcome-driven revision in a recurrent loop. CAFE addresses this objective under predefined cleaning, safety, and storage/organization categories. The robot receives no instance-specific goal, target-object list, or action sequence. Online semantic memory stores objects, functional zones, and execution states. Trigger-LLM admits formulation from accumulated evidence, and a staged Tri-LLM pipeline separates Draft, Complete, and Audit. Execution outcomes update the same memory for recovery and subsequent task discovery. The contributions are: • A memory-centered autonomy loop linking scene-driven task discovery to execution-state updates without instance-specific human task instructions. • A mechanism for recurrent task formulation from scene state, implemented through selective admission, task-status context, and Draft–Complete–Audit reasoning. • Evaluation across three backbones and complexity tiers, with controls for formulation, feedback, triggering, and prompt examples, plus physical storage, cleanup, and grasp-recovery demonstrations.

## III Method
Fig. 1 guides the method: the left module maintains memory and admits reasoning; the center converts opportunities into an ordered task sequence; the right executes it and returns outcomes to memory for correction and subsequent discovery. III-A Semantic State and Autonomy Scope The memory module in Fig. 1 operates under a fixed discovery prompt specifying cleaning, safety, and storage/organization. No scene-specific goal, target list, or action sequence is supplied. For t\geq 1 , current observations and preceding action outcomes form evidence D_{t} : M_{t}=\Phi(M_{t-1},D_{t}),\quad M_{t}=\{\mathcal{O}_{t},\mathcal{Z}_{t},\mathcal{X}_{t}\},\quad M_{0}=\emptyset. (1) \mathcal{O}_{t} stores grounded objects and states, \mathcal{Z}_{t} functional zones, and \mathcal{X}_{t} timestamps, confidence, and execution status. The maintained M_{t} supplies a shared state interface for triggering, formulation, and feedback. A zone’s landmark bounding volumes are projected onto the ground plane; their minimum and maximum coordinates define B_{z}=(x_{\min},x_{\max},y_{\min},y_{\max}) . Objects inside this footprint are assigned to the zone; otherwise assignment remains deferred until further spatial evidence is available. In Fig. 2 , sink/tools support washing, refrigerator/shelf storage, and worktops preparation. Zones group objects by their functional workspaces. III-B Selective Discovery Admission At each heartbeat, the Trigger-LLM in Fig. 1 compares M_{t} with a frozen reference \hat{M}_{t} , initialized as \hat{M}_{1}=M_{0} . The comparison includes new objects, object–zone associations, state or affordance changes, and execution outcomes. Context C_{t} records ongoing/completed tasks and outcomes. Prompt P_{\mathrm{trig}} instructs the model to identify scene-supported candidates, remove equivalent cues, and return a decision, distinct-candidate count, and brief reason: (\tilde{r}_{t},\hat{K}_{t})=\Psi_{\mathrm{LLM}}(M_{t},\hat{M}_{t},C_{t},P_{\mathrm{trig}}). (2) \tilde{r}_{t} is the recommendation and \hat{K}_{t} the estimated distinct-candidate count. The actual decision r_{t} is Trigger if \hat{K}_{t}\geq 4 or at least 20 objects are new relative to \hat{M}_{t} , and No Trigger otherwise: \hat{M}_{t+1}=\begin{cases}M_{t},&r_{t}=\mathrm{Trigger},\\ \hat{M}_{t},&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases} (3) Between formulation rounds, execution/exploration continues and the reference stays frozen, accumulating evidence across observations. The trigger admits high-level task candidates whose execution parameters are resolved by the subsequent formulation stages. Failure correction bypasses this discovery gate. The controls compare triggering policies and heartbeat intervals using the admission rules above (Sec. IV-D ). III-C Staged Task Formulation The central module in Fig. 1 grounds admitted candidates through three reasoning roles: \displaystyle\mathcal{T}_{t}^{(A)} \displaystyle=\operatorname{DraftLLM}(M_{t},C_{t}), (4) \displaystyle\mathcal{T}_{t}^{(B)} \displaystyle=\operatorname{CompleteLLM}(\mathcal{T}_{t}^{(A)},M_{t}), (5) \displaystyle\Pi_{t} \displaystyle=\operatorname{AuditLLM}(\mathcal{T}_{t}^{(B)},M_{t}). (6) The three operators implement task proposal, completion, and auditing. Draft proposes \tau_{j}=\langle a_{j},\mathrm{target}_{j},z_{j},\mathrm{pred}_{j},p_{j},g_{j}\rangle : action, target, zone, prerequisites, priority, and geometry. Draft is instructed to reference entities and zones in M_{t} and flag unresolved geometric bindings. Complete receives the flagged draft in a single forward pass. Its prompt requires resolving targets, spatial associations, and geometry from memory. The retained structure is the high-level goal set and inter-goal dependencies; Complete may add atomic navigation, preparation, and manipulation steps to realize these goals, without proposing new goals or unobserved entities. Audit is prompted to check semantic consistency, object availability, safety, and predecessor coherence, then repair recoverable inconsistencies or reject unsupported candidates. Ordering prioritizes safety, task completeness, and human-aware preferences subject to prerequisites. These roles preserve the goal layer while refining its execution layer. For potato storage, Complete expands the goal into navigation, pickup, refrigerator opening, and insertion; Audit checks the opening-before-insertion dependency. Task status in C_{t} carries ongoing and completed goals into subsequent trigger and Draft calls, connecting action outcomes to goal de-duplication. Fig. 2 grounds washing, storage, and preparation zones from N1–N4. The bowl is bound to a sink and cloth; washing requires faucet activation followed by shutoff. The action frames connect these bindings to execution. (7) u_{t} denotes executor output, y_{t}^{+} post-action observations, and e_{t}^{\mathrm{fb}} status/state mismatch.
