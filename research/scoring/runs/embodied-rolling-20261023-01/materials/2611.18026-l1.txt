# A Generalizable Approach to Safety-Aware Robotic Behavior Planning with an Explicit Constraint Space

## Abstract
Large language and vision-language models have substantially advanced robotic behavior planning from natural-language instructions and visual observations. However, safe manipulation in tabletop environments remains challenging because the safety constraints required for a task depend on the surrounding scene and may not be explicitly specified in the instruction. We propose a safety-aware behavior planning framework based on an explicit Constraint Space that stores reusable textual safety rules. Rather than retrieving rules solely by semantic similarity, we formulate constraint selection as task- and environment-conditioned applicability learning. A lightweight query-conditioned dual-tower model identifies the constraints applicable to the current context, which are then provided together with the original task and image to a multimodal behavior planner. To handle incorrect constraint selections and previously unseen hazards, we introduce a multimodal Safety Inspector that evaluates generated primitive plans, reconstructs the complete applicable constraint set, and provides corrective planning feedback. Validated inspection feedback is further used for test-time adaptation of constraint applicability and parameter-efficient adaptation of the Safety Inspector through LoRA, while newly identified constraints are incorporated into the Constraint Space. The resulting framework integrates explicit safety knowledge representation,context-aware constraint selection, corrective safety inspection, and test-time knowledge adaptation for safety-aware robotic behavior planning.

## I INTRODUCTION
Large language models (LLMs) and vision-language models (VLMs) have significantly advanced robotic behavior planning by enabling robots to interpret natural-language instructions, understand visual scenes, and generate long-horizon action sequences [ 1 , 2 , 3 ] . However, completing a task does not necessarily imply completing it safely. In tabletop manipulation, the same instruction may involve different safety requirements depending on the surrounding environment. For example, manipulating a liquid near electronic devices or operating a sharp object near a human requires safety considerations that may not be explicitly stated in the task instruction. Therefore, a safety-aware robot must not only understand what task to perform, but also identify the safety constraints imposed by the current task and environment [ 4 ] . To make such safety knowledge explicit and reusable, we maintain a Constraint Space consisting of textual safety rules. This representation separates safety knowledge from the planner itself and allows the same constraints to be reused across different tasks and scenes. However, maintaining a rule library introduces another challenge: only a small subset of constraints is typically applicable to a particular task and environment. Selecting rules merely according to semantic similarity does not necessarily reflect whether a constraint should actually be enforced in the current scene. The key problem is therefore to determine the applicability of each constraint conditioned jointly on the task and the environment. We address this problem with a lightweight query-conditioned constraint applicability model. Given a natural-language task and a visual observation, the environment is first represented in textual form and combined with the task to form a contextual query. A dual-tower model then evaluates the constraints in the Constraint Space and selects an applicable subset, which is provided together with the original task and image to a multimodal behavior planner. In this way, safety constraints are explicitly selected before planning rather than being left entirely to the implicit safety reasoning of the planner. A fixed Constraint Space and a fixed applicability model, however, cannot cover every safety situation encountered at test time. The selected constraint set may contain irrelevant rules, omit safety-critical ones, or fail to represent a previously unseen hazard. We therefore introduce a multimodal Safety Inspector that examines the generated primitive plan and, when necessary, jointly produces a corrected plan proposal and reconstructs the complete set of constraints applicable to the current task and environment. The reconstructed constraints are returned to the planner for corrective re-planning. After validation by a post-reviewer, Inspector-generated feedback is further used for test-time adaptation of both the constraint-applicability model and the Safety Inspector, with the latter adapted through LoRA. Newly identified constraints are also incorporated into the Constraint Space. In this way, the framework can continuously improve constraint selection, safety inspection, and the explicit safety knowledge available for future planning. Our contributions are threefold: • Query-Conditioned Constraint Applicability Learning. We formulate safety-constraint selection as task- and environment-conditioned applicability learning and introduce a lightweight dual-tower model in which each contextual query induces a separating boundary over learned constraint representations. • Multimodal Safety Inspection with Constraint Reconstruction. We introduce a multimodal Safety Inspector that goes beyond binary safety verification by jointly producing a corrected primitive-plan proposal and reconstructing the complete set of constraints applicable to the current task and environment. • Inspector-Guided Test-Time Adaptation and Knowledge Expansion. We use validated Inspector feedback to adapt both the lightweight constraint-applicability model and the Safety Inspector through parameter-efficient LoRA updates, while newly identified constraints are incorporated into the Constraint Space for explicit safety-knowledge expansion.

## III Methodology
III-A Problem Formulation and System Overview Given a natural-language task T_{t} and an environment image I_{t} , our goal is to generate a primitive action sequence P_{t} that accomplishes the task while satisfying the safety requirements induced by the current task–environment context. We maintain an external Constraint Space C_{t} containing reusable textual safety knowledge, from which an applicable constraint set S_{t}\subseteq C_{t} is selected for the current input. A multimodal behavior planner then generates P_{t}=\mathcal{P}(T_{t},I_{t},S_{t}). Before execution, the generated plan is evaluated by a multimodal Safety Inspector. A plan is executed only after receiving a PASS decision. Otherwise, the Inspector jointly reconstructs the complete applicable constraint set \hat{G}_{t} and produces a corrected primitive-plan proposal \hat{P}_{t} . The reconstructed constraint set, rather than the Inspector-proposed plan, is returned to the planner for corrective re-planning: P_{t}^{\prime}=\mathcal{P}(T_{t},I_{t},\hat{G}_{t}). The resulting plan is inspected again, and the planning–inspection loop continues until a PASS decision is obtained. After three failed inspection attempts, the system invokes call_human_help() as a safety fallback. Validated Inspector feedback is further reused during deployment for test-time adaptation. Specifically, it provides supervision for updating the lightweight constraint-applicability model, adapting the Safety Inspector through LoRA, and expanding the explicit Constraint Space with newly identified safety rules. III-B Query-Conditioned Constraint Applicability Learning Given the Constraint Space C_{t} introduced above, let c_{i}\in C_{t} denote the i -th textual safety constraint. The first stage of our framework determines which stored constraints should apply to the current task and environment. A constraint can be semantically related to a task while still being unnecessary under the current scene configuration. We therefore formulate constraint selection as a task–environment-conditioned applicability learning problem rather than relying solely on semantic similarity. Given a natural-language task T_{t} and a 2D environment image I_{t} , a vision-language model first converts the visual observation into a textual environment description D_{t}=\mathcal{V}(I_{t}). The task and environment description are then concatenated to form a contextual query Q_{t}=T_{t}\oplus D_{t}. The encoder remains frozen during both offline training and test-time adaptation. Rather than directly comparing q_{t} and r_{i} using semantic similarity, we introduce a lightweight dual-tower constraint applicability model.
