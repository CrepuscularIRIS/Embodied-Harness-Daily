# LHNav: Long-Horizon Humanoid Navigation via Multirate Coordination and Physics-Grounded Action Data

## Abstract
We study humanoid navigation across multiple destinations under long-horizon language instructions. This task is challenging because robots must maintain progress across successive goals while continuously producing natural, stable whole-body motion through confined spaces. However, task decomposition in existing methods does not by itself ensure progress throughout execution, while waypoint-based navigation does not guarantee natural, route-faithful whole-body motion. Our key idea is to organize navigation into three complementary roles operating at different time scales: a Brain for task reasoning, a Cerebellum for local motion generation, and a Brainstem for whole-body execution. We realize this idea in LHNav, an asynchronous system that uses progress and arrival feedback to coordinate navigation goals, VLA motion-token prediction, and whole-body control. To train the Cerebellum for natural, route-faithful motion, we develop a physics-grounded data engine that improves physical tracking of motion references along collision-aware routes and pairs the resulting demonstrations with rendered visual observations. Together, these designs allow task planning to proceed alongside continuous movement and extend single-goal navigation to successive destinations with natural, route-faithful whole-body motion. In simulation, our navigation VLA outperforms OmniNav and DualVLN in single-goal success and motion smoothness, with fewer collisions and falls. Using a VLA trained only on single-goal demonstrations, LHNav achieves a 75.56% success rate on long-horizon navigation tasks. Deployment on the Unitree G1 further demonstrates single-goal navigation and zero-shot long-horizon task execution in real-world environments, including layouts with repositioned objects. Project website: https://robbyant-research.github.io/LHNav/.

## I Introduction
Humanoid robots assisting people in everyday environments need to follow long-horizon language instructions that involve multiple destinations and task dependencies [ 1 , 2 ] . For example, preparing a meal may require visiting the refrigerator, chopping board, stove, and dining table in an appropriate order. In this work, we focus on the navigation required by such instructions, where a humanoid must infer navigation goals and maintain progress across destinations without step-by-step guidance. This setting requires both task-level coordination across successive goals and natural, stable whole-body motion along long routes through confined spaces. Long-horizon humanoid navigation is challenging because task progress must remain consistent with continuous execution across multiple destinations. Existing methods couple navigation decisions to reasoning [ 3 , 4 , 5 ] , while asynchronous and hierarchical approaches allow task planning and action to proceed at different time scales [ 6 , 7 , 8 , 2 ] . However, separating these processes does not itself ensure coherent task progression: goal transitions must reflect actual execution progress while navigation continues. A second challenge is reconciling route constraints with natural, stable whole-body motion, particularly in confined spaces. Waypoints, trajectories, and velocity commands describe intended movement but leave its whole-body realization to downstream controllers [ 9 , 7 , 2 ] , so a collision-free route does not directly guarantee collision-free physical execution. Recent methods introduce richer body-level actions and multi-stage behaviors [ 10 , 11 , 12 , 13 ] , yet maintaining natural motion while limiting position and heading errors over long, confined routes remains challenging. We present LHNav , combining asynchronous navigation with a physics-grounded data engine for long-horizon humanoid navigation. Inspired by the human brain, it comprises three layers: a Brain that maintains task context and dispatches goals, a Cerebellum that predicts motion tokens with auxiliary waypoint-based geometric supervision, and a Brainstem that executes whole-body motion through a frozen SONIC decoder [ 14 ] . Progress and arrival feedback coordinate successive goals, allowing a single-goal policy to execute navigation while task planning proceeds asynchronously. Our data engine uses ARDY [ 15 ] to generate natural motion references along collision-aware routes and trains the SONIC encoder with additional reference-root and velocity information for accurate tracking through the frozen decoder. Rendering closed-loop rollouts in reconfigurable Gaussian–mesh scenes [ 16 ] pairs visual observations with synchronized motion-token targets for VLA training. Together, asynchronous coordination and physically executed supervision support natural, route-faithful whole-body navigation across multiple goals. Joint training on these simulated navigation demonstrations and real teleoperated manipulation data further enables navigation followed by tabletop pushing on Unitree G1. Our contributions are threefold: 1. An asynchronous multirate system integrating task reasoning, motion-token prediction, and whole-body execution, using progress-aware transitions that extend a single-goal policy to long-horizon humanoid navigation. 2. A scalable, physics-grounded data engine that improves global motion-reference tracking through a privileged encoder and frozen SONIC decoder, generating execution-aligned navigation demonstrations in Gaussian–mesh scenes. 3. Experimental validation demonstrating higher navigation success in unseen simulation scenes, smoother motion, fewer collisions, and faster long-horizon completion, alongside real-world single-goal navigation and zero-shot long-horizon navigation.

## III Method
We consider a G1 humanoid robot operating in a scene with a catalog of reference images depicting candidate destinations. Given a long-horizon language instruction, LHNav selects the required destinations from this catalog and navigates the robot to them in sequence. As shown in Fig. 3 , the Brain decomposes the instruction into navigation subtasks ( Section III-A ), the Cerebellum predicts local motion tokens, and the Brainstem decodes them into whole-body actions ( Section III-B ). All three layers execute asynchronously. III-A Brain: Agent with Tools The Brain coordinates long-horizon navigation through a planning agent with task-management and arrival-verification tools, tracking progress, revising the remaining plan, and deciding when to advance. The planning agent receives the full instruction and target catalog, together with task-decomposition and tool-use guidance and recent observations, decisions, and execution feedback. It can be implemented with GPT-6 or Qwen3.8-27B. The task management tool (Tool 1 in Fig. 3 ) maintains an explicit plan of completed, active, and pending navigation subtasks. The agent uses this tool to create the plan and revise pending subtasks during execution using new observations, task updates, and execution feedback. Revisions can remove goals that are no longer needed, while preserving completed visits and the active goal. The arrival verification tool provides goal-completion feedback through an independent Qwen VLM, which checks arrival at up to 1 Hz using the active subtask, target reference image, and temporal observations. Diagnostic analyses and subtask-specific arrival judgments are written to a shared buffer for the planning agent to access. At a navigation stop, the runtime harness blocks new VLA requests and discards outstanding predictions. It records completion after verified arrival, motion-plan exhaustion, and odometry-confirmed root stability, then dispatches the next subtask from the updated plan following the external stage-completion event.
