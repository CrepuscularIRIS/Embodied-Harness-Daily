# Reins: Frontier Multimodal Models Driving Real Robot Arms on Long-Horizon Tasks, Zero-Shot

## Abstract
Frontier models such as GPT-6.1 Sol, GPT-6 Astra and Claude Opus 5.5 can now plan and call tools, yet they still struggle to drive real robots through long-horizon, contact-rich manipulation, blind while the arm moves and while they think. We present Reins, a harness in which an off-the-shelf model operates a dual-arm robot with 3D perception tools and atomic actions that report contact and grip. The model chains actions in one call, each with a condition that must hold, stopping at the first that fails; a second instance, the narrator, watches the cameras continuously and reports what changed with every result. From images, Reins also builds a simulation-ready twin of the scene for continual policy learning. With natural-language prompts and no task-specific code, GPT-6.1 Sol succeeds in 88% of real episodes on five tasks; without the harness, it fails every shell game, taking 8 times as long. Three frontier models perform alike but differ in tracking a scene that changes while the actor thinks. Code will be released upon publication.

## 2 Method
Actor loop. At decision k , the actor, an off-the-shelf model \pi given a task prompt g , outputs a tool call or chain of calls a_{k}\sim\pi(\cdot\mid g,h_{k}) ; its result y_{k}=(r_{k},I_{k},q_{k},E_{k}) (measurements, new images, both arms’ state and the narrator’s entries since y_{k-1} ) joins the history h_{k+1} (App. A ). An episode of K decisions lasts T=\sum_{k}(\lambda_{k}+d_{k}) , model latency plus execution. A chain of thought takes time: latency is 41–78% of T in our runs, so each decision should cover several checked steps (1.6–3.4 on average), and the scene may change while the actor thinks. Perception and atomic actions. Of the eighteen tools (App. B ), find (SAM 3 [ 5 ] on RGB-D) and measure turn an object name or image point into a 3D point in the base frame, to about 1.6 mm with the arm’s own wrist camera. Atomic actions move an arm, tilt its tool or work its gripper; move_until_contact stops when joint current indicates contact, and close_gripper reports whether the fingers hold anything. Guarded chains. do executes a chain P=\big((p_{i},\rho_{i},u_{i},n_{i})\big)_{i=1}^{n} of up to eight calls and sixteen runs. Step i runs tool p_{i} up to n_{i} times until u_{i} holds, and each result r_{i,m} must pass \rho_{i} . The chain stops after its last step or at the first run that fails its check or uses up its repeats: (i,m)^{\star}=\min\big\{(i,m)\;:\;\neg\rho_{i}(r_{i,m})\;\lor\;\big(m=n_{i}\land\neg u_{i}(r_{i,m})\big)\big\}. (1) Narrator. A second instance \nu of the model watches the head camera at 1 frame/s in one ongoing conversation. Call j receives the frames in W_{j}=(\tau_{j-1},\tau_{j}] (at most 20, plus wrist frames while an arm moves) and, after latency \ell_{j} , returns an entry e_{j}=\nu(W_{j},e_{<j}) that reaches the actor as what_happened . The next window closes when the call returns, \tau_{j+1}=\tau_{j}+\ell_{j} , so |W_{j+1}|=\ell_{j},\qquad\ell_{j}\;\leq\;t-\tau_{j}\;<\;\ell_{j}+\ell_{j+1}\quad\mbox{for }t\in[\tau_{j+1},\tau_{j+2}){:} (2) each window lasts as long as the previous call, and the newest frame reported is 1–2 latencies old.

## 3 Experiments and Findings
Setup and tasks. Two 6-DoF AirBot Play arms with stock parallel grippers share a table, with RGB-D cameras on the head and each wrist; episodes start at the zero pose and the operator judges success. We run five episodes per condition with GPT-6.1 Sol (Ultrafast tier), GPT-6 Astra and Claude Opus 5.5, at high reasoning effort. In the shell game , the first of three mugs lifted after a 20–30 s shuffle must cover a hidden red object; the drawer is opened by its 3–4 mm lip, filled with a block and closed; a towel is folded twice, a shirt sleeves-in and then twice; in sweep , one arm holds a dustpan and the other brushes objects in. The drawer and sweep prompts add grip and pose hints. The narrator and the harness. All three models open the drawer every time; the shell game, whose scene changes while the actor waits, separates them. Without the narrator, Sol wins only 2 of 5. By Eq. ( 2 ), a faster narrator sees more: Sol’s (Ultrafast tier) answers in 1–6 s and sees a shuffle in 8–11 windows; Astra’s and Opus’s (standard tier) take 3–40 s and see it in 2–4 (Fig. 2 b). Astra’s narrator still tracks the red object in 4 of 5 episodes, Opus’s in none; Opus’s two wins were guesses. Without the harness (raw images, straight-down moves, wait , look ), Sol never lifts the correct mug: in four episodes it closes the gripper 6–9 times in 60 calls but lifts no mug, and once it lifts the wrong one.

## 4 Limitations and Future Work
Limitations. Task knowledge enters only through natural-language prompts; contact is sensed only from joint current and gripper width; we test one platform, five episodes per condition; and a slow narrator reports fast changes late (Eq. ( 2 )). Future work. Every episode is fully logged (100 Hz state, images, text); we will turn successful episodes into reusable chains and checks, rehearsed in simulation twins (App. C ), so that the same model finishes tasks faster, from shorter prompts.
