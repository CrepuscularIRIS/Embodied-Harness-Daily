# EviSeek: An Evidence-Centric Agentic Framework for Embodied Active Information Seeking

## Abstract
Active embodied question answering requires agents to acquire evidence through viewpoint changes and physical interaction. The central challenge is to avoid undirected interaction: taking plausible actions without specifying what evidence they should reveal for answering the question. Action plans and accumulated histories alone can leave the informational purpose of an interaction implicit. We introduce EviSeek, a training-free framework that makes question-derived evidence demands explicit acquisition objectives, linking observation-conditioned actions and selectively retained evidence to the information the agent seeks. On 3,436 ESI-Bench questions, EviSeek improves accuracy over the Single-model agent by 7.1 percentage points with Qwen3-VL-32B and 3.7 points with Gemini 3.1 Pro, while improving visual-support-weighted accuracy and reducing mean token usage. These findings support making the informational purpose of interaction explicit, with action selection and evidence retention organized around what the agent needs to learn to answer the question.

## 1 Introduction
Active embodied question answering (A-EQA) requires agents to acquire information through interaction with a partially observed environment ( Das et al., 2018a ; Gordon et al., 2017 ; Majumdar et al., 2024 ) . Relevant evidence may be occluded, distributed across locations, or revealed only by viewpoint changes and physical intervention ( Bajcsy, 1988 ; Sawyer et al., 2024 ) . Agents must decide what to observe, how to acquire those observations, and what to retain for answering. ESI-Bench highlights action blindness : poor action choices yield inadequate observations and subsequent reasoning errors ( Hong et al., 2026 ) . We focus on three related difficulties: questions may leave the required observations unspecified; acquiring them may require several stages of interaction; and limited visual history may discard earlier evidence before answering. For example, assessing a stacking configuration requires observing its state after a physical test and retaining that outcome. A plausible manipulation sequence alone does not establish that the relevant evidence was acquired or preserved. Existing agents guide interaction through histories, exploration, retrieval, and confidence ( Yao et al., 2022 ; Huang et al., 2022 ; Ren et al., 2024 ; Cheng et al., 2025 ) . ScoutVLA seeks missing visual evidence ( Lu et al., 2026 ) , MemoryEQA shares memory across planning, stopping, and answering ( Zhai et al., 2025 ) , and R4 decomposes questions for structured retrieval ( Sohn et al., 2026 ) . Building on these directions, we ask whether explicit evidence demands can serve as a shared reference for acquisition and retention, improving active question answering with a fixed pretrained vision-language model (VLM). We introduce EviSeek , a training-free framework organized around this representation. Evidence-Demand Planning derives observational targets from the question and initial view without assuming a candidate answer is correct, then assigns acquisition subtasks to them. Evidence-Seeking Interaction pursues these goals through viewpoint changes or physical intervention, conditioning actions on observations and execution feedback. Evidence-Oriented Memory selectively retains views and action outcomes on an evidence board, linked to their originating subtask and demand. Subtasks organize acquisition, while the board separates retained answer evidence from transient control observations. Spatial reasoning and evidence interpretation remain dependent on the pretrained VLM. On 3,436 ESI-Bench questions ( Hong et al., 2026 ) , EviSeek improves overall accuracy over the official Single-model active agent by 7.1 percentage points with Qwen3-VL-32B and 3.7 with Gemini 3.1 Pro, under the same task interface and environment-action ceiling. Both backbones also improve visual-support-weighted accuracy while using fewer model calls and tokens on average. Qwen ablations show that removing demands while retaining task decomposition lowers online accuracy. On fixed trajectories, replacing the board with recent-view memory or replacing selective retention with uniform sampling under the same frame-capacity limit also lowers accuracy. These comparisons support explicit demands and selective retention within the evaluated framework. A navigation variant improves grounded correctness over the RGB Single-model baseline on EXPRESS-Bench ( Jiang et al., 2025 ) . Benefits remain task-dependent: Metric Comparison regresses with both ESI-Bench backbones, delimiting the gains from evidence organization. Our main contributions are as follows: • We introduce a demand-indexed representation linking question-derived observational targets, acquisition subtasks, and retained evidence. • We develop EviSeek, a training-free framework that uses this representation to organize viewpoint acquisition, physical intervention, and selective evidence retention. • We report gains in overall accuracy and visual-support-weighted accuracy with lower mean model usage on two ESI-Bench backbones, supported by demand and memory ablations, and evaluate a navigation variant on EXPRESS-Bench.

## 4 Method
4.1 Overview EviSeek formulates A-EQA as an evidence acquisition problem. EviSeek represents the observations required by a question as evidence demands, then uses these demands to guide acquisition and retention. The key idea is to maintain an evidence state that decouples what information is required , how evidence is acquired , and which observations are retained . At interaction step t , EviSeek instantiates the information state M_{t} of Section 3 as a demand-indexed representation of the interaction history H_{t} : M_{t}=\bigl(\mathcal{D},\mathcal{P}_{t},\mathcal{B}_{t}\bigr), where \mathcal{D} is a set of K question-conditioned evidence demands, \mathcal{P}_{t} records the execution status of the initial acquisition plan, and \mathcal{B}_{t} is the evidence board that stores retained observations and interaction outcomes with demand-level provenance. The plan’s goals and demand assignments remain fixed within an episode; its active subtask and completion statuses change as execution proceeds. EviSeek updates M_{t} through the three stages detailed in turn below. All components are implemented through prompting: throughout this paper, functions F_{\mathrm{dem}} , F_{\mathrm{plan}} , and the policies \pi_{\mathrm{seek}} , \pi_{\mathrm{keep}} , and \pi_{\mathrm{ans}} denote inference-time prediction procedures instantiated by a pretrained vision-language model , involving no task-specific training or parameter updates. EviSeek operates through the benchmark observation and action interfaces. In ESI-Bench, it uses RGB, task-exposed action feedback, and relative poses accumulated from camera actions (Appendix E ). 4.2 Evidence-Demand Planning An embodied question specifies the requested information but does not directly reveal the observations required to establish it. Given a question Q and the initial observation o_{0} , EviSeek first constructs a set of evidence demands: \mathcal{D}=F_{\mathrm{dem}}(Q,o_{0})=\{d_{1},\ldots,d_{K}\}. Each demand d_{i} specifies an observable acquisition target intended to support answering the question; each corresponds to one information dependency of Q and is constructed to satisfy three properties. First, it is question-conditioned : the demand targets information relevant to resolving Q . Second, it is observable : the demand can be supported by visual observations or by outcomes produced through legal interactions. Third, it is answer-agnostic : it specifies what should be observed without assuming that a candidate answer is correct. A physical-test goal can name a candidate configuration, such as a stacking order, and request an observation of its outcome. The demands define observational targets, while the interaction policy determines how to acquire them. Based on them, EviSeek constructs an initial acquisition plan: \displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{0} \displaystyle=F_{\mathrm{plan}}(Q,\mathcal{D},o_{0})=\langle u_{1},\ldots,u_{J}\rangle, \displaystyle u_{j} \displaystyle=(g_{j},\rho(j)), where g_{j} is the subtask’s observable acquisition goal and \rho(j)\in\{1,\ldots,K\} indexes the demand d_{\rho(j)} that u_{j} serves. A subtask describes what evidence should be obtained rather than how actions should be executed. Its realization is delegated to the interaction module, allowing the agent to adapt acquisition according to newly observed scene information. 4.3 Evidence-Seeking Interaction Given an active acquisition subtask u_{j} with goal g_{j} , EviSeek selects actions that are expected to produce informative evidence.
