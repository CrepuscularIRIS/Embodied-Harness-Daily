# MessyNav: Zero-Shot Navigation via Manipulation in Messy Environments

## Abstract
Real-world environments are messy, with paths often blocked by everyday objects like chairs and toys, requiring robots to perform interactive navigation by manipulating obstacles to clear a path. Existing methods rely on extensive simulation-based learning or per-scene instructions specifying which objects to avoid or manipulate. They struggle to generalize across novel objects, diverse environments, and varied contexts such as a cart of fragile glassware that should be avoided while an empty one can be pushed. We formalize this problem as open-world interactive navigation and present MessyNav, a zero-shot framework based on vision-language models (VLMs). At its core, MessyNav performs explicit VLM-based affordance reasoning before task planning to decide whether to swipe, pick up, or avoid an object and produce a high-level plan. Evaluated on a mobile manipulator across real-world indoor and outdoor environments, MessyNav improves success rate by 27 percentage points (pp) over the strongest baseline in real-robot trials and achieves statistically significant gains in a larger-scale offline evaluation, without task-specific training.

## 1 Introduction
Picture a mom retrieving her child’s backpack at daycare. To get to the closet, she needs to push aside a chair and maneuver around a fragile toy train set. It is natural for humans to navigate such scenarios by actively manipulating their environment to create paths. However, robots running classical navigation algorithms treat every obstacle, whether a light toy or an immovable wall, as an obstruction to avoid, often failing in cluttered environments where progress requires interaction. In messy settings especially, robots need interactive navigation: the ability to move through an environment by manipulating the objects that block their path. While classical planning is primarily a geometric problem, interactive navigation is a semantic decision problem under geometric constraints. When blocked, a robot must reason about how to move an object, and before that, whether it should be moved at all. This decision depends on fine-grained semantic and contextual cues about fragility, portability, and function that are difficult to infer from geometry alone and costly to capture through task-specific training. We refer to this problem as open-world interactive navigation , where success requires generalizing across unseen objects, environments, and contexts. Existing interactive navigation approaches fall short: prior methods [ 1 , 2 , 3 , 4 ] rely on simulation-based learning or predefined category-level affordances, limiting their ability to generalize to new objects and contexts. These limitations make it challenging to operate in messy, real-world environments requiring fine-grained semantic reasoning, such as recognizing that a robot can safely push a wheeled chair but must avoid an adjacent box labeled “fragile”. Conversely, classical navigation stacks [ 5 ] do not require training data but treat all obstacles as static geometric boundaries, planning only collision-free paths without reasoning about object interactability. Achieving open-world interactive navigation requires moving beyond both hand-crafted geometric heuristics and data-intensive simulation toward the zero-shot, commonsense reasoning provided by internet-scale VLMs. In this work, we present MessyNav , a framework for zero-shot interactive navigation in open-world, cluttered environments. Given an egocentric image and a navigation goal, MessyNav infers object-level affordance labels from appearance and context, then passes these labels to a VLM-based task planner that generates high-level actions which are executed through navigation and manipulation primitives (Figure 1 ). The main contributions of this work are: (1) Problem formulation: We provide the first formalization of open-world interactive navigation: given an egocentric observation and a navigation goal, the agent must decide whether interaction is necessary and, if so, how to interact with obstructing objects to reach the goal, while generalizing zero-shot across (i) object types, (ii) environments, and (iii) semantic contexts. (2) Framework: We present MessyNav , a zero-shot framework whose core design separates affordance reasoning (via an explicit VLM module) from task planning. This explicit prior lets the robot make better context-dependent decisions. Ablations show this separation raises interactive navigation success from 40% to 67% over end-to-end VLM planning. (3) Open-world affordance benchmark: We release a real-world benchmark for instance-level, context-dependent affordance reasoning, consisting of 100 cluttered scenes with ground-truth interactability labels, on which MessyNav achieves 85.7% affordance accuracy, 19.3 percentage points above the closed-set oracle baseline that relies on predefined category labels. Finally, we evaluate MessyNav across all three axes of open-world variation, including novel objects, varied contexts, and diverse environments (indoor and outdoor, day and night), without any system modifications. By formalizing this problem and providing a concrete system, MessyNav offers a foundation for in-the-wild interactive navigation.

## 3 Problem Formulation
Interactive navigation [ 25 , 3 ] addresses scenarios where a robot must actively manipulate movable obstacles to clear a path to its goal, rather than just avoiding them. To better reflect the complexity of real-world environments, we extend this setting to open-world interactive navigation, where the robot must reason about novel objects and their specific context. We define three axes of variation along which an open-world interactive navigation system should generalize zero-shot. (1) Novel objects: Real environments contain objects that cannot be accounted for in advance, so a robot must infer whether and how to interact with objects it has never encountered. For example, it might find a rolled yoga mat in a hallway or cables on the floor attached to a valuable instrument. (2) Diverse environments: A deployed robot must operate across varied environments without any scene-specific tuning. The system must generalize across different rooms (kitchens, hallways, living areas), locations (indoor, outdoor), and lighting conditions (daytime, nighttime). (3) Varied contexts: Affordance is not just a property of an object, but of an object given its surroundings. The same object may be interactive or not depending on its context: a packaged extension cord is movable, but one plugged into a socket should be avoided. Individual toy blocks may be pushable, but when assembled as a child’s creation, they are fragile, valuable possessions. MessyNav is designed to meet all three requirements without task-specific training or prompting.

## 4 Approach
We present MessyNav , an interactive navigation framework that leverages VLMs for zero-shot affordance reasoning and task planning to go beyond closed-set prediction or environment-specific tuning. Our central design decision is to separate what to interact with from how to interact : an explicit affordance reasoning stage first decides which objects can be moved, and only then does a task planner decide how to move them. We make affordance reasoning an explicit stage rather than letting a task planner implicitly handle affordance reasoning, allowing it to make more grounded decisions in context-dependent scenarios. In Section 5 , we ablate this core design by comparing MessyNav to end-to-end VLM planning. As shown in Figure 2 , the framework consists of three components: (1) Affordance Reasoner : Identifies which objects are interactive based on robot feasibility and object properties. (2) Task Planner : Generates a sequence of navigation and manipulation actions based on the affordance labels to clear the blocking objects and reach the goal. (3) Open-Language Skill Library : Grounds open-language object references in task plans to 3D world coordinates and executes motion primitives.
