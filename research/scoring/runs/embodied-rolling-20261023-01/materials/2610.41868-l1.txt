# META-Robot: Self-Cognition and Capability Scaling in Agentic Robots

## Abstract
LLM-based robotic agent systems can generate programs for robot control, yet their generalization to new tasks often depends on elaborate scaffolding. Expanding their skill repertoires alone offers limited guidance on how to assess new capabilities or identify what remains beyond their reach. We introduce META-Robot, a framework that couples self cognition with capability scaling by expanding the criteria through which an agent evaluates and develops its skills. At its core, action-level predicates make execution outcomes explicit and available for LLM reasoning. The agent uses these assessments to identify its own limitations, extend relevant evaluation criteria, and guide the structured expansion of its skills. Proprioceptive feedback further grounds this process in the robot's physical state, enriching the evidence available for self-cognition. Experiments in simulation and on real robots demonstrate stronger overall task performance, generalization to new tasks, continued expansion of skills and evaluation criteria, and effective use of non-visual proprioceptive signals.

## 1 Introduction
Robotic agents have emerged as a promising paradigm for general-purpose robotic manipulation recently with generated code as control policy Liang et al. (2023) ; Xiao et al. (2026) . While these systems Kumar (2026) can adapt to new tasks without training to some extent, their scalability remains limited. When tasks require evaluation criteria, feedback modalities, or skills beyond predefined scaffolds, agents cannot revise these system-level limitations, leading to repeated strategy switching and compounding ineffective modifications that make meta-level task generalization or reliable recovery nearly impossible. Predicate scaling offers a natural solution by allowing agents to retain and reuse predicates acquired from previous tasks. This accumulation can improve transfer and reduce the need for additional predefined scaffolding. However, scaling predicates alone cannot overcome fixed system scaffolding, as systems cannot have explicit cognition of task and its own capability limits. Recent efforts to improve scalability follow a primary strategy: replacing accumulation of fixed predicates with validated, agent-discovered skills distilled from execution processes Zhang et al. (2026) ; Lu et al. (2026) . This reduces manual engineering and allows past repairs to guide future tasks. However, these skills are accumulated without structured links to their expected effects and required evidence. Consequently, the skill library may continue to grow while the system’s understanding of the task and its own capabilities remains unchanged, limiting substantive capability scaling. The emergence of Neuro-Symbolic Predicates (NSPs) Liang et al. (2025) ; Athalye et al. (2026) presents a compelling foundation for scaling robot agent system cognition beyond fixed mechanisms. By combining neural perceptual queries with programmatic logic, NSPs can construct abstractions for evaluation, offering a path toward adapting to new evaluation criteria. Nevertheless, extending predicate invention to self cognition and capability scaling in robotic agent systems remains challenging. Predicates must assess not only whether task goals are satisfied, but also whether the current system has sufficient observations, evaluation criteria, and executable skills. We argue that grounding predicates with physical evidence at the action level is crucial, as it allows the agent to exploit available proprioceptive signals when vision is insufficient, construct corresponding skills and evaluation criteria, and integrate them into control and verification, effectively coupling cognitive and capability scaling with evidence-grounded assessment. To this end, we propose META-Robot, as illustrated in Fig. 1 , a system for self cognition and capability scaling that grounds robotic meta-generalization in evidence-based, action-level predicates for both system self-assessment and task evaluation. By recording predicate transitions around skill executions, coupling validated predicates with reusable skills, and orchestrating proprioceptive feedback, META-Robot creates a scalable system for jointly scaling robotic cognition and capabilities. META-Robot incorporates several key innovations. First, to mitigate the ambiguity between coarse execution logs and actual task-state changes, we introduce a closed-loop execution agent that evaluates each action execution through action-level predicate transitions. The agent records observations, inputs, outputs, and pre- and post-execution predicate states, associating program behavior with changes in robot action. When an error is detected, it performs targeted local repair and re-execution, followed by full-task validation of the revised program. Second, fixed evaluation criteria make failures beyond the coverage of the existing system difficult to diagnose and can lead to unstructured skill accumulation. To address this, META-Robot maintains coupled predicate and skill libraries that expand through validated execution experience. Although this design supports continual knowledge scaling, it also requires precise alignment between the two libraries. META-Robot establishes explicit predicate–skill associations to support grounded skill retrieval, composition, and expansion. We conduct extensive experiments in both simulation environments [23] and real-world environments to validate the effectiveness of META-Robot. (i) META-Robot achieves stronger overall performance than prior methods, reaching a success rate of 95.8% on the LIBERO-Pro Spatial-Task, compared with 60.0% for other baselines. Crucially, we observe (ii) emergent cognition and capabilities including the scaling of reusable skills and their corresponding evaluation criteria from validated execution experience. We further demonstrate (iii) robust generalization through zero-shot transfer to held-out long-horizon tasks without additional debugging and successful deployment across diverse real-robot tasks. ## 3 Method
We introduce META-Robot, a novel framework for meta-level generalization to new tasks. The META-Robot framework is grounded in the scaling of cognition and capability, where evaluation criteria, feedback acquisition, available signals, and executable skills are jointly expanded, thereby reducing dependence on predefined scaffolding. META-Robot operates directly on action-level predicates throughout the process, instead of coarse execution outcomes, which better connects program behavior with changes in task conditions. Specifically, the closed-loop execution agent records observations, inputs, outputs, and pre- and post-execution predicate states for every skill invocation and subtask end. The predicate-state trace enables the system to consistently assess progress, attribute failures, and verify targeted repairs through local re-execution before full-task validation. We also introduce coupled action-level predicate and Skill Libraries and a proprioceptive signal orchestration mechanism to overcome the limitations of unstructured skill accumulation and vision-only verification. With these components, META-Robot can enrich its task evidence and cognition, consolidate validated repairs as reusable skills, and acquire additional physical evidence when existing observations are insufficient, progressively expanding both system cognition and capability. For each subtask, the agent generates action-level predicates from the task description and the LLM’s own reasoning, establishing explicit criteria for execution assessment. These predicates are evaluated after each skill invocation and at subtask completion, providing fine-grained evidence of whether the intended action conditions have been satisfied.
