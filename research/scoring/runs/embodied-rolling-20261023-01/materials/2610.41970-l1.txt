# When Does Test-Time Search Help World Action Models? An Empirical Audit of Candidate Selection and Simulator Integrity

## Abstract
World-action models increasingly apply test-time scaling, spending extra inference computation on candidate selection and adaptive allocation to improve control. Such gains are credible only when candidates carry exploitable signal, nominal compute savings become real speedup, and evaluation stays reliable. We audit all three on Cosmos Policy and RoboCasa with a premise-first, pre-registered methodology. On clean data, a learned value selector is 1.66 pp worse than random; a hindsight oracle gains only 1.40 pp, with an episode-level interval spanning zero; and 82% of states show identical candidate outcomes, leaving little selection headroom. Token pruning cuts per-forward FLOPs by up to 47% but yields at most a measured 1.15$\times$ end-to-end speedup on the deployed path (1.25$\times$ on a decode-excluded path), short of a 1.5$\times$ target, and our adaptive-allocation experiment is inconclusive under a power gate. We further identify and fix a silent rendering corruption whose rate rises sharply along compute-heavy planning paths while leaving the official direct-policy result unaffected, and release a detector, fix, and scripts. These findings show why test-time scaling should be audited as a chain of premises (decision signal, realized efficiency, evaluationintegrity) rather than by success rate alone. Conclusions are scoped to the tested configuration.

## 1 Introduction
World-action models (WAMs) such as Cosmos Policy [ 14 ] adapt large pretrained video models into robot policies that jointly generate actions, future states, and values within a shared latent diffusion process. A central promise of this design is test-time scaling : by generating candidate actions, predicting their future consequences, and evaluating them with a value function, WAMs can allocate additional inference-time computation to identify promising actions. Related peer-reviewed work has explored complementary aspects of this problem, including model-based planning with action-conditioned video world models [ 27 ] and efficient diffusion-based robot policies through one-step distillation [ 22 ] , training-free adaptive caching [ 11 ] , and rollout-adaptive pruning and activation reuse [ 12 ] . Yet whether extra inference-time computation improves control remains insufficiently examined. More computation helps only if it exposes decision-relevant candidate differences, produces practical end-to-end effects, and is evaluated reliably; success rates alone cannot distinguish a weak selection signal, unrealized compute savings, and simulator artifacts [ 17 ] . We therefore audit test-time scaling as a chain of premises rather than a single performance number: is useful signal present, do nominal savings translate into end-to-end gains, and does the evaluation path remain reliable under added computation? We instantiate this audit on Cosmos Policy and RoboCasa [ 20 ] . Rather than proposing a planner, we combine paired candidate comparisons and a deployment-conditioned hindsight oracle, pre-registered decision criteria and a power gate, complete-query latency measurements, and a framebuffer-sentinel integrity check. The audit yields three findings. (1) Candidate selection is limited by the available signal, not just by the selector. On render-verified clean data, the deployable value selector performs 1.66 pp worse than random, and even a hindsight oracle (an upper bound) gains only 1.40 pp with an interval including zero, because in 82\% of query states all candidates share an outcome. This does not show selection is generally ineffective; it shows selector quality must be read alongside the separability of the candidates it receives. (2) Nominal compute reduction is not a reliable proxy for practical acceleration. Directly timing the complete query, token pruning yields at most a measured 1.15\times end-to-end speedup on the deployed path ( 1.25\times with future-image decode excluded), below the pre-specified 1.5\times target, because the gain is bounded by the irreducible autoencoder encode and decode. A separate joint-allocation diagnostic falls below the measurement’s noise resolution and did not clear its power gate, so it is inconclusive rather than evidence of no effect. (3) Evaluation integrity can depend on the inference path being evaluated. We identify and fix a silent rendering corruption in the RoboCasa/ robosuite stack whose frequency is small under the official direct-policy protocol but rises sharply in the compute-intensive planning path. It is more than a generic simulator bug: changing inference-time computation changes exposure to a measurement failure, so the compute-intensive paths that realize test-time scaling are the most exposed. Together, these findings make the contribution a testable audit methodology and a configuration-specific case study, not a claim that test-time scaling fails for all WAMs.

## 3 A Diagnostic Methodology for Test-Time Compute
Because extra inference compute can change outputs without improving control, success-rate movements alone are not evidence of scaling gains. Our pre-registered, falsification-first audit tests for usable candidate signal and reliable evaluation before interpreting success (Section 6 ). 3.1 Verify the Premise Before Measuring the Gain Before evaluating an inference-time mechanism, we test whether its required signal exists. For best-of- N planning, candidate outcomes must differ in ways a selector can recover; we test this directly in Section 4 , rather than infer it from downstream success. Supplement Figure 5 summarizes the audit gates. 3.2 Causal Ablation and an Oracle Headroom Estimate For each query state, we replay all N candidates under a fixed continuation policy and let a hindsight oracle select a successful one. This estimates outcome-level selection headroom in the current candidate pool; because it uses unavailable outcome information, it is not deployable. We also probe input importance by leave-one-out patch masking, trusting the probe only if irrelevant background ranks below relevant end-effector and contact regions in a pre-registered sanity check. 3.3 Pre-Registration and Statistical Power Control For the selection audit, we fix decision thresholds and the replicate-consistency gate before inspecting the corresponding data and report the gate’s verdict even when it fails. A power gate avoids launching experiments unlikely to yield a verdict: from a smoke sample, we estimate the paired-residual standard deviation \hat{\sigma}_{r} and project the two-sided confidence-interval half-width at the planned sample size, h(N)=z_{0.975}\,\hat{\sigma}_{r}/\sqrt{N}, (1) launching the full run only if h(N_{\text{full}})<\Delta_{\text{prereg}} , the separation between the pre-registered outcomes; otherwise the run is not launched. Every reported comparison states its bootstrap resampling unit and interval, so that an effect whose significance depends on that choice is presented as such rather than as a settled result. Closed-loop success is measured by snapshot replay, in which we restore a state, execute a candidate action chunk, and continue under a fixed dense base policy, rather than inferred from a single-step action deviation. 3.4 Scope Our empirical findings are limited to Cosmos Policy on RoboCasa. The audit components—premise checks, oracle headroom, a power gate, and render-integrity verification—can also inform audits of other diffusion control policies. We release a framebuffer-sentinel detector, a fix for the identified OpenGL-context bug, and reproduction scripts; the detector principle may transfer across backends, but this fix is stack-specific.

## 4 Auditing Best-of- N Planning Selection
We apply the audit to best-of- N planning, comparing the model’s value-based ordering with random selection and estimating outcome-level headroom using a hindsight oracle. 4.1 Setup On render-verified clean data, we compare the value selector, random selection, and the deployment-conditioned hindsight oracle on paired sets of N{=}8 candidates with common continuation seeds. Differences from random are reported with state-within-task and episode-within-task bootstrap intervals. Continuation protocol. In snapshot replay, each candidate’s first 16 steps of a generated 32 -step chunk are executed, stopping on success; the episode then replans every 16 steps under a fixed base policy until success or the 500 -step task limit. The base policy uses one sample, all tokens, 10 denoising steps, and no value head; all selectors share the same outcome matrix and continuation seeds ( R=2 ).
