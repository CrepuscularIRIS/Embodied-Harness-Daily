===ABSTRACT===
To advance the development of embodied general navigation, zero-shot open-vocabulary multi-object navigation has attracted increasing attention, aiming to enable robots to efficiently search multiple objects in unseen environments without training. For this task, the key lies in improving navigation policies from earlier searches to locate subsequent goals more efficiently. Recent methods automate this process through trajectory reflection and rule revision, enabling policy self-improvement. However, the mechanisms for diagnosing problems and generating revisions are typically designed by humans and remain fixed across iterations. Consequently, the system's capacity for continued self-improvement remains constrained, even as its navigation policy evolves. To this end, we propose RPSI-Nav, a Recursive Policy Self-Improvement framework for zero-shot multi-object navigation that revises both the navigation program and its revision mechanism. RPSI-Nav diagnoses search behavior, requests diagnostic views when existing observations cannot distinguish competing causes, and generates targeted navigation revisions. Feedback from subsequent goal searches is linked to the rules that produced these revisions, guiding changes to diagnosis, observation acquisition, and revision planning. The updated improver governs subsequent navigation improvements, forming a recursive loop in which RPSI-Nav revises its own improvement process. Across goals, the robot conditionally inherits accepted program updates and spatial memory, enabling search experience to improve both future navigation and adaptation. Benchmark experiments and qualitative real-robot evaluations validate RPSI-Nav's effectiveness.

===INTRO===
Zero-Shot Object Navigation (ZSON) is a challenging task in Embodied AI, requiring an agent to navigate to target objects in unfamiliar environments without training. Unlike conventional object navigation methods that focus on a single target, we consider zero-shot open-vocabulary multi-object navigation, where a robot must search for and locate multiple language-specified targets in succession within the same environment [ 1 , 19 ] . In this setting, agents can use experience from earlier searches to refine their navigation strategies and locate subsequent goals more efficiently. When agents autonomously reflect on past searches and revise their decision rules, this process constitutes policy self-improvement. Related work [ 2 , 28 , 15 ] explores such self-improvement across navigation settings. However, their updates primarily concern navigation rules or decision guidance, while the procedures that generate these updates remain predefined. In multi-object navigation, subsequent searches can reveal whether earlier revisions resolved their intended problems. However, the observations needed to explain an ineffective revision may not yet be available, and acquiring them consumes the same movement budget as navigation. Later searches can therefore expose omissions in the diagnostic checks or observation requests that guided earlier repairs. If this feedback only triggers further policy updates, the rules producing these omissions may remain unchanged, even as the navigation policy evolves. To this end, we propose RPSI-Nav , a R ecursive P olicy S elf- I mprovement framework for zero-shot open-vocabulary multi-object navigation. Its improvement program diagnoses search problems, gathers observations that distinguish competing explanations, and generates targeted revisions to the navigation program. For each revision, the system records its intended behavioral change and actual execution outcome. Subsequent goal searches test these revisions and provide feedback on the diagnosis and repair decisions behind them. When this feedback exposes limitations in its improvement rules, the current improver applies those rules to propose revisions to itself. These changes can alter which causes are considered, which observations are requested, and which navigation functions are revised. The accepted version then governs the next round of navigation revisions, whose outcomes drive further self-revision, closing the recursive loop. Conditional inheritance carries accepted updates into later searches where applicable. We evaluate RPSI-Nav on public open-vocabulary multi-object navigation benchmarks, complemented by qualitative real-robot experiments.
