===ABSTRACT===
Coding agents can construct and revise robot behavior during execution, but using one agent for the whole robot can delay otherwise independent work. We introduce Asynchronous Coding Agents as Policy (ACAP), an asynchronous framework that organizes one robot as a team of persistent agents. Each member reasons and acts for a configurable control group, such as an arm with its gripper or the torso and chassis, enabling asynchronous reasoning and execution. However, different robot components often have physical dependencies that require coordination. To address this challenge, ACAP introduces a hyper-agent that assigns tasks, coordinates dependencies, and dynamically allocates control between the lead and member agents. This allows independent work to progress concurrently while supporting tightly coupled manipulation when necessary. Across 72 real-world rollouts covering 25 task/platform settings, ACAP reduces task-balanced time cost by 28.2% compared with a single-agent Direct Policy, with improvements across Fully Parallel, Parallel-Sequential, and Collaborative tasks. Across all eight Collaborative settings, ACAP also achieves lower mean time cost than Fixed Delegation, where only member agents can control their assigned groups. An additional 90-rollout study shows lower mean 30-minute-capped time cost than Direct on all six tasks. We further demonstrate mobile manipulation using the same member interface for chassis and torso control. We present the framework and a real-world rollout dataset containing observations, generated programs, actions, and feedback to support research on how coding agents reason and act in the physical world.

===INTRO===
Recent coding-agent policies can inspect a scene, construct numerical models, and write and revise robot-control programs during execution ( Jia et al., 2026 , Fu et al., 2026 ) . They extend the executable-policy interface of Code as Policies ( Liang et al., 2023 ) into sustained interaction with physical feedback. While learning from demonstrations produces increasingly capable visuomotor policies ( Chi et al., 2023 , Kim et al., 2024a , Black et al., 2024 ) , coding agents can construct task-specific procedures. This flexibility raises a practical question: how can we organize coding agents to achieve efficient robotic manipulation? Reasoning takes time. In a typical coding-agent policy, a single agent is responsible for the entire robot. Although it can issue concurrent motion commands, decisions about different robot components still pass through the same reasoning process. For example, diagnosing a failed grasp with one arm may delay an independent action with the other. This suggests that distributing reasoning across different robot components could improve task efficiency. We introduce Asynchronous Coding Agents as Policy ( ACAP ), an asynchronous framework that organizes a single robot as a team of persistent coding agents . Each agent is assigned to a control group, such as an arm with its gripper or a body group comprising the torso and chassis. Each member observes, reasons, and acts within its scope while other members continue their work, enabling parallel reasoning and execution. Member agents can use lightweight reasoning configurations for local tasks, while a more capable coordinating agent reasons about the overall task. However, independent reasoning does not imply independent physical actions. The agents share one physical robot, where one group’s actions can affect another’s reach, support, or clearance. A straightforward approach assigns each control group permanently to a member agent. While this can work well for independent tasks, tightly coupled manipulation, such as folding cloth or opening a bottle, requires close coordination between agents. Repeated communication and synchronization can introduce substantial overhead, while one agent’s recovery actions may change the object state and invalidate another’s plan. The challenge is therefore to coordinate agents efficiently as physical dependencies change. To address this challenge, ACAP introduces a hyper-agent that orchestrates the member agents. Unlike a fixed delegation scheme, the hyper-agent decides not only what tasks to delegate, but also when the lead should take direct control of multiple robot components. It uses task progress and execution feedback to coordinate dependencies and dynamically allocate control between the lead and member agents. For example, in four-flower insertion ( Figure 1 ), the hyper-agent coordinates access to the shared vase while member agents prepare flowers independently. For a shared towel-folding action, the lead can instead directly control both arms while members continue their analysis. The division of control can therefore adapt to the physical dependencies of the task. We evaluate ACAP on real-world manipulation tasks, including Fully Parallel, Parallel-Sequential, and Collaborative tasks. Across 72 real-world rollouts covering 25 task/platform settings, ACAP reduces the task-balanced time cost by 28.2% compared with a single-agent Direct Policy, with improvements across all three task categories. To evaluate agentic coordination, we compare ACAP with Fixed Delegation, where only members control their assigned groups. ACAP achieves lower mean time cost across all eight evaluated Collaborative settings, supporting the benefits of flexible coordination for collaborative manipulation. An additional 90-rollout repeated-trial study shows lower mean 30-minute-capped time cost than Direct on all six tasks. We also demonstrate ACAP in mobile manipulation. Furthermore, we collect a real-world rollout dataset containing observations, generated programs, agent interactions, actions, and physical feedback to support future research on embodied coding agents. Our contributions are threefold: 1. Asynchronous agentic policy framework. We introduce ACAP , which organizes a single robot as a team of persistent coding agents assigned to configurable control groups, enabling asynchronous reasoning and execution across different robot components. 2. Agentic coordination. We introduce a hyper-agent that coordinates task dependencies and dynamically manages control allocation between the lead and member agents, supporting both independent and tightly coupled manipulation. 3. Real-world evaluation and rollout dataset. We evaluate the efficiency and coordination capabilities of ACAP across diverse real-world manipulation tasks. We also present a rollout dataset containing observations, generated programs, agent interactions, and physical execution feedback.
