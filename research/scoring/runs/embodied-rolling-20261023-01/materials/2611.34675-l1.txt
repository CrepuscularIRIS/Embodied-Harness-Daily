===ABSTRACT===
Robots should adapt to unfamiliar environments and acquire new skills through interaction, then execute those skills efficiently with practice. Vision-language-action policies provide efficient control, but often need additional human demonstrations to handle unfamiliar settings; reasoning agents can discover solutions using broad prior knowledge and robot-control APIs, but repeated deliberation and repair are costly. Inspired by human learning through reasoning and practice, we propose RoboEvo$^2$, a framework for recursive harness-policy co-evolution. In Policy-to-Harness (P2H) evolution, policy failures guide a coding agent to search complementary Code, Code+ICL, and Code+Policy routes, consolidating verified solutions into reusable skills, routing strategies, and experience memory. In Harness-to-Policy (H2P) evolution, verified trajectories provide supervised training, while Counterfactual Preference Distillation (CPD) teaches the policy to favor successful corrections over failures from the same starting state. The improved policy provides stronger motor skills for subsequent search, while its remaining failures redirect harness evolution, closing the reciprocal loop. Through recursive co-evolution without additional human demonstrations, RoboEvo$^2$ improves standalone success from 11.0% to 84.1% and hybrid success from 30.4% to 90.5% on LIBERO-PRO, with similar gains and reduced agentic costs on RoboTwin C2R. Further experiments demonstrate autonomous task acquisition, cross-embodiment harness transfer, and 95.0% hybrid success on the Unitree G1-D. Our models and code will be publicly released.

===INTRO===
Robots should adapt rapidly to unfamiliar environments, acquire new skills through interaction, and execute those skills efficiently with practice. Human learning motivates this progression from deliberate problem solving to fluent execution, while retaining the flexibility to tackle new situations. Current robot systems offer complementary strengths toward this goal. Vision-language-action models (VLAs) [ 15 , 3 , 2 , 45 ] and world action models (WAMs) [ 41 , 11 , 13 ] enable efficient visuomotor control, but remain vulnerable to task and scene shifts [ 46 , 30 ] . Adapting them through imitation learning often requires collecting many additional human demonstrations, making continual skill acquisition costly. Foundation-model agents can draw on broad prior knowledge, reason about unfamiliar situations, and invoke robot-control APIs to discover and refine solutions [ 21 , 10 , 44 , 43 ] . However, repeated reasoning, tool use, and repair introduce latency and API costs that limit reliance on agentic execution for routine behavior. Realizing this progression requires a reciprocal learning loop between an agent’s harness —its executable skills, memory, and orchestration—and its low-level policy. Harness-centered recursive self-improvement methods refine programs and accumulate experience through feedback [ 9 , 20 , 32 , 35 ] , but keeping the motor policy fixed does not transfer newly acquired skills into the learned controller. Policy-centered methods use foundation models to guide autonomous data collection and improve learned controllers [ 12 , 47 , 23 ] , but do not jointly adapt the harness to the controller’s changing abilities. Co-evolution must therefore connect learning efficient policy behaviors from harness discoveries with adapting the harness to the improving policy . This reciprocity is essential because policy improvement changes both the motor skills available to the agent and the failures that still require exploration and repair. We introduce RoboEvo 2 , a framework for recursive harness–policy co-evolution that uses current-policy failures to drive both updates ( ). In Policy-to-Harness (P2H) evolution, a coding agent diagnoses failures, discovers verified corrections, and consolidates them into reusable skills, routing strategies, and persistent memory. In Harness-to-Policy (H2P) evolution, successful trajectories and matched failure–correction pairs teach the policy to reproduce effective corrections and disfavor failed alternatives. The updated policy then supplies stronger motor skills for subsequent agentic search, while its remaining failures redirect harness refinement. Concurrent studies also investigate harness–policy coupling [ 7 , 4 , 36 , 34 ] . RoboEvo 2 connects failure-guided, multi-route solution discovery with explicit learning from matched failures and corrections, progressively reducing repeated agentic intervention as acquired skills are internalized into the policy. First, to discover complementary capabilities, we develop a failure-conditioned, multi-route P2H repair engine. For each policy failure, it searches three solution routes in parallel: Code refines control programs, Code+ICL incorporates demonstration guidance, and Code+Policy composes programs with learned motor skills. Execution feedback drives iterative repair, and the successful candidate with the lowest normalized motion jerk is selected under matched conditions. This design combines complementary control capabilities to generate verified, smooth corrections targeted at current policy weaknesses. Second, to internalize corrective capabilities, we introduce Counterfactual Preference Distillation (CPD). CPD pairs failed and corrected continuations from the same starting state and optimizes a margin between their action-prediction losses relative to a frozen reference policy. Asymmetric proximity penalties discourage degradation of corrective behaviors and excessive suppression of failed alternatives. Combined with supervised learning on verified trajectories, CPD teaches both which corrections to reproduce and which failed alternatives to disfavor. Failure-aware sampling and continual-learning regularization support targeted acquisition and capability retention. Third, to sustain iterative capability reuse, we develop a harness update mechanism coupled with policy reuse across rounds. Compatible repairs update executable skills and routing logic, while persistent memory retains failure contexts, attempted solutions, and validated guidance. The updated policy then drives subsequent rollouts and participates in Code+Policy search, changing both the failures requiring exploration and the motor primitives available for repair. This closes the reciprocal loop: accumulated harness knowledge supports correction discovery, while internalized policy capabilities directly support subsequent search.
