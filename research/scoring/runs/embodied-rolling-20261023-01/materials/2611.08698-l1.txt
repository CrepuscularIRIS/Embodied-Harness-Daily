# VICTR: Value-Informed In-Context Learning for Vision-Language-Action Models

## Abstract
Recent advances in robot foundation models have highlighted the potential of in-context learning (ICL) for adapting robot behavior from demonstrations without further fine-tuning. Previous work has used retrieval-based post-training to equip pretrained vision-language-action (VLA) models with the capability for ICL by teaching them to condition action predictions on retrieved visually-relevant demonstration chunks. However, the effectiveness of retrieval hinges on selecting context relevant to the current execution stage, which visual similarity alone may not capture. We introduce VICTR, a framework enabling in-context learning in VLAs via progress-informed context retrieval built around an In-Context Value Function Estimator (IC-VFE). IC-VFE conditions on a reference demonstration video, a language instruction and the current observation to estimate task-relative progress. By conditioning progress estimation on a reference demonstration, IC-VFE can align the current state with a demonstrated execution even for tasks unseen during training. VICTR uses the estimated progress to select demonstration chunks at corresponding stages of execution and supplies them to a $π_{0.5}$ policy post-trained for in-context action generation. On unseen out-of-domain real-world manipulation tasks, VICTR raises the success rate from 6.2% for a fine-tuned $π_{0.5}$ baseline to 23.8% and more than doubles the task progress rate, from 28.9% to 55.5%. ## I Introduction
In-context learning (ICL) enables robot policies to adapt their behavior from demonstrations supplied as context, without repeatedly fine-tuning parameters for new tasks at deployment – mirroring large language models, which can infer new tasks from examples in their context without updating model parameters [ 1 ] . The emergence of this capability in large pretrained models motivates an analogous question for robotics: whether generalist policies can similarly adapt their behavior from demonstrations supplied at inference time. This form of adaptation can potentially address a persistent challenge for robotics foundation models. Despite substantial progress through training on diverse datasets [ 2 ] and reinforcement learning [ 3 ] , reliable out-of-the-box performance across diverse tasks and environments remains challenging [ 4 , 5 ] . Adapting a policy to a new domain often requires additional demonstration collection and fine-tuning, leading to a recurring cost as deployment requirements change. Recent industry systems have begun to demonstrate the promise of ICL, either as an emergent capability of large-scale pretraining [ 6 ] or through pretraining objectives that explicitly use demonstration videos to specify tasks [ 7 ] . Alongside these advances, RICL [ 8 ] demonstrates that pretrained VLAs can acquire ICL capabilities through retrieval-based post-training without retraining from scratch with extensive resources, learning to generate actions in-context conditioned on selected demonstration chunks. Retrieval-based ICL relies on selecting demonstration chunks that provide useful guidance for the robot’s next action. Existing retrieval methods rely on visual similarity [ 8 , 9 ] , but visually similar observations often occur at different execution stages, making appearance an ambiguous selection signal. We argue that task-relative progress provides an important retrieval signal beyond visual similarity, aligning the current observation with demonstration chunks at corresponding stages of execution. A related notion appears in RECAP [ 3 ] , whose value function estimator (VFE) predicts the negative number of steps remaining until success as a distribution over discretised bins, and forms advantages from it for reinforcement learning. Normalised by the episode horizon, that value runs from -1 at the start of an episode to 0 at success—the scale on which we define task-relative progress , a unitless quantity in [0,1] comparable across tasks of different lengths. We keep RECAP’s formulation and this support, but supervise it with semantic progress annotations rather than elapsed time, and use the estimate to retrieve demonstration chunks at inference rather than to optimise the policy. However, RECAP is conditioned on the current observation and language instruction without a reference demonstration, meaning that adaptation to new tasks depends on the VFE’s prior knowledge of the new language instruction. We seek a progress estimator that can directly use demonstration context to interpret task-specific execution progress, without requiring task-specific fine-tuning. To this end, we make progress estimation itself an in-context learning problem. We introduce an In-Context Value Function Estimator (IC-VFE) that jointly conditions on the current observation, a language instruction, and a reference demonstration video. The demonstration provides a task-specific reference in-context for the task goal and how it unfolds, supplying execution information beyond the language instruction. IC-VFE uses this reference to assess the current state’s progress relative to a demonstrated execution, enabling adaptation through context without task-specific fine-tuning. This estimator forms the core of VICTR, our V alue- I nformed C on t ext R etrieval framework for in-context VLA policies. In VICTR, we instantiate IC-VFE with a Gemma-based [ 10 ] vision-language architecture to predict task-relative progress, and post-train a \pi_{0.5} policy to generate actions conditioned on demonstration chunks retrieved by progress alignment. Both models’ weights remain fixed at deployment. In real-world evaluations, IC-VFE agrees with human progress annotations more closely than existing online value estimators on both seen and unseen tasks, and this advantage carries through to control: on unseen out-of-domain tasks, VICTR raises the success rate of a fine-tuned \pi_{0.5} baseline from 6.2% to 23.8% and more than doubles the task progress rate, from 28.9% to 55.5% (Figure 1 ). ## III Methodology
III-A VICTR Overview We consider a multi-task vision-language-action setting. A task is specified by a language instruction \ell\in\mathcal{L} , and an observation o_{t}=[X_{t}^{1},\dots,X_{t}^{n},q_{t}] consists of n camera images and proprioceptive state q_{t} . A VLA policy predicts action chunks a_{t:t+H} conditioned on the instruction and observations. a_{t:t+H}=\pi_{\theta}(o_{t},\ell) (1) In the following paragraphs we explain how \pi_{\theta} is adapted for retrieval-based in-context learning. Demonstration Pool. At deployment, each task is provided with a pool of 10 context demonstrations \mathcal{D}_{\ell} , each split into chunks aligned with the VLA action chunks. All demonstrations supply candidate chunks; the shortest demonstration additionally serves as the reference \tau_{\text{ctx}} for IC-VFE, detailed in Section III-C . Among successful demonstrations, shorter trajectories tend to contain fewer retries and corrective motions, making duration a simple proxy for demonstration quality and favoring a clean expert execution as the progress reference. Let c_{1},\dots,c_{M} denote all the candidate chunks across all demonstrations in the pool, and let o_{i} denote the observation at the start of chunk c_{i} . Conditioned on the task instruction and the shared reference demonstration, IC-VFE f_{\phi} assigns each chunk a progress estimate: \hat{v}_{i}=f_{\phi}(o_{i},\ell,\tau_{\text{ctx}}), (2) where \hat{v}_{i}\in[0,1] is the predicted progress. For retrieval, the demonstration pool is indexed at the chunk level as a key-value store. Each chunk c_{i} has a key consisting of its estimated progress \hat{v}_{i} and DINOv2 visual embedding h_{i} . Its associated content consists of the three camera images and proprioceptive state at the start of the chunk, together with the FAST-tokenized [ 25 ] action sequence.
