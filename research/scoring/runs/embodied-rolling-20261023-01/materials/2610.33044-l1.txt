# EKT-Router: Expert-Evidence Alignment for Embodied Vision-Language Models

## Abstract
Embodied vision-language models must answer questions from partial, evolving observations. Their success depends on both the reasoning capability selected and the evidence available to it. Expert routers adapt internal computation, while tool-augmented systems acquire external evidence; choosing these independently can leave a suitable expert without the evidence it needs. We introduce EKT-Router, a bounded framework that jointly selects a capability expert, optional knowledge, and perceptual tools. Outcome-derived signatures represent the overlapping strengths of General, Spatial Reasoning, and Spatiotemporal experts. A joint utility scores complete expert-evidence routes under a call budget. After acquisition, a packet verifier admits valid evidence with positive predicted expert-conditioned gain. The selected expert then answers once, reverting to the unchanged observation if all packets are rejected. Across 6 embodied QA benchmarks, EKT-Router achieves a mean score of 61.4, the highest among the compared systems, and averages 74.7 on 10 general vision-language benchmarks. With all external evidence disabled, question-conditioned expert selection alone improves over the best fixed expert by 1.75 points. Ablations show that learned routing is the dominant component and that knowledge and perception are complementary.

## 1 Introduction
Embodied vision–language models (EVLMs) answer questions about evolving scenes from partial observations. Such grounded understanding supports language-guided robotic planning and interaction ( Liang et al., 2025 ) . Queries require semantic knowledge, precise geometry, or temporal state tracking; decisive cues may occupy only a few pixels or span several frames ( Majumdar et al., 2024 ; Sermanet et al., 2024 ; Yang et al., 2025 ) . Success depends on both the selected capability and the evidence it receives. A relative-position question may benefit from localization, whereas an event-order question requires temporal context; neither source is useful merely because it is available. The route must therefore account for what the question demands, what each expert can infer from the original observation, and what an external provider can add. We study expert–evidence alignment under partial observability : matching a reusable capability with query-specific evidence, while retaining a generalist path and avoiding an unbounded agent loop. Existing methods address different parts of this problem. Embodied generalists such as LEO and RynnBrain integrate perception, language, and action capabilities, whereas SpatialVLM, SpatialRGPT, and RoboSpatial use geometric supervision to strengthen spatial reasoning ( Huang et al., 2024 ; Dang et al., 2026 ; Chen et al., 2024a ; Cheng et al., 2024 ; Song et al., 2025 ) . Mixture-of-experts methods provide conditional parameter paths for efficient specialization ( Wu et al., 2024 ; Shen et al., 2024 ) ; ViperGPT and LLaVA-Plus instead compose external vision modules to recover information that a backbone cannot reliably infer ( Surís et al., 2023 ; Liu et al., 2024a ) . These expert routers focus on selecting a parameter path, whereas the cited tool-augmented systems acquire evidence for a fixed or implicit reasoner. Jointly choosing the capability path and its external evidence makes their dependence an explicit part of the routing decision. These coupled choices pose two challenges. (i) Evidence value is expert-dependent. Expert strengths overlap, and the same knowledge or tool output can provide different gains to different experts. Selecting the expert and evidence independently can therefore overlook useful combinations. (ii) Predicted evidence value need not match the usefulness of the returned packets. A promising route may still yield incomplete or ambiguous evidence; deciding what to retain requires inspecting packet coverage, confidence, ambiguity, and provenance after execution. The challenge is to account for this newly available information without restarting expert selection or expanding the acquisition budget. Our design separates these uncertainties in time: select a complete expert–evidence route before acquisition, then revise only the evidence decision after inspecting the packets. Conditional evidence use thus requires neither repeated expert selection nor repeated answer generation. EKT-Router implements this decision in two stages (Figure 1 ). Offline, independent capability post-training produces three Qwen3-VL-8B LoRA experts: General through continual pretraining (CPT), Spatial Reasoning through spatial supervision, and Spatiotemporal through video supervision. Diagnostic outcomes encode their overlapping strengths as continuous capability signatures, while paired executions supervise a complete-tuple route utility. Online, a frozen Qwen3-VL-2B provides a query-aware control view; the router selects one expert, optional Knowledge, and a budget-feasible Tool subset. Knowledge supplies task priors, while Tools return grounding, depth, or text as typed packets. A bounded verifier selects a valid evidence subset by its predicted score margin over the null reference. The fixed expert answers once from the observation and retained evidence; rejecting every packet recovers the evidence-free path. The two stages assign different roles to prediction and observation: the router estimates value before paying for evidence, while the verifier reassesses the returned packets after that cost is incurred. The null action belongs to both stages, so evidence use is optional throughout. Our contributions are: 1) We formulate expert–evidence alignment under partial observability as one decision coupling a capability path with optional knowledge and a budget-feasible tool set. 2) We introduce EKT-Router , which derives continuous expert signatures and learns complete-route utility from answer outcomes. Typed packet verification refines evidence use after acquisition and preserves an exact evidence-free fallback. 3) Across 16 benchmarks, EKT-Router achieves the best embodied QA average among the compared systems while matching strong general VLMs. Matched controls show that expert selection pays off even without external evidence, and ablations identify learned routing as the dominant component and knowledge and perception as complementary.

## 3 Method
EKT-Router makes a two-stage decision under partial information. The Utility Router first selects an expert and typed Knowledge and Tool actions. Once the packets return, the Packet Verifier revises the evidence decision on the same utility scale, keeping the expert fixed. Verification uses packet attributes, not candidate answers; only the final expert generates an answer.
