# ProLoop: Progress-Aware Closed-Loop Framework via Structured State Relations for Long-Horizon Robotic Task Planning

## Abstract
Long-horizon robotic tasks are commonly decomposed into semantic subgoals to simplify planning. However, subgoal progress in LLM-based planning is typically inferred implicitly from interaction histories. Inaccurate estimates can misguide execution and allow local errors to propagate. To address this, we propose ProLoop, a Progress-Aware Closed-Loop framework that derives explicit subgoal progress from structured relations between desired and execution states. A learned State-Relation Monitor assesses these relations, which are then aggregated into subgoal progress as ongoing, deviated, or completed. The resulting progress guides execution adaptation, enabling reliable progress-aware control throughout execution. Experiments on AgentBoard ALFWorld and a real-world robotic platform show that ProLoop improves success rate by 12.94% overall and by 15.46% on Hard tasks, while reducing environment interactions by 32.3%.

## I Introduction
Recent advances in robot task planning increasingly leverage large language models (LLMs) to generate coherent multi-step plans [ 1 , 2 ] . Despite strong performance on complex tasks, LLM-based planning remains susceptible to hallucinations and logical inconsistencies [ 3 , 4 ] . To manage long-horizon planning complexity, hierarchical methods decompose tasks into semantic subgoals, allowing each stage to focus on a simpler intermediate objective [ 5 , 6 , 7 ] . However, during subgoal execution, subgoal progress is still commonly inferred implicitly by the LLM from accumulated interaction trajectories and multi-source information [ 8 ] . Inaccurate progress estimates can therefore misguide execution, allowing local errors to accumulate and propagate across subsequent subgoals. Building on these observations, recent studies [ 9 , 8 ] address this problem by introducing explicit checks at selected checkpoints to assess the current execution state and then support replanning (see Fig. 1 ). These checks form a feedback loop that reduces reliance on implicit progress inference by the LLM. However, such checkpoint-based feedback cannot timely respond to changes in subgoal progress, since reassessment occurs only at the next checkpoint. During robotic execution, this delay can allow erroneous actions to alter the environment state, making recovery costly or even infeasible [ 10 , 11 ] . We therefore ask: How can robots maintain reliable progress awareness throughout subgoal execution to enable timely closed-loop adaptation? A useful intuition comes from how people perform everyday tasks. People assess progress by relating the current state to the intended outcome before deciding what to do next. Following this intuition, we define robotic subgoal progress by aggregating structured relations between desired subgoal conditions and the evolving execution state into ongoing , deviated , or completed . This formulation raises two key challenges. Constructing comparable desired and execution states. A semantic subgoal specifies its desired outcome in natural language, whereas the execution state must be reconstructed from evolving actions, observations, perception, and contextual information. Making the two states comparable is difficult, but essential for accurate subgoal progress assessment. Using progress to guide execution without sacrificing LLM flexibility. Even with reliable progress assessment, different progress states call for different responses. Excessive intervention during ongoing execution may disrupt useful LLM reasoning and action generation, whereas insufficient intervention after deviation may allow errors to persist. Moreover, completion should be leveraged beyond stage termination, as its associated outcomes must be selectively transferred to support subsequent planning. To address these challenges, we propose ProLoop, a Progress-Aware Closed-Loop framework for long-horizon robotic task planning. For each active subgoal generated by the LLM-Based Subgoal Planner , Structured State Construction first converts the subgoal’s desired outcome and evolving execution information into comparable desired and execution states under a shared task-state schema. A learned State-Relation Monitor then assesses these condition-level relations and derives the active subgoal progress as ongoing, deviated, or completed. The resulting progress drives Progress-Guided Adaptation : ongoing execution preserves the underlying LLM’s reasoning process, deviation triggers targeted correction, and completion permits transition to the next subgoal. Additionally, upon completion, Completion-Gated Memory Update uses planner-generated retention cues to selectively summarize task-relevant execution information for subsequent planning. To assess the effectiveness of ProLoop, we conduct experiments on the AgentBoard [ 12 ] ALFWorld household planning benchmark, which contains six task categories with easy and hard difficulty levels. Empirical results demonstrate improved overall performance, with especially substantial gains on hard tasks. We further validate ProLoop on a physical robot. Our contributions are threefold: 1. We propose ProLoop , a unified progress-aware closed-loop framework for robotic task planning that integrates structured state construction, state-relation monitoring, and progress-guided adaptation. 2. We define explicit subgoal progress through structured relations between desired and execution states, distinguishing ongoing , deviated , and completed progress. 3. We demonstrate that ProLoop improves task success rate and progress rate on ALFWorld while requiring fewer environment interactions. We further validate its feasibility on a physical robot.

## IV Methodology
In this section, we detail our proposed framework, ProLoop . As illustrated in Fig. 2 , the framework forms a closed loop around each active subgoal through structured state construction, state-relation monitoring, progress-guided adaptation, and completion-gated memory update. The LLM-Based Subgoal Planner generates an active semantic subgoal and retention cues. Structured State Construction represents the desired subgoal outcome and evolving execution information under a shared task-state schema. The State-Relation Monitor compares each desired condition against the current execution state and classifies the resulting relation as supported , unresolved , or contradicted . These relations are then aggregated into ongoing , deviated , or completed progress. Progress-Guided Adaptation maps the resulting progress state to continuation, correction, or subgoal transition. Upon completion, Completion-Gated Memory Update uses the retention cues to summarize the local trajectory into a new memory entry before the next subgoal is generated.
