# METATRON: A Promptable Whole-Body Execution Layer for Humanoids

## Abstract
Humanoid controllers often require task-specific training despite sharing the need for balance and whole-body coordination. We present Metatron, a promptable whole-body execution layer combining a generative base policy with a programmable skill layer. The base policy receives partial goals, collectively called a prompt, that can combine timed body-part targets, velocity commands, and style cues. It produces diverse whole-body motions consistent with these goals. Training combines motion capture, synthetic recovery trajectories, and retargeted scene-interaction motions. After training, the policy is frozen. Stateful programs called skills express task intent as prompts at each control step, drawing rough geometric sketches that the policy turns into coordinated movement. Skills are ordinary code, refined by coding agents that analyze motion data and fit compact rules without task-specific policy training. On the physical Unitree G1, one frozen checkpoint supports walking, recovery, teleoperated object pickup, and sitting on furniture. Comparative evaluations show higher reaching success and push robustness than the tested reference-tracking pipelines. The same recipe produces climbing and acrobatics on a simulated character. Metatron enables coding agents to turn learned motor capabilities into new skills through code, without further policy training.

## I Introduction
Consider a humanoid that must walk to a table in a particular style, grasp a cup at a designated moment, recover after a shove, present it to a human, and shake hands. Today this typically requires separate learned controllers for locomotion [ 3 ] , manipulation [ 4 ] , recovery [ 5 ] , compliant control [ 6 ] , and motion tracking [ 7 ] . These controllers duplicate capabilities such as balance and coordination, and transitions between them demand additional engineering. We argue that a more scalable recipe is to separate what a motion should accomplish from how to coordinate the body: train one general-purpose whole-body policy with a flexible goal-conditioning interface, and let task-specific programs specify the goals. The policy becomes an execution layer that provides physical coordination; the programs become the controllers that supply intent. Each program specifies a partial motion goal that we call a prompt : a sitting command specifies the shape and location of a nearby object, while a style cue biases the generated motion toward example poses. We present Metatron , a promptable whole-body execution layer for humanoids ( Figure 1 ). It combines a capable base policy with a programmable skill layer. The base policy builds on MaskedMimic [ 8 ] , which trains a dense motion tracker and distills it into a generative policy that accepts sparse body-part targets at specified future times. Once trained, the policy is frozen and a runtime layer runs programs called skills that convert task intent into prompts at each control step ( Section IV ). Skills are ordinary code, authored by humans or coding agents. We show how LLM agents analyze motion clips and fit compact rules that capture shared motion characteristics, turning task design into a software problem. Reinforcement learning trains the motion experts; on-policy distillation teaches the base policy to imitate them under partial goals, including at states reached by the student itself. This training supports approximate goal sketches rather than requiring exact reference trajectories. Metatron also extends MaskedMimic in several directions. Forward kinematics reconstructs local body poses from joint and inertial measurements, without external motion capture; world-position goals additionally require localization. Scene elements are represented as geometric primitives specified by measured dimensions and poses. Style conditioning allows operators and skills to select the generated motion’s character. We augment the training data with synthetic push-recovery and get-up trajectories, extending the repertoire beyond what motion capture alone provides. On the modeling side, spherical projection bounds the stochastic latent, while predictive and temporal auxiliary losses regularize its fused representation ( Section III-C ). Our contributions are a whole-body policy with a flexible prompt interface, training data augmented with scene interactions and synthetic recoveries ( Section V ), and a skill layer whose code can be refined by coding agents without policy retraining ( Section IV ). One frozen checkpoint per embodiment supports the demonstrated tasks on physical G1 and simulated SOMA. Separate G1 comparisons show higher whole-body reaching success and push robustness than the tested reference-tracking pipelines ( Section VI ).

## III Metatron: Base Policy
Training proceeds in two phases. Dense motion trackers learn whole-body coordination from complete reference motions using privileged simulator state ( Section III-A ). Masked on-policy distillation then trains one generative policy per embodiment, conditioned on partial prompts through a flexible interface ( Sections III-B and III-C ). Distillation can combine multiple specialist trackers, with each frozen expert labeling states from its own training group. The deployable policy \pi_{\theta}(a_{t}\mid o_{t},a_{t-1},g_{t}) maps a prompt g_{t} , current observations and sensor history o_{t} , and the previous action a_{t-1} to joint actions using only deployment-available inputs. Once frozen, stateful programs called skills construct prompts to express diverse behaviors without retraining ( Section IV ). III-A Dense Motion Tracker An MLP policy learns whole-body coordination from complete reference motions: a_{t}^{\mathrm{trk}}=\pi^{\mathrm{trk}}\bigl(s_{t}^{\mathrm{trk}},y_{t},a_{t-1}\bigr), (1) where s_{t}^{\mathrm{trk}} includes privileged simulator state and y_{t} encodes complete reference states at a configured set of future offsets. We optimize the tracker with PPO [ 23 ] . Its reward combines tracking terms [ 9 ] and action-rate regularization [ 7 ] : r_{t}=\sum_{k}w_{k}\exp(-\lambda_{k}e_{k,t})-w_{a}\mathcal{R}_{\mathrm{act},t}+r_{t}^{\mathrm{extra}}. (2) The errors e_{k,t} measure body position, orientation, and velocity tracking; w_{k} and \lambda_{k} set their weights and sensitivities. The action regularizer \mathcal{R}_{\mathrm{act},t} penalizes changes between consecutive commands, and r_{t}^{\mathrm{extra}} collects selected-body precision terms and embodiment-specific penalties. These rewards train the trackers, not the distilled student. The supplementary material details the G1 BONES and SOMA experts’ rewards, architectures, and optimization settings. III-B Prompt Interface To support partial goals at runtime, we distill the frozen trackers into Metatron . At timestep t , a prompt combines four optional goal streams and scene context, g_{t}=\left(\mathcal{C}_{t}^{w},\mathcal{C}_{t}^{\ell},u_{t},\xi_{t},b_{t}\right). (3) Masks identify which bodies, properties, and timesteps are constrained, so an omitted goal can remain unspecified. Spatio-temporal goals: \mathcal{C}_{t}^{w} specifies body positions or orientations in world coordinates, each with a time-to-arrival. Position and orientation constraints are independently optional: a prompt can request a planar location while leaving height free, specify a timed hand target, or supply dense whole-body poses. Canonicalized pose goals: \mathcal{C}_{t}^{\ell} describes the desired pose independently of its world placement. Horizontal positions are relative to the target torso and aligned with its heading; heights are measured above the target pose’s lowest body point. A raised-hand pose therefore retains its meaning regardless of world position or heading. These goals are not offsets from the robot’s current pose. Velocity: u_{t} specifies planar travel velocity in the agent’s heading frame, without specifying an endpoint. Style cue: \xi_{t} specifies the character of the motion through an example clip. A small set of frames provides canonicalized body poses and optional heading-aligned velocities. Pooling these frames into one token discards temporal order: the cue describes example motion states without assigning arrival times. A location goal can therefore be paired with running or crouched-walking examples without prescribing their trajectory. Scenes: For object interaction, each scene element is decomposed offline into bounding boxes b_{t} (e.g. a sofa yields seat and backrest boxes). The policy observes the eight corner positions of each box in the agent’s heading frame, from which both position and orientation are recoverable. This compact, fixed-width representation needs only pre-measured dimensions and an object-pose estimate at deployment. III-C Generative Policy A partial prompt admits many valid motions.
