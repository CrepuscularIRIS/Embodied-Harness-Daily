# BiPOP: Partial-Order Planning for Long-Horizon Bimanual Robotic Manipulation

## Abstract
Bimanual robotic manipulation is versatile, yet its central challenge lies not only in executing dexterous motion with each hand, but also in coordinating the two arms for long-horizon manipulation under coupled spatial and temporal constraints. Prior work has largely focused on endowing robots with human-level manipulation skills, paying less attention to the structure of bimanual behaviour itself: many practical tasks are a coordinated composition of reusable unimanual skills and a small set of synchronous bimanual skills. This makes high-level spatio-temporal coordination, rather than skill acquisition alone, a key bottleneck for efficient bimanual execution. We propose BiPOP, a framework that couples Large Language Models (LLMs) with Temporal Partial-Order Planning for long-horizon bimanual manipulation. At its core is an explicit spatio-temporal model that partitions the workspace by arm reachability and captures inter-arm dependencies, arm assignment, synchronization requirements, and opportunities for safe concurrent execution. Building on this model, an LLM translates natural-language instructions into a formal PDDL specification, while a temporal planner enforces the constraints and schedules actions, yielding plans that are logically consistent at the symbolic level and efficient through parallel execution. On simulated long-horizon tasks of varying complexity, BiPOP outperforms open-loop planning with strong reasoning models and closed-loop ReAct-style planning in success rate, planning time, and plan efficiency, and the full pipeline runs end to end on the real bimanual robot NICOL. A failure analysis further shows that LLM-only planners mainly violate spatial and temporal coordination constraints, which motivates delegating these constraints to a symbolic planner. ## I Introduction
Humans effortlessly perform bimanual tasks, from tying shoelaces to flipping book pages while holding a coffee cup, orchestrating complex spatial and temporal synchronization without conscious thought. Robots, in contrast, struggle even with apparently simple bimanual tasks, which demand planning, synchronization, and coordination. Bridging this gap remains an inherent challenge in robotics [ 1 , 2 ] . Deep learning techniques ease the acquisition of dexterous bimanual manipulation, such as folding shirts [ 3 ] , cutting vegetables [ 4 ] , and scooping food [ 5 ] . However, most approaches focus on learning human-level skills as a whole and overlook high-level task planning, which demands task decomposition, subtask assignment between the arms, execution sequencing and parallelization over long horizons. Large Language Models (LLMs) have shown strong reasoning and planning abilities across domains [ 6 ] , and recent work applies them to robotic task planning [ 7 , 8 , 9 , 10 ] . Yet generating embodiment-aware, executable plans is difficult for complex manipulation, where LLMs remain prone to long-horizon reasoning errors and hallucinations [ 11 , 12 ] , especially in spatially constrained scenarios. To mitigate these limitations, LLMs have been combined with classical symbolic planners [ 13 ] : by translating natural-language instructions into declarative representations such as PDDL [ 14 ] , LLMs enable plans with logical-correctness guarantees. However, these approaches generally do not address the spatio-temporal coordination intrinsic to bimanual manipulation, in particular inter-arm dependencies, task allocation, synchronization, and parallelization. Our research question is therefore: how can LLMs be integrated with symbolic planning to achieve efficient spatial and temporal coordination for long-horizon bimanual robotic tasks? To address this, we propose BiPOP (Bimanual Partial-Order Planning), a framework (Fig. 1 ) for long-horizon bimanual task planning that integrates semantic reasoning with explicit spatio-temporal coordination. Our contributions are threefold. First, we introduce a spatio-temporal model that partitions the workspace by arm reachability and encodes inter-arm dependencies, synchronization, and safe concurrency as durative PDDL actions with mutual-exclusion predicates. Second, we couple LLM-based problem generation with temporal planning: an LLM translates the task description and scene information into a PDDL problem, and a temporal planner computes a validated partial-order plan with a short symbolic makespan. Third, we evaluate BiPOP against open-loop and closed-loop LLM planners on three simulated bimanual task domains and deploy it on a real bimanual robot NICOL [ 15 ] . The results show that BiPOP outperforms open-loop planning with recent OpenAI and DeepSeek reasoning models and closed-loop ReAct-style planning in success rate, planning time, and plan efficiency.

## III Method
Given a task description in natural language, we aim to generate a valid and efficient plan for a bimanual robot based on the initial scene configurations, e.g., spatial information about the objects with respect to the robot. To this end, this section introduces the three elements of BiPOP : (A) Spatio-temporal modelling, (B) Bi manual PDDL definition with the LLM, and (C) P artial- O rder P lanning with the temporal planner. III-A Spatio-temporal Modelling To enable efficient bimanual manipulation, we first develop a spatio-temporal model that characterizes the reachable workspace of each arm and the coordination relations between them, as illustrated in Fig. 3 . The workspace is partitioned into two uncoordinated regions, each reachable by a single arm, and one coordinated region (the overlap area ) reachable by both. Arms operating in separate uncoordinated regions on distinct objects are spatially independent and can execute concurrently as parallel unimanual skills. Within the coordinated region, the arms become spatially and temporally coupled, requiring either asynchronous coordination, in which one arm establishes the preconditions for the other, or synchronous coordination, in which both arms execute a tightly coupled bimanual action. This workspace partitioning operates at the task-planning level: it determines whether two actions may proceed concurrently based on the arms, objects, and regions they involve, rather than on the geometric feasibility of the underlying trajectories. Two actions are scheduled concurrently only when they share no symbolic resource (neither arm, object, nor region), while all remaining cases are serialized through explicit coordination (ordering) constraints. Consequently, the resulting schedules are sound at the task level. Collision-free execution of concurrent motions, including cross-arm trajectory interference near the coordinated region, is delegated to the downstream motion planner. When a concurrent assignment proves geometrically infeasible, the motion planner may serialize it without affecting the validity of the produced plan. Planning for a bimanual task thus begins with identifying the regions in which the task-relevant objects and target poses lie: an open-vocabulary object detector (e.g., LocateAnything [ 38 ] ) localizes the objects, and the resulting bounding boxes are mapped onto the workspace partition. III-B Bimanual PDDL Definition with the LLM III-B 1 PDDL Formalization The Planning Domain Definition Language (PDDL) [ 14 ] is a declarative language for formalizing planning problems in a standardized form. A planning problem is defined as a tuple \mathcal{T}=\langle\mathcal{O},\mathcal{P},\mathcal{A},\mathcal{S},\mathcal{G}\rangle, where \mathcal{O} is the set of objects, \mathcal{P} the set of predicates, \mathcal{A} the set of actions, \mathcal{S} the initial state, and \mathcal{G} the goal. A state is a set of grounded predicates, instantiated over objects with Boolean values, and the goal is a logical expression over such predicates; each action is specified by typed parameters together with preconditions and effects expressed as predicates over these parameters. A PDDL specification comprises a domain , declaring the object types, predicates, and parameterized actions of the task world, and a problem , instantiating a concrete set of objects, an initial state, and a goal. III-B 2 Domain Definition To encode the proposed spatio-temporal model and the underlying manipulation logic, we define four object types, arm , area , object ( obj ), and point , together with predicates describing their properties and relations. To support the durative actions introduced below, we additionally define predicates that represent the execution state. The predicate available indicates whether an arm is unoccupied, e.g., not holding an object, while arm_free indicates whether it is currently idle, which prevents a single arm from executing multiple actions at once.
