# When Black-Box Robot Policies Fail: Online Sensor Diagnosis and Repair with a Transparent Surrogate

## Abstract
Robot foundation models may generalize across tasks and embodiments, but their actions still depend on sensors that can fail. When the policy is frozen or inaccessible, can an external monitor diagnose and repair corrupted inputs without access to its internals? We test a structured surrogate that separates periodic gait from sensor-dependent corrections to guide input repair around a frozen locomotion policy. On simulated Go2, offline diagnosis raises walking success from 259/640 to 429/640 episodes; online repair reaches 382/640, with no triggers in 64 held-out fault-free episodes. A matched boosted-tree surrogate predicts clean actions similarly well but yields no detected repair improvement over no repair. Separate fits improve walking on Go1 and ANYmal C, although phase-aware gradients perform better. Evidence remains limited to one policy and a partly screened simulated fault suite; robot foundation model and hardware validation remain open.

## 1 Introduction
Robot foundation models promise transfer across tasks and embodiments [ 9 , 7 ] , yet their actions depend on reliable sensor observations. When internals are inaccessible, monitors see only inputs and actions. We study the frozen multi-embodiment URMA locomotion policy [ 2 ] . On Go2 at 0.5\,\mathrm{m/s} , a 0.3\,\mathrm{rad/s} yaw-gyroscope bias prevents walking in 16/16 episodes. Classical diagnosis uses plant or sensor models [ 6 ] , whereas gradient attribution and VLA steering require policy access [ 13 , 16 , 5 , 3 ] . Runtime guardrails constrain plans [ 11 ] , while masked training builds sensor robustness into the policy [ 10 ] . Unusual sensor readings may nevertheless result from legitimate disturbances. Prior work distills locomotion into boosted trees or symbolic expressions [ 1 ] . Structured controllers and sparse mixtures also expose control structure [ 14 , 17 ] . Our surrogate separates periodic gait from sensor-dependent corrections, ranks abnormal input contributions, and guides repair. The frozen policy produces every action, so the surrogate never replaces the controller. We contribute diagnosis and repair without policy gradients, paired offline and online evaluations, and tests on three quadrupeds. This controlled setting motivates external supervision for robot foundation models [ 15 ] . Direct validation on those models remains open.

## 2 Method
Structured action surrogate. Used only for diagnosis, the surrogate predicts 12 joint-action targets u_{t} at command speed v (Figure 1 ): u_{t}=u_{\rm F}(\varphi_{t},v)+\operatorname{clip}_{[-0.15,0.15]}(r_{t}),\qquad r_{t,j}=b_{j}(\varphi_{t},v)+\sum_{i}c_{i,t,j}. (1) We represent the gait u_{F} using four Fourier harmonics. Both the gait and correction models are conditioned on the commanded speed v . The correction r_{t} combines a phase-dependent baseline b_{j}(\varphi_{t},v) with contributions from individual input features. The contribution c_{i,t,j} of standardized input z_{i,t} to joint j is c_{i,t,j}=z_{i,t}[a_{ij}(v)+a^{\mathrm{s}}_{ij}(v)\sin(2\pi\varphi_{t})+a^{\mathrm{c}}_{ij}(v)\cos(2\pi\varphi_{t})] . The 57 inputs describe base motion, gravity, joint states, previous actions, contacts, command, and timing. The baseline and contributions exactly reconstruct the surrogate’s correction before clipping. We collect 64,000 policy state–action pairs at four speeds, fit the gait, then fit the remaining action error by ridge regression. Feedback fitting filters startup and invalid-phase rows and holds out episodes. We also collect 32,000 policy action targets at states reached under alternating policy/surrogate control [ 12 ] to broaden feedback training. Front-left touchdown resets the gait phase. Between touchdowns, the clock advances using its latest period estimate; touchdown intervals of 8–60 steps update this estimate with averaging rate 0.2. Offline fitting and fault-free reference construction use simulator-derived contacts. During diagnosis and repair, phase is computed from the potentially corrupted contact readings visible to the policy. Diagnosis from abnormal corrections. Mechanism-based diagnosis (MBD) uses the surrogate’s additive decomposition to identify which sensor contributions drive abnormal corrections. We discretize gait phase into 20 equal bins (5% of a cycle each); this resolution was fixed before fault evaluation. From fault-free trajectories, we estimate in each bin \beta the mean and standard deviation of each joint correction, \bar{r}_{j}(\beta) and s_{j}(\beta) , and the mean contribution of each input, \bar{c}_{i,j}(\beta) . At time t , joint j is flagged when its correction deviates from the phase-matched reference by more than two standard deviations: \mathcal{F}=\{(t,j):|r_{t,j}-\bar{r}_{j}(\beta_{t})|>2s_{j}(\beta_{t})\} . For each sensor unit U , MBD then computes S_{U}=\sum_{(t,j)\in\mathcal{F}}\sum_{i\in U}\bigl(c_{i,t,j}-\bar{c}_{i,j}(\beta_{t})\bigr)\operatorname{sgn}\bigl(r_{t,j}-\bar{r}_{j}(\beta_{t})\bigr). (2) Thus, contributions that push a flagged correction farther in its abnormal direction increase the sensor’s score, whereas opposing contributions decrease it. MBD ranks 34 sensor units: the gyroscope, gravity vector, 12 joint positions, 12 joint velocities, four contacts, and four contact ages. Offline diagnosis aggregates scores across 16 faulty trajectories, excluding the first 25 steps and the final 50 steps before a fall. Although evaluation assumes that a fault is present, MBD abstains when no corrections are flagged. Baselines. Phase deviation measures departure from each sensor’s healthy value at the same phase: |x_{i}-\bar{x}_{i}(\beta)|/s_{i}^{x}(\beta) . It averages over channels in each sensor unit and sums over time. A gated variant uses only steps where MBD flags an abnormal correction. Phase-aware gradients require policy internals. They compute (x_{i}-\bar{x}_{i}(\beta))^{2}\|\partial\pi/\partial x_{i}\|_{2}^{2}/12 per channel, subtract its healthy phase-conditioned baseline, average over time, and sum across channels in each sensor unit. GBM–TreeSHAP uses twelve boosted regressors [ 4 ] . Both fits use the same 12,740 clean and 7,827 alternating-control rows, with identical inputs, splits, normalization, nominal gait, residual target, and correction bound. TreeSHAP uses tree-path-dependent expectations [ 8 ] . We group its attributions by sensor and apply Eq. ( 2 ) with its own anomaly mask. All methods share sensor groups, diagnosis seeds, repair episodes, and PRR. Input repair and online detection. Phase-referenced repair (PRR) changes only the selected unit U . For a sensor that varies with phase, it supplies the healthy standardized mean, \hat{z}_{U,t}=\bar{z}_{U}(\beta_{t}) . Otherwise, it estimates and subtracts bias: \hat{z}_{U,t}=z_{U,t}-\hat{b}_{U,t},\qquad\hat{b}_{U,t}=0.99\hat{b}_{U,t-1}+0.01\left[z_{U,t}-\bar{z}_{U}(\beta_{t})\right],\quad\hat{b}_{U,0}=0. (3) The score \kappa_{U}=|U|^{-1}\sum_{i\in U}\operatorname{SD}_{\beta}[\bar{z}_{i}(\beta)] measures how strongly a sensor normally varies over the gait cycle. If \kappa_{U}\geq 0.4 , PRR replaces its reading with the healthy reference for the current phase. Otherwise, it estimates and subtracts an additive bias. We selected the threshold using development data. Contacts and contact ages are replaced with constant nominal values. Repaired values return to policy input units before the next policy call. All repair rules were fixed before evaluation. Offline MBD diagnoses prior faulty trajectories; repair runs in fresh episodes. Online MBD starts after 25 steps and measures the fraction of flagged joint–time pairs in nonoverlapping 50-step windows at 50\,\mathrm{Hz} . From the fifth window onward, it triggers when the lower quartile over all completed windows exceeds a calibrated threshold. We calibrate on 128 fault-free episodes in total. Each noise regime’s threshold is the maximum of this statistic over its calibration episodes and decision times. Evaluation supplies the true noisy/noiseless regime. At the first trigger, accumulated scores select a sensor; repair starts on the next step and lasts until episode end. Runtime needs no policy weights, gradients, or additional policy calls.

## 3 Experiments
Protocol. URMA controls Go2 in MuJoCo for 20 seconds at 50\,\mathrm{Hz} from randomized starts, commanded at v=0.5\,\mathrm{m/s} . Pushed episodes receive a 250\,\mathrm{N} force in a random horizontal direction for 0.2 seconds at six seconds. Survival means completing the episode. Walking also requires mean velocity in each planar axis, after the first two seconds, to be within \max(0.05\,\mathrm{m/s},0.25v) of its command.
