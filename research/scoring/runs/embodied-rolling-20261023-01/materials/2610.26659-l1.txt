# SpatialScout: Can VLM Agents Actively Explore and Map 3D Scenes?

## Abstract
Spatial intelligence requires agents to actively acquire visual evidence and integrate observations into a coherent representation of the three-dimensional world. Yet spatial question answering offers limited evidence of whether models can construct accurate metric maps through autonomous exploration. We introduce SpatialScout, a benchmark for evaluating vision-language model (VLM) agents on active object-level 3D mapping. SpatialScout comprises 80 diverse indoor scenes containing 3,140 target objects. Given an object inventory, RGB observations, camera calibration, and camera poses, agents control a camera to explore each scene and estimate object centers in a shared coordinate frame. The task requires coordinating viewpoint selection, cross-view association, geometric estimation, and map revision under bounded interaction budgets. Evaluation jointly measures localization accuracy and target coverage through object-scale overlap scores that penalize missing predictions. Experiments with frontier VLMs reveal substantial limitations: the strongest evaluated model, Claude Opus 5.5, achieves a mean overlap score of 41.71%, with 47.23% of targets exceeding an intersection-over-union (IoU) threshold of 0.5 and only 3.25% exceeding 0.9. These results reveal a substantial gap between recovering coarse object layouts and constructing complete, precise metric maps. SpatialScout provides a quantitative testbed for developing agents that build reliable 3D spatial representations through active perception. Code is available at https://github.com/JesseZZZZZ/SpatialScout.

It requires agents to actively observe their surroundings to resolve occlusion and perspective ambiguities, infer object locations and spatial relationships, and integrate evidence across views into a consistent 3D representation [ 20 , 21 ] . Evaluating this entire process is central to assessing the spatial capabilities of vision-language models (VLMs). Existing spatial intelligence benchmarks widely adopt image- and video-based question answering (QA) to assess counting, direction, distance, and spatial relations [ 55 , 50 ] . Although these evaluations have revealed substantial model limitations, answer accuracy imposes only limited constraints on spatial understanding. Finite answer choices allow models to arrive at correct answers through guessing or elimination; language priors and biases in answer distributions may also enable correct responses based on non-spatial cues [ 15 , 1 ] . More importantly, spatial QA does not fully capture real-world demands: agents must autonomously gather evidence, update spatial representations, and use them to guide subsequent actions [ 34 , 62 ] . Evaluating frontier VLMs therefore requires end-to-end assessment of this multi-step process, beyond answer accuracy alone. Bridging this gap requires evaluating autonomous information acquisition and spatial representation construction within a single task, forming a closed loop of observation, reasoning, and decision-making. Recent benchmarks such as ESI-Bench [ 21 ] and S 3 -Bench [ 51 ] introduce active interaction into spatial QA, while Theory of Space [ 62 ] examines the construction of discrete cognitive maps. Our key advance is to make the construction of object maps in continuous 3D space through active interaction a direct evaluation objective for spatial intelligence. Models must autonomously acquire visual evidence and recover the 3D positions of a target set in a common coordinate frame. This task couples active perception, cross-view association, geometric estimation, and scene modeling: the current spatial estimate determines what to observe next, while the selected viewpoint shapes the evidence available thereafter. Through spatial construction beyond QA, feedback-driven active exploration, and end-to-end task evaluation, we examine whether VLMs can transform local observations into accurate and complete metric spatial representations. To this end, we introduce SpatialScout , a benchmark for evaluating the spatial intelligence of vision-language agents through active object-level 3D mapping. Starting from an initial observation and a target inventory, an agent autonomously explores the scene and progressively constructs a 3D map of target object centers in a common coordinate frame. The benchmark has three core features: \scriptsize1⃝ Explicit spatial construction. Models must output a set of 3D object positions, enabling quantitative evaluation of spatial representation quality through localization accuracy and target coverage. \scriptsize2⃝ Closed-loop active exploration. Models select subsequent viewpoints based on existing observations and the current map, then update position estimates using new evidence, forming a feedback loop between the active acquisition of perceptual evidence and spatial understanding. \scriptsize3⃝ Long-horizon end-to-end capability evaluation. Models coordinate observation planning, object recognition, cross-view association, geometric estimation, and map updates across multiple interaction steps, with overall performance measured by final map quality. This sustained coordination goes beyond QA tasks with short reasoning chains and better reflects real-world spatial understanding. ## 2 SpatialScout Construction
SpatialScout evaluates active object-center mapping by VLM agents. Given an object inventory and calibrated camera poses, an agent selects RGB views, associates objects across observations, and maintains a three-dimensional map of their centers. The benchmark combines a fixed scene catalog, a shared interaction interface, and object-level evaluation to measure the accuracy and completeness of the resulting map (Fig. 3 ). 2.1 Task Definition Table 2: Shared agent interface.
