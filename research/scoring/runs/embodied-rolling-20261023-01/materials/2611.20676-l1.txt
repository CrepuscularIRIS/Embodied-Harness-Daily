# Learning and Verifying Progress: Closed-Loop Agentic Planning with Progress-Enhanced Execution for Long-Horizon Vision-Language Navigation

## Abstract
Long-horizon vision-language navigation (VLN) requires agents to track instruction progress and recover from local errors before they propagate across subtasks. However, executors trained on expert trajectories encounter policy-induced state shifts during deployment, and their stop signals do not reliably indicate subtask completion. We introduce Learning and Verifying Progress for VLN (LeaP-VLN), an agentic VLN system that couples progress-aware execution with independent progress verification through plan--execute--verify--recover cycles. LeaP-VLN leverages progress both explicitly, through a progress verifier that verifies subtask completion before the agent advances and triggers dual-phase recovery upon route deviation, and implicitly, by co-training the executor on progress reasoning and action prediction to enhance the executor's progress awareness and corrective control. To adapt the executor to this closed-loop interface, we propose a two-stage progress-enhanced curriculum: behavior cloning on expert demonstrations, followed by joint training on demonstrations, expert action corrections, and progress-reasoning supervision. Policy-generated trajectories expose the executor to both intra-subtask drift and inter-subtask state shifts, aligning training with the deviations encountered during closed-loop execution. Extensive experiments on R2R-CE and RxR-CE demonstrate state-of-the-art success rates among monocular methods trained without additional navigation datasets, while real-world experiments further validate the effectiveness of LeaP-VLN in long-horizon navigation.

## I Introduction
Vision-language navigation (VLN) requires an embodied agent to follow a natural-language route instruction in a previously unseen environment [ 1 , 2 , 3 ] . In long-horizon settings, instructions specify sequences of landmark transitions and spatial relations. The agent must therefore predict appropriate actions while inferring from evolving egocentric observations which parts of the instruction have been completed and what remains to be executed. Moreover, local execution errors or instruction misinterpretations can compound across successive subtasks, causing increasing drift from the intended route and making subsequent stages harder to complete. Reliable long-horizon VLN requires progress tracking, accurate subtask execution, and recovery from local failures. Existing VLN approaches differ in how they organize instruction interpretation and action execution, as illustrated in Fig. 1 . End-to-end policies built on vision-language models (VLMs) map the complete route instruction, the visual history, and the current observation directly to actions [ 4 , 5 , 6 ] . Route understanding, progress tracking, and action prediction are integrated into a single policy, leaving instruction progress implicit and subtask transitions difficult to control explicitly. Hierarchical planner–executor systems separate high-level route reasoning from low-level control: a planner specifies target locations, waypoints, local instructions, or modular skills, and an executor carries out the corresponding navigation actions [ 7 , 8 , 9 , 10 ] . This separation makes intermediate navigation goals explicit, but reliable execution still depends on the executor’s ability to handle policy-induced deviations and the planner’s ability to assess subtask outcomes. Two challenges arise at this interface. First, executors trained on expert demonstrations face policy-induced state shifts during closed-loop execution, including intra-subtask drift and inter-subtask boundary shifts. Second, unverified subtask transitions can propagate local failures into inaccurate route-progress estimates. Existing recovery strategies either replan from a potentially deviated state [ 11 , 9 ] or backtrack without correcting instruction grounding [ 12 ] . Reliable execution therefore requires handling both intra-subtask drift and inter-subtask state shifts, verifying subtask outcomes, and coupling state recovery with instruction re-grounding. To address these gaps, we introduce LeaP-VLN, a closed-loop agentic VLN system that exploits subtask progress on both sides of the planner–executor interface (Fig. 1 , bottom). On the planner side, progress is handled explicitly. A high-level multimodal agent decomposes the route instruction into local subtasks and orchestrates plan–execute–verify–recover cycles. An independent verifier assesses subtask completion and route alignment using the observed execution history. The agent advances only after completion is verified, and the corresponding terminal state is stored as a trusted recovery anchor. Upon route deviation, dual-phase recovery restores a trusted execution context and re-grounds the pending subtask for a refined retry. On the executor side, progress awareness is learned implicitly by co-training the executor on progress reasoning and action prediction. We propose a two-stage progress-enhanced curriculum that first initializes the executor through behavior cloning on expert demonstrations, then co-trains it using these demonstrations and expert-corrected intra- and inter-subtask policy rollouts. Learning to reason about progress content improves action prediction while promoting progress–action consistency, aligning the executor’s decisions with the internal subtask progress. Our contributions are summarized as follows. • We introduce LeaP-VLN, a closed-loop agentic navigation system for long-horizon VLN that integrates progress-aware execution, progress verification, and dual-phase recovery to maintain route alignment and limit error propagation across subtasks. • We develop a two-stage progress-enhanced curriculum for training a low-level VLN executor. By co-training action prediction and progress reasoning on expert demonstrations and policy-generated trajectories, the curriculum trains the executor to handle intra-subtask drift and inter-subtask state shifts during closed-loop execution. • We achieve state-of-the-art success rates on R2R-CE and RxR-CE among monocular methods trained without additional navigation datasets. Component ablations evaluate the contributions of agentic planning, progress-enhanced training, and test-time verification and recovery, while real-world experiments further validate the effectiveness of LeaP-VLN in long-horizon navigation.

## III Problem Formulation
We consider VLN in continuous environments (VLN-CE), where an agent receives a natural-language instruction I at the beginning of an episode. At each step t , the agent observes an egocentric RGB image o_{t} and executes a low-level action a_{t}\in\{\texttt{FORWARD},\allowbreak\texttt{TURN\_LEFT},\allowbreak\texttt{TURN\_RIGHT},\allowbreak\texttt{STOP}\} ( 0.25\,\mathrm{m} forward, 15^{\circ} turns). The objective is to reach the destination specified by I . We adopt a hierarchical formulation in which a high-level agent \mathcal{G} decomposes I into an ordered sequence of initial local sub-instructions, \mathbf{S}^{(0)}=\mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{dec}}(I)=(S_{1}^{(0)},\ldots,S_{N}^{(0)}), (1) where \mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{dec}} denotes the agent’s instruction-decomposition operation, S_{i}^{(0)} specifies the initial instruction for the i -th subtask, and N is the number of subtasks. We use S_{i} to denote the active local instruction dispatched to the executor, which may be adjusted from S_{i}^{(0)} based on verified execution progress. A low-level VLN executor \pi_{\theta} predicts actions conditioned on the active sub-instruction S_{i} , the visual history H_{t} , and the current observation o_{t} , a_{t}\sim\pi_{\theta}(\cdot\mid S_{i},H_{t},o_{t}). (2) The executor’s STOP signal triggers high-level progress verification, which determines whether to advance to the next subtask, continue the current subtask, or initiate recovery. The following sections detail the subtask planning, executor training, verification, and recovery mechanisms.
