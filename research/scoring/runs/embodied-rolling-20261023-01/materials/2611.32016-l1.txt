===ABSTRACT===
Agentic harnesses enable frontier vision-language models (VLMs) to manipulate robots through perception, planning, and control tools, yet achieving comparable performance with compact open-weight VLMs remains challenging. We present RoboHarness-RL, an agentic reinforcement learning framework for improving VLM-based robot manipulation through tool calls. Starting from supervised fine-tuning on successful teacher demonstrations, the VLM further improves through multi-turn reinforcement learning with task-outcome rewards, without relying on vision-language-action models or world action models. To support efficient multi-turn training, we develop LiteHarness, which uses hierarchical memory to keep interaction contexts compact while preserving task performance. With Qwen3.5-9B, RL raises success from 31.2% to 44.0% on RoboTwin 2.0 and from 57.8% to 73.3% on LIBERO-Pro; these gains extend to randomized RoboTwin scenes unseen during training (26.4% to 38.0%). Behavioral analysis further shows that RL reduces repetitive-call rates by up to $4\times$, shortens successful executions, and improves recovery from negative feedback. ===INTRO===
Agentic harnesses let vision-language models (VLMs) manipulate robots through tool calls for perception, planning, and control [ 28 , 61 , 6 ] . Robot systems have traditionally separated a slow model that reasons about the task (System 2) from a fast learned policy that produces motor actions (System 1). In a harness, this boundary blurs: the VLM not only decides what to do next, but also specifies end-effector poses and gripper commands, so its decisions directly shape how the robot moves. Yet achieving comparable performance with compact open-weight VLMs remains challenging. On randomized RoboTwin 2.0 scenes, the ORA harness reaches 69.2% success with DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash but only 6.8% with the open-weight Qwen3.5-9B (Sec. 4.3 ). Approaches to improving compact VLM agents include refining the harness [ 53 , 12 ] or distilling frontier models through supervised learning [ 28 , 6 ] . Building on these advances, we investigate whether reinforcement learning from the model’s own interactions can further improve manipulation performance beyond supervised imitation. Reinforcement learning (RL) has improved vision-language-action models (VLAs) [ 5 , 49 , 27 ] and world action models (WAMs) [ 41 , 62 ] beyond imitation. In parallel, agentic RL trains language models through multi-turn tool use in digital environments such as web search [ 22 ] and graphical interfaces [ 50 ] . Harness-based manipulation lies between the two (Fig. 2 ): the VLM uses high-level tool calls as in agentic RL, while controlling physical robot actions. Concurrent approaches include RL with a learned VLA executor [ 35 ] and single-turn RL for control-program generation [ 10 ] . We instead train the VLM to specify robot actions itself through multi-turn tool calls. Two challenges make RL costly in this setting. First, every model call carries a growing interaction history, including camera images, across episodes of up to 200 calls, increasing the cost of both rollout collection and policy updates. Second, tasks that the current policy consistently solves or fails may provide little group-relative learning signal, yet still consume rollout budget. We address both with RoboHarness-RL , an agentic RL framework that trains VLMs to manipulate through tool calls, with no VLA or WAM generating actions. After fine-tuning on teacher demonstrations, the VLM learns through multi-turn GRPO with task-outcome rewards. Interaction runs through LiteHarness , whose hierarchical memory keeps recent interactions in detail, summarizes older actions, and records failures separately. Compared with compressed full history, it roughly halves the prompt tokens per call while achieving higher success. Adaptive scheduling postpones solved tasks, removes persistently failed ones, and allocates more rollouts to tasks with a learning signal. With Qwen3.5-9B [ 42 ] , RL raises success from 31.2% after SFT to 44.0% on RoboTwin 2.0 [ 4 ] and from 57.8% to 73.3% on LIBERO-Pro. Although trained only in clean scenes, the policy also improves from 26.4% to 38.0% in randomized RoboTwin scenes. RL also succeeds on tasks that SFT fails to solve in evaluation. Behavioral analysis shows how RL changes the way the model uses the harness: on RoboTwin, repetitive-call rates drop from about 10% to under 3%, jointly successful episodes require about a third fewer model calls, and the policy more often recovers to complete the task after execution failures (Fig. 1 c). Our main contributions are as follows: • We propose RoboHarness-RL , an agentic RL framework that trains VLMs to manipulate robots through multi-turn tool calls without relying on VLAs or WAMs. • We design LiteHarness and adaptive task scheduling for efficient multi-turn RL. Hierarchical memory roughly halves prompt tokens per call and improves success. • We show that RL improves over supervised imitation by 12.8 and 15.5 percentage points on RoboTwin 2.0 and LIBERO-Pro, respectively. These gains extend to randomized scenes unseen during training. RL also reduces repetitive tool calls, shortens successful executions, and improves recovery after negative feedback.
