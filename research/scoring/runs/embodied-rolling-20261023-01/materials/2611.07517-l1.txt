# Embodied RAG++: Retrieval-Augmented Generation From What Exists to What Works in Embodied Scenes

## Abstract
To serve a request in a human environment, a robot system must find an object that satisfies a functional requirement and a robot that can feasibly reach it. Scene graphs record which objects exist and where, but they often omit capabilities documented only in product manuals. Retrieval-augmented generation methods can read such manuals, yet they rarely reason jointly over object capabilities, physical instances, robot states, and dynamic connectivity. We introduce E2W-Bench, the Exists-to-Works Benchmark, which links dynamic hybrid scene graphs with real product manuals to evaluate capability-grounded robot--object assignment under availability, mobility, vertical-reach, and connector constraints. We also present Embodied RAG++, a spatially grounded retrieval framework that scopes retrieval to the manuals linked to candidate objects, caches extracted capabilities per product model, and resolves robot--object feasibility through deterministic spatial reasoning. On E2W-Bench, Embodied RAG++ reaches a pair accuracy of 0.90 with Qwen2.5-32B and 1.00 with Llama3.3-70B, compared with at most 0.30 and 0.55 for the baselines, while requiring the shortest preprocessing time among the retrieval-based methods and tying for the lowest total token count.

## I Introduction
Robots operating in human environments need more than knowledge of which objects exist and where they are located. To serve a request, a system has to find which physical object can satisfy the user’s functional requirement and which available robot can feasibly reach it. Consider a request to heat a 27 L food container to 240°C. A scene graph may contain several microwave ovens, but the required cavity capacity and convection temperature are specified only in their product manuals. Even after identifying a suitable appliance, the system has to check whether an available robot can reach it under the current mobility and connectivity conditions. If an elevator becomes unavailable, for example, a wheeled robot may be unable to access another floor, whereas a legged robot may use the stairs. Resolving such a request therefore requires combining capabilities documented in manuals with the current state of the objects, robots, and connectors in the scene. Existing work addresses parts of this problem but not their combination. 3D scene graphs [ 1 , 2 ] , open-vocabulary maps [ 3 ] , and hierarchical planners [ 4 ] model spatial layouts and navigation paths, while benchmarks such as ScanRefer [ 5 ] and SQA3D [ 6 ] evaluate visual language grounding. These representations capture what exists and where, but not the non-visual functional attributes stored in external documentation. Graph-structured retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) [ 7 ] frameworks such as GraphRAG [ 8 ] , RAPTOR [ 9 ] , and HippoRAG [ 10 ] , for their part, retrieve document knowledge but return textual answers that are not tied to physical instances and ignore reachability, robot availability, and changing scene state. EmbodiedRAG [ 11 ] introduces spatial retrieval over observational memory, but its knowledge is limited to what the agent has perceived, not to external manuals. In addition, processing multi-page manuals anew for every query and every physical instance repeats the same work whenever several instances share a product model. We introduce E2W-Bench (Exists-to-Works Benchmark), a benchmark for capability-grounded robot–object assignment over dynamic hybrid scene graphs. E2W-Bench connects physical object instances with product manuals and unifies robots, spatial connectors, and dynamic environmental states within a single graph. Each query has a machine-verified unique ground-truth pair, so a single exact-match check evaluates document reasoning and spatial allocation together. We also propose Embodied RAG++ , a spatially grounded retrieval framework that couples document-grounded capability inference with deterministic feasibility verification (Fig. 1 ). Embodied RAG++ uses the scene graph’s object-to-manual links to restrict retrieval to the manuals of candidate objects. It resolves reachability, mobility, and connectivity constraints with deterministic spatial reasoning, which requires no large language model (LLM) calls. To avoid reprocessing the same manuals, Embodied RAG++ stores extracted facts in Capability Cards , memory units keyed by product model that grow incrementally as queries request new fields. Every physical instance of a model and every later query that needs the same field reuses the card. Our contributions are as follows: • E2W-Bench , which links dynamic hybrid scene graphs to real product manuals and gives every query a machine-verified unique ground-truth pair (§ III ). • Embodied RAG++ , which combines manual-scoped, aspect-split retrieval, model-level Capability Cards, availability filtering, and deterministic spatial reasoning (§ IV ). • An evaluation with three LLM backbones and four baselines, in which Embodied RAG++ reaches a pair accuracy of 0.90 with Qwen2.5-32B and 1.00 with Llama3.3-70B, compared with at most 0.30 and 0.55 for the baselines, with the shortest preprocessing time among the retrieval-based methods and the lowest total token count, tied with EmbodiedRAG (§ V ).

## III E2W-Bench
III-A Task Formulation We formulate E2W-Bench as a robot–object pair selection problem over a dynamic hybrid scene graph with four node/edge types: appliance nodes (category, model, a link to that model’s real product manual, availability, position); robot nodes (mobility profile, availability, position); a spatial hierarchy of scene \rightarrow floor \rightarrow room \rightarrow object/robot; and connector edges (stairs, elevators, doors) carrying a traversal cost, an accessibility list of robot types, and a status that can change between queries. Fig. 2 shows one such graph in full. Its node and edge states are not fixed at construction time; they track the physical scene and change as a robot moves or is dispatched, an appliance is switched on or taken out of service, or a connector’s status changes. These state updates make the graph dynamic rather than a static record of object positions and labels. Given the graph’s current state and a natural-language query, a system returns a robot identifier, a target-object identifier, and both of their positions. A returned pair is correct only if four independent conditions hold: (i) capability : the target’s real manual establishes that it meets the query’s functional requirement; (ii) availability : that instance is not in_use or out_of_order ; (iii) mobility : a candidate robot can reach the target’s floor given the current connector states and its own accessibility profile; and (iv) vertical reach : that robot’s body or arm can physically reach the object’s mounted height. III-B Archetype-Based Generation We generate benchmark scenes in the MuJoCo simulator using three robot platforms: a quadruped, a humanoid, and a wheeled mobile manipulator equipped with an AgileX Piper arm. The scenes include appliance 3D models reconstructed from real objects and their corresponding product manuals. Fig. 3 summarizes the 20 released scenes and the underlying manual corpus. Each scene is a verified snapshot of the dynamic graph defined in § III-A : it fixes robot positions, appliance availability, and connector states, the same fields a deployed system updates online, rather than replaying continuous physical dynamics. Each scene instantiates one of 10 hand-designed query archetypes , with two archetypes for each of five appliance categories: microwave, rice cooker, washing machine, water dispenser, and bread maker. Each archetype has two scene variations, yielding 20 scenes in total. A threshold archetype requires the target to satisfy a compound predicate, such as cavity capacity \geq 27 L and bake temperature \geq 240 °C, while every listed distractor fails at least one condition. An extremum archetype requires all candidates to satisfy a baseline predicate, with the target minimizing or maximizing a specified field. Before scene generation, we manually extract the relevant capability values from the 13 product manuals covering these five categories (Fig. 3 b) and verify each predicate against the resulting reference table, so every scene agrees with its source manuals. The reference table is used only to construct and validate the ground truth; at inference time, Embodied RAG++ derives its Capability Cards directly from the manuals (§ IV-B ). Each scene contains the qualifying appliance, all distractors specified by the archetype, and several unrelated manual-linked appliances and furniture items.
