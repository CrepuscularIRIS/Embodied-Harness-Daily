# TinyAstral: Transferring Self-Evolving Harnesses to Tiny Generalist Manipulation Agents

## Abstract
Robot agents built on vision-language models depend on both the planner and its harness: the tools, feedback, memory, and recovery logic around it. Can improvements to a large teacher's harness be carried into a compact agent with lower inference compute cost? We present TinyAstral, a two-stage approach. We first freeze an open-weight teacher and improve its harness through recursive self-improvement (RSI): frontier LLM agents read the teacher's trajectories and propose harness edits, and each edit is accepted only if it passes a paired statistical test. We then freeze the final harness, collect teacher trajectories with reasoning and failure recovery, and distill a small student that replaces the teacher in the same harness. On RoboTwin, harness evolution raises the teacher's success from 33.4% to 63.6%; the final 4B student reaches 62.8%. Across four harness versions, distilled 4B students score close to their corresponding teachers, and later versions yield stronger students, showing that harness evolution gains carry into compact models. The harness gains also transfer to other frozen planners without retraining. On identical episodes, the final 4B student outperforms open 4B agents, the open 27B harness-RSI model SenseNova-RoboRSI, and our own teacher model placed in three competitors' harnesses. It stays close to its teacher on all three training benchmarks, and on three benchmarks never used for RSI or training it scores close to a 552B planner running the same harness. On the measured deployment episodes, it uses about one fifth of its teacher's accelerator time.

## 1 Introduction
Vision–language models (VLMs) can operate robot arms by reading images and calling perception and motion tools [ 25 , 18 , 11 , 12 ] . The model chooses the object, action sequence, and stopping point; the surrounding harness grounds these choices, executes them, and reports what happened. Tool interfaces, feedback, task memory, and failure recovery therefore contribute to the agent’s performance alongside the model itself [ 57 , 40 , 46 , 10 ] . Two lines of work offer complementary ways to build these agents. Harness evolution improves the system around a frozen planner [ 40 , 46 , 10 ] ; agent distillation trains compact models from a stronger agent’s trajectories [ 27 , 24 , 56 ] . We connect these approaches by asking: can we first improve a large teacher’s harness, then transfer the resulting decision-making capability to a small model? This would allow the improved system to run with substantially less inference compute. We present TinyAstral , a family of 0.8B–4B manipulation agents built in two stages ( Fig. 2 ). First, we freeze the weights of an open-weight teacher, Qwen3.8-Flash-Next [ 34 ] , and improve its harness through a master-guided recursive self-improvement (RSI) loop. Humans specify the rules and budget in advance. Frontier LLM agents then inspect failures and propose harness edits, which the system automatically accepts or rejects according to pre-registered paired statistical tests. Second, we freeze the final harness and collect teacher trajectories containing reasoning, tool calls, and recovery from injected failures. Together with auxiliary offline real-robot supervision, these trajectories train a compact student to interpret feedback, choose actions, recover from failure, and decide when to finish. At deployment, the student replaces the teacher in the same frozen harness. We evaluate both stages and the connection between them. Harness evolution raises the frozen teacher’s RoboTwin success from 33.4% to 63.6%, and distillation produces a 4B student at 62.8%. To test whether this connection holds beyond the final endpoint, we distill students at four harness versions. The four students score 36.2%, 49.8%, 60.0%, and 62.8%, each close to its corresponding teacher. The same evolved harness also improves DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash and the untrained 4B initialization, showing that its gains are reusable across planners. On identical episodes the student outperforms every open competitor we ran, including our own teacher model inside three competitors’ harnesses (93.6% vs. 78.9% on Guava; 78.3% vs. 57.3% and 53.0% on LIBERO-PRO), and on unseen benchmarks the small 4B student scores close to a 552B planner in the same harness. Our contributions are: • Harness evolution followed by distillation. A two-stage approach first improves a frozen teacher’s manipulation harness, then trains a compact planner from that teacher’s reasoning and recovery trajectories for deployment in the same harness. • Evidence that evolution gains transfer. The harness improves other frozen planners, and distilled students at four harness versions track the performance of their corresponding teachers, showing that system improvements carry into compact models. • Compact agents with lower inference compute cost. A single TinyAstral-4B model covers six benchmarks: on identical episodes it outperforms every open competitor we ran, including our own teacher model inside three competitors’ harnesses, scores close to its teacher on the three training benchmarks and close to a 552B planner on unseen ones, and uses about one fifth of the teacher’s accelerator time per episode in our deployment measurement.

## 3 Method
TinyAstral has two stages ( Fig. 2 ). In the first, a master improves the harness of a frozen teacher through a statistically gated loop ( Sec. 3.2 ). Once evolution ends, we freeze the final harness, collect teacher trajectories through it, and distill the resulting reasoning and recovery behavior into a compact student ( Sec. 3.3 ). At deployment, the student replaces the teacher in the same harness. We first define the agent and its interface ( Sec. 3.1 ). 3.1 Planner, harness, and scoring Agent and harness. A planner model \pi controls a robot through a harness H . At turn t the harness gives \pi an observation o_{t} , and \pi produces a reasoning trace followed by exactly one tool call a_{t} . The harness executes the call and returns a structured result r_{t} . The harness determines how an action is carried out: perception (SAM 3 segmentation [ 5 ] lifted to depth point clouds), grasp and placement geometry, collision-free motion planning, and closed-loop corrections. The planner determines what to do: which object, which part of it, which reference and spatial relation, which arm, in what order, and when the task is finished. The model never outputs joint angles or coordinates unless a tool explicitly requests them. Observations combine camera images, the task instruction, recent tool results, and gripper state. Object-level tools provide grasping, placement, verification, and recovery primitives; the full interface is specified in Appendix G . Autonomous termination and scoring. The agent ends an episode itself by calling done with the predicates it believes hold ( e.g . on(a,b) , upright(a) , lifted(a,cm) ). The harness checks these predicates with the cameras and gripper sensors; if a check fails, it rejects the call and returns the measurement so the agent can keep working. After two rejections, the next done ends the episode without a check. An episode also ends if the agent calls give_up or exhausts its budget (30 tool calls on RoboTwin; per-benchmark limits in Appendix B ). The environment’s success flag never enters the model’s input. Our primary metric, strict success S , counts an episode only if the agent ended it with done and the benchmark’s success predicate holds in the final state. We also report ever success, which counts an episode if the task was achieved after any tool call. 3.2 Stage 1: Evolving the teacher harness The self-improving system consists of the frozen teacher \pi_{T} , the harness H , and the master M . Only H changes. The weights of \pi_{T} stay fixed throughout, and M does not act in the environment. Humans specify the rules (including the pre-registered acceptance criteria) and budget before the loop begins. The master is a set of frontier LLM agents that read trajectories, propose edits, implement them, and audit one another’s code. The system applies the pre-registered paired statistical tests to determine acceptance. Each round runs the current teacher, analyzes its failures, proposes edits, and tests a candidate against the current harness. 1. Read trajectories. The current accepted harness H_{k} is run on development seeds.
