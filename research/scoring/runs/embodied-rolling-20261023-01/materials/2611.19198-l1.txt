# Self-Calibrating Vision-Agent-Action: Deploying Pick-and-Place to New Robot Cells without Demonstrations or Fine-Tuning

## Abstract
In simulation, Vision-Agent-Action (VAA) performs pick-and-place in robot cells with gripper or base-frame changes without robot demonstrations or fine-tuning. It uses pre-trained vision-language and language models for interpretation, measures the command map and camera geometry from its own motion and fingertips, estimates lens distortion, and plans grasps from measured object depth. A controlled study of eight public LIBERO-fine-tuned vision-language-action models (VLAs) varies robot hardware, camera geometry, appearance, and base frame. Gripper and frame changes dominate their losses in the tested fractional design. Across 18 cells with these changes, frozen VAA achieves 230/360 successes (64%), the refined VAA 268/360 (74%), and the strongest released VLA on this subset 76/360 (21%). Paired ablations support the self-calibration bundle, lens estimation under compounded changes, and the measured-depth pipeline. On a two-step LIBERO-Goal chain with object state carried between instructions, VAA completes 54/60 chains in changed cells, while four evaluated VLAs complete none.

## I Introduction
Moving a manipulation system to another robot cell changes more than its images. A different gripper changes fingertip geometry and object contact. A rotated command frame changes the direction in which an action moves the tool center point (TCP). Camera pose, lens, lighting, support surfaces, and objects may also change. Collecting demonstrations and fine-tuning a policy for each cell adds deployment work. Public vision-language-action models (VLAs) fine-tuned on LIBERO can perform tasks in their training setup. Transfer to a new cell also requires their commands to produce the intended physical motion under changed hardware and coordinate frames. Fig. 1 illustrates this deployment problem. We first examine where released VLAs lose success in a controlled cell design. Gripper and base-frame changes dominate their losses in the tested fraction. Both changes alter the relation between commands and fingertip or object motion. This diagnosis motivates measuring the new cell’s geometry before manipulation. Vision-Agent-Action (VAA) performs this measurement and executes pick-and-place without robot demonstrations or fine-tuning. It combines pre-trained VLM/LLM components with self-calibration. Before the task, the robot observes its own motion and fingertips to establish a cell model. During the task, language models identify the relevant objects, while geometric modules determine where to grasp and where the TCP must move. Fig. 2 separates these stages. The study contributes: • A controlled diagnosis identifying gripper and command-frame changes as dominant VLA transfer factors in the tested design. • A self-calibrating pipeline combining command and camera measurement, lens estimation, and measured-depth grasp planning. • Paired ablations and two-step manipulation testing the roles of cell measurement and calibration reuse.

## III Controlled Study: Where Do VLAs Break?
III-A Tasks, Factors, and Baselines All experiments are in simulation. We use LIBERO-spatial [ 11 ] , with 10 tasks and 2 initial states per task, giving 20 episodes per cell. Every method receives the same episodes. The controlled study contains 30 changed design cells. The factors are arm (A: Panda to UR5e), gripper (B: Panda to Hand-E), camera pose (C: moved 10 cm and rotated 10^{\circ} ), lens (D: k_{1}=0.25 ), light (E: dim warm illumination), table (F: blue cloth), objects (G: red bowl and blue plate), and base frame (H: 45^{\circ} rotation). Single-factor cells include additional levels: RB5 and Doosan arms; PGC, 2FG7, and Schunk grippers; a high camera; and a 90^{\circ} frame rotation. Combined changes follow a 2^{8-4} resolution-IV fraction: E=BCD,\quad F=ACD,\quad G=ABC,\quad H=ABD. (1) The fraction contains 15 changed cells and a nominal reference. Changed-cell totals exclude the nominal reference. In a resolution-IV fraction, main effects are not aliased with two-factor interactions, but their interpretation assumes negligible higher-order interactions; two-factor interactions remain aliased with one another. Five additional changed development cells provide whole-cell stress tests. The baselines are X-VLA, \pi 0.5, \pi 0, GR00T N1.7, OpenVLA, SmolVLA, \tau 0, and VLA-JEPA [ 7 , 5 , 4 , 9 , 3 , 6 , 10 , 8 ] . We evaluate each released system with its own model, training, action interface, and authors’ inference stack. VAA uses a third-person RGB-D camera and proprioception; the VLAs receive RGB images without measured depth. The RGB-only VAA ablation examines this sensor difference. III-B Evaluation Protocol Each baseline uses one seed per evaluation run. Reference runs of the same setup yield 14/20 and 19/20 successes for OpenVLA, and 16/20 and 12/20 for SmolVLA. Frozen VAA was fixed before the design-cell evaluations. Execution refinements in VAA were selected using the training setup and development cells, with frozen design outcomes available. Both versions are reported on the gripper/frame subset selected from the factor diagnosis. For the chain evaluation, VAA was not developed on LIBERO-Goal, and the chain comprises the two most reliable pick-and-place steps in its shared scene. III-C Factor Diagnosis Fig. 3 identifies gripper and base-frame changes as the dominant VLA losses in this fraction, and Fig. 4 shows one rollout per cell for the same task and initial state. The remaining evaluation focuses on cells that change the gripper or rotate the base frame. In cells that change neither the gripper nor the frame, several released VLAs retain high aggregate success, with performance varying by model. Both dominant factors change the relation between a command and the resulting motion of the fingers or object. Measuring that relation through controlled motion provides the cell-specific information used by VAA.

## IV Vision-Agent-Action
IV-A Self-Calibration of the Cell Model VAA measures command effects, jaw behavior, and camera geometry before task execution (Figs. 2 and 5 ). These measurements replace assumptions inherited from the training setup. IV-A 1 Command map and jaw behavior The robot commands small motions through the available action channels and measures the resulting TCP displacement: \Delta\mathbf{x}_{\mathrm{TCP}}\approx\mathbf{J}\Delta\mathbf{u}, (2) where \Delta\mathbf{u} is an action-channel command and \mathbf{J} is its measured local motion map. In the figures, command channels 0–2 are assigned to base +x / +y / +z directions through \mathbf{J} . The controller uses this map to interpret desired TCP displacements in the current cell. VAA also measures open and closed fingertip widths and the settling time after a gripper command. The usable opening constrains grasp selection, while settling time determines when to observe the commanded jaw state. The cell model uses parallel-jaw closure. IV-A 2 Camera and lens geometry The robot observes its fingertips at multiple poses and associates these observations with its motion and proprioception to estimate camera intrinsics \mathbf{K} (focal length and principal point) and pose relative to the robot base [\mathbf{R}\,|\,\mathbf{t}] . The resulting geometry maps base-frame coordinates to image pixels. VAA estimates radial distortion k_{1} using table flatness as a constraint. Geometric errors from a changed lens can compound with camera and hardware changes even when the object remains recognizable. VAA also uses workspace exploration views, a table-height touch check, and real-time re-reading. Section VI evaluates these procedures separately. IV-B Grounding and Depth-Based Grasp Planning RGB-D observations provide object proposals. A pre-trained VLM (Qwen3.8, 27B) labels the proposals, and a pre-trained text LLM (Qwen3, 30B instruct) interprets the instruction to select a target and destination; both run locally without fine-tuning. A geometric resolver and size checks connect those semantic choices to measured scene entities. The grasp planner uses the target’s own three-dimensional points to choose where the fingers should approach and close. The measured jaw opening constrains feasible grasp geometry, and the cell model connects the selected grasp to a TCP goal. IV-C Execution and Verification A closed-loop servo executes motion through the calibrated command map. Verification checks whether placement succeeded. After an unsuccessful attempt, VAA diagnoses the observed outcome and can retry. After initial calibration, a short drift check determines whether to reuse the cell model or recalibrate, separating cell preparation from execution of each instruction.
