# MoE-DAgger: Transition-Aware Router-Entropy Gating for Selective Human Correction in Phase-Structured Manipulation

## Abstract
Selective data acquisition in Dataset Aggregation (DAgger) on robots requires deciding which learner rollouts to correct and when to intervene. In manipulation with ordered phases, router entropy from a phase-supervised soft mixture-of-experts (MoE) is insufficient: the same value can reflect either a normal adjacent-phase handoff or support for nonadjacent or earlier phases. We propose MoE-DAgger, which uses causal router history to distinguish these cases without providing the policy with a phase label, progress variable, or hard phase command during execution. The acquisition rule allows an adjacent transition to settle, but requests correction when high entropy persists outside such a transition, the transition times out, or routing persistently favors an earlier phase. Under matched active human-control budgets, MoE-DAgger achieves 84.0% task success on Green and 78.0% on Red, exceeding phase-unaware filtered entropy by 22 percentage points on Green and 26 points on Red, with no observed collisions in 50 rollouts per task. On 50 shared intervention-free traces per task, 83.3% and 80.0% of its first proposals fall on failed rollouts within predefined failure windows when 15 and 25 traces are selected, compared with 70.0% and 72.0% for temporal action-chunk disagreement and 63.3% and 60.0% for ensemble disagreement. Shadow replay and an active no-veto ablation indicate that suppressing queries at normal adjacent handoffs is a key contributor to the improved allocation.

## I Introduction
Visuomotor behavior cloning can reproduce long-horizon manipulation from demonstrations [ 1 , 2 ] , but compounding errors can move a deployed policy beyond the demonstrated state distribution. DAgger addresses this shift by adding expert supervision on learner-visited states [ 3 ] ; with intermittent human takeover, however, monitoring and corrective control are costly in real-world interactive systems [ 4 , 5 , 6 , 7 ] . Under a fixed budget of active human control, acquisition must choose both which rollout to correct and when to take over. Rollout-level selection alone is insufficient: a proposal after collision onset is too late, whereas one at a benign handoff prevents autonomy from reaching later interaction states. We therefore evaluate selective acquisition by rollout selectivity, failure-mode-specific critical-window alignment, and burden on successful rollouts. Our demonstrations follow a known ordered semantic phase graph and supervise phase-anchored ACT-style action heads within a soft MoE [ 8 , 9 ] . Each head predicts a complete Action Chunking with Transformers (ACT) [ 1 ] chunk rather than a hand-authored skill or finite-state mode. During execution, routing remains soft and observation-driven; phase topology is used only by the acquisition rule. Consequently, the same scalar entropy can describe either a normal current-to-successor transition or support for nonadjacent or earlier phases ( Figure 1 ). MoE-DAgger uses only causal router history to track legal current-to-successor support and temporal settling. It suppresses queries at bounded compatible handoffs and triggers on persistent non-benign ambiguity, transition timeout, or regression. The phase graph is therefore an acquisition prior, not a runtime action-routing constraint. We evaluate (i) downstream utility from independently acquired correction sets under matched active human-control time and (ii) count-matched first-proposal allocation on the same 50 Green and 50 Red intervention-free traces. The latter credits a failed rollout only when its first proposal lies in the corresponding fixed failure-mode-specific critical window. Phase-unaware filtered-H is the primary same-signal mechanism ablation, while ACT-Temporal-Disagreement and Ensemble-Disagreement are the two principal phase-unaware action-space baselines. Across the two tasks, MoE-DAgger improves task success over the same-signal gate by 22/26 percentage points and achieves the highest aligned yield in all four task–budget blocks. Shadow replay and an active no-veto test isolate the adjacent-handoff mechanism. Contributions. We (1) show that scalar router entropy does not determine transition structure in an unclamped phase-supervised soft MoE; (2) introduce a causal history-only gate that conditions persistent entropy on legal support and settling while retaining timeout and regression triggers; and (3) evaluate downstream utility, critical-window-aligned allocation, and the adjacent-veto mechanism under matched human-control time.

## III Method
III-A Problem Setup and Phase-Supervised Soft MoE For task d\in\mathcal{T} , we train a separate policy \pi_{\theta}^{d} and annotate demonstrations with the ordered semantic graph 0\!\rightarrow\!1\!\rightarrow\!\cdots\!\rightarrow\!S-1 . At runtime, the phase graph is used only by the acquisition gate, not to command the policy: no phase/progress variable or hard mode command is provided to the policy, and the router remains free to blend or revisit heads. Starting from \mathcal{D}_{0}^{d} , round i executes the learner, proposes takeover from policy-output history, records an accepted suffix \mathcal{C}_{i}^{d} , and retrains on \mathcal{D}_{i+1}^{d}=\mathcal{D}_{i}^{d}\uplus\mathcal{C}_{i}^{d} . Proposal timing is part of acquisition because an accepted takeover replaces the remainder of the autonomous rollout with human control, changing which later learner-induced states are visited and corrected. We therefore evaluate proposal timing against synchronized physical task semantics rather than treating the router’s head index as ground-truth phase. At policy step t , \mathbf{o}_{t}=(\mathbf{I}_{t-L:t}^{1:V},\mathbf{q}_{t},\dot{\mathbf{q}}_{t}) contains synchronized external/wrist views, task-relevant crops, and proprioception. In both tasks, the S=5 phase anchors are approach, entry/alignment, push, withdrawal/clearance, and home return. A shared encoder produces \mathbf{h}_{t} ; each of the K=S phase-anchored heads predicts a complete ACT chunk \widehat{\mathbf{A}}_{t}^{(k)}\in\mathbb{R}^{C\times n_{a}} , and \displaystyle\begin{aligned} p_{t,k}&=\frac{\exp(r_{k}(\mathbf{h}_{t})/T_{r})}{\sum_{j=0}^{K-1}\exp(r_{j}(\mathbf{h}_{t})/T_{r})},\quad k\in\{0,\ldots,K-1\},\\[-1.99997pt] \widehat{\mathbf{A}}_{t}&=\sum_{k=0}^{K-1}p_{t,k}\widehat{\mathbf{A}}_{t}^{(k)}.\end{aligned} (1) We temporally ensemble overlapping chunks for execution. Each supervised frame has a manually reviewed phase label z_{t} ; correction suffixes label only the phases they contain. With label-smoothed target \widetilde{\mathbf{y}}_{t} , training uses \begin{split}\mathcal{L}={}&\mathcal{L}_{\rm BC}+\lambda_{r}\operatorname{CE}(\widetilde{\mathbf{y}}_{t},\mathbf{p}_{t})+\lambda_{e}\sum_{k}\widetilde{y}_{t,k}\mathcal{L}_{\rm BC}^{(k)}\\[-1.99997pt] &+\lambda_{b}\mathcal{L}_{\rm balance}+\lambda_{s}\mathcal{L}_{\rm chunk\text{-}smooth}.\end{split} (2) Because \mathbf{p}_{t} is not projected onto the phase graph, the router remains re-entrant and backward routing remains observable to the acquisition gate. III-B Router Ambiguity and Legal Phase Support We compute normalized Shannon entropy and its causal W_{H} -step mean, \displaystyle H_{t}=-\frac{1}{\log K}\sum_{k=0}^{K-1}p_{t,k}\log p_{t,k},\quad\bar{H}_{t}=\frac{1}{W_{H}}\sum_{\ell=0}^{W_{H}-1}H_{t-\ell}, (3) with clipping only for numerical evaluation of \log p_{t,k} . Entropy is an ambiguity statistic, not a calibrated failure probability, reward, or safety certificate. Let s_{t}^{-} be the stabilized phase frozen before the tracker is updated. Legal adjacent support and off-path mass are \displaystyle\mathcal{A}(s_{t}^{-})=\begin{cases}\{s_{t}^{-},s_{t}^{-}+1\},&s_{t}^{-}<S-1,\\ \{S-1\},&s_{t}^{-}=S-1,\end{cases}\quad M_{t}=1-\sum_{\mathclap{k\in\mathcal{A}(s_{t}^{-})}}p_{t,k}. (4) Proposition 1 (entropy does not determine transition structure). A router supported on a legal pair satisfies H_{t}\leq\log 2/\log K . More generally, if the legal pair is top-2 and 0\leq M_{t}=m\leq\tau_{M}\leq(K-2)/K , maximizing entropy within the legal and off-path groups gives \displaystyle H_{\max}^{\rm veto}(m)=\frac{h_{2}(m)+(1-m)\log 2+m\log(K-2)}{\log K}, (5) where h_{2}(m)=-m\log m-(1-m)\log(1-m) . Hence entropy combines legal current–successor sharing, uncertainty about being on path, and dispersion among off-path heads. For K=5 , H_{\max}^{\rm veto}(0)=0.431 and H_{\max}^{\rm veto}(0.15)=0.731 ; thus a locally compatible handoff can exceed both deployed thresholds once allowed off-path mass is present.
