# Agentic-Teacher: An Agentic Data Engine for Failure-Driven VLA Post-Training

## Abstract
Improving a pretrained vision-language-action (VLA) model requires demonstrations that address its weaknesses and provide effective supervision for learning. Coding agents can autonomously generate demonstrations through environment interaction, yet successful execution alone does not ensure effective supervision for VLA learning. We introduce Agentic-Teacher, an autonomous data engine for failure-driven VLA post-training. Given a VLA checkpoint, a task specification, and configured environment tools, the engine analyzes failed rollouts, selects intervention states, and iteratively develops closed-loop code policies through execution feedback. Validated policies become reusable demonstration generators that collect successful continuations from states where the VLA struggles. To make these demonstrations suitable for learning, Agentic-Teacher introduces temporally correlated action noise to broaden state-action coverage, removes idle frames while preserving gripper transitions, and filters trajectories using the frozen base model's flow-matching loss. The resulting demonstrations are combined with successful VLA rollouts for post-training, yielding an updated policy that operates without coding-agent assistance. Agentic-Teacher thus connects the weaknesses of a target VLA to both the generation and curation of its training data. In simulation, it improves $π_0$ success rates from 79.4% to 87.8% on LIBERO-10, from 80.4% to 90.6% on MetaWorld-10, and from 0% to 30.0% across three RoboCasa tasks.

## 1 Introduction
During deployment, vision-language-action (VLA) policies may encounter states outside their training distribution due to environmental noise or accumulated control errors, potentially leading to task failure. DAgger addresses this distribution shift by collecting expert actions at states visited by the learned policy and aggregating them for further training ( Ross et al., 2011 ) . Obtaining this supervision from human experts requires repeated monitoring and intervention. Although HG-DAgger and ThriftyDAgger improve takeover mechanisms and intervention allocation, they still depend on human corrections ( Kelly et al., 2019 ; Hoque et al., 2022 ) , making corrective learning costly to scale across tasks and environments. Advances in multimodal understanding, code generation, and tool use have enabled coding agents to construct and refine robot behaviors through environment interaction. Code as Policies connects language-model-generated programs to perception and control interfaces ( Liang et al., 2022 ) . Within this tool-based execution paradigm, Harness VLA exposes a frozen VLA as a callable manipulation primitive alongside analytic controllers, enabling agents to coordinate learned and programmed behaviors without policy fine-tuning ( Zhang et al., 2026 ) . To refine behaviors through environmental feedback, CaP-X, Act-Observe-Rewrite, and Agent as Policy incorporate observation, execution, and program revision into iterative feedback loops ( Fu et al., 2026 ; Kumar, 2026 ; Jia et al., 2026 ) . ASPIRE and Uni-Skill further support skill construction and reuse, enabling subsequent exploration to build on accumulated experience ( Lu et al., 2026 ; Xie et al., 2026 ) . A complementary line of work uses agents to support robot policy learning. RoboClaw coordinates self-resetting rollouts to collect training data and iteratively improve VLA skill policies ( Li et al., 2026 ) , while ENPIRE enables coding agents to manage real-world policy evaluation and refine training algorithms ( Xiao et al., 2026 ) . InSight acquires missing action primitives through guided interaction and incorporates successful demonstrations into VLA training ( Wang et al., 2026 ) . Recova develops task and recovery behaviors in digital twins and refines separate policies through real-world DAgger. This motivates using coding agents to autonomously construct corrective experts for a target VLA’s observed failures, reducing reliance on human-provided corrections. Our focus is on both generating successful corrective demonstrations and adapting them into effective supervision for VLA post-training. This leads to our central question: Can coding agents autonomously identify the causal frame and reason from failure trajectories, generate successful repair demonstrations, and distill the resulting corrective behaviors back into the model? To answer this question, we introduce Agentic-Teacher , an autonomous data engine that acts as a teacher for failure-driven VLA post-training (Fig. 1 ). Rather than assuming access to a predefined expert capable of correcting every failure, Agentic-Teacher constructs localized code-policy experts on demand, targeting the specific weaknesses revealed by the VLA’s own execution. Given a VLA checkpoint, a task specification, a set of interaction tools, and a success verifier, Agentic-Teacher coordinates failure discovery, corrective policy exploration, data collection, and model evaluation within a shared execution harness. The agents analyze recurring VLA failures, use perception and control tools to synthesize and refine closed-loop code policies, and validate candidate repairs through environment interaction. The resulting successful behaviors provide corrective demonstrations for post-training the VLA. Once updated, the model is evaluated independently without assistance from agents or code policies, while any remaining failures can initiate a subsequent repair cycle. However, successful code-policy execution does not necessarily yield effective supervision for VLA learning. Program-generated trajectories may exhibit limited behavioral coverage, temporal redundancy, or poor compatibility with the base policy, making task success alone an insufficient criterion for training-data quality. We therefore introduce a trajectory adaptation pipeline that improves behavioral diversity, removes uninformative redundancy, and filters candidate trajectories according to their compatibility with the base model before supervised fine-tuning. In this way, Agentic-Teacher bridges the gap between successful agent execution and learnable corrective supervision, enabling validated repair behaviors to be transferred into the VLA itself. ## 3 Agentic-Teacher
Agentic-Teacher acts as an autonomous teacher for a pretrained VLA by generating and curating corrective demonstrations from the VLA’s own failures. Given a checkpoint and a task description, it collects rollouts, diagnoses failures, and groups episodes that require similar corrections. Coding agents then develop and test repair programs for these groups. We process the resulting trajectories and use them to fine-tune the VLA. Figure 1 shows the three stages of this process. 3.1 Rollout and Failure Diagnosis As shown in Figure 1 , VLA rollouts provide failed trajectories for diagnosis and successful trajectories, when available, as references. The agent first localizes causal frames and then clusters trajectories with similar failure modes. Causal frame localization. The agent examines the task instruction, camera observations, and action traces to identify the interaction responsible for each failure. Successful references help distinguish the expected behavior from the failed execution. The agent traces the observed failure back to its cause and selects a causal frame at or before the faulty interaction as the repair starting point. For example, a placement failure may originate in an unstable grasp earlier in the trajectory; localizing this frame allows the repair to address the grasp before attempting transport and placement. The harness reconstructs the corresponding environment state and checks it against the recorded rollout, providing a verified starting point for code-policy exploration. Failure mode cluster. The agent groups failed trajectories within each task according to their causal frames, failing stages, and underlying causes. Each group forms a failure mode (FM), such as a picking, transport, or placement failure, with supporting trajectory evidence and associated repair starting points. This organization allows a coding agent to develop and test a targeted repair across similar failures. The resulting failure-mode clusters are passed to code-policy synthesis, where each cluster guides the search for a reusable corrective program.
