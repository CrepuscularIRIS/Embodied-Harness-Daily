===ABSTRACT===
Vision-language models (VLMs) can act as closed-loop robot-use agents (RUAs) by writing and executing control code through tool interfaces. ===INTRO===

Closed-loop robot manipulation demands tight coordination between perception, planning, and control: an agent must decide how to structure control commands over time, when acquiring a fresh observation warrants another model turn, and how to recover when execution diverges from expectation. Recent robot-use agent (RUA) systems [ 15 , 46 , 7 ] demonstrate that vision-language models (VLMs) can supply this coordination directly through tool use [ 49 , 36 , 30 , 23 , 6 ] , extending the interaction paradigm of digital computer-use agents [ 1 , 38 ] to physical environments. Yet equipping an open-weight VLM with reliable closed-loop manipulation capability is not a prompting problem—it is a full-pipeline post-training problem. Off-the-shelf open VLMs fail completely under interactive control contracts (0/100 held-out tasks for an untrained 9B model); frontier models capable of solving these tasks zero-shot are often accessible only as black-box endpoints without exposed weights, hidden states, or token logits; and supervised fine-tuning (SFT) on teacher trajectories alone stalls early (14/100) because an imitated policy encounters its own execution histories, errors, and observations at test time rather than the teacher’s. To close this end-to-end gap, we build EmbodyAgent , a complete post-training stack for embodied VLM agents organized as three successive pipelines: 1. Black-box strong-teacher data synthesis. A frontier VLM teacher interacts with the environment strictly as a black-box agent through the tool harness—requiring zero access to teacher weights or logits. Our synthesis pipeline enforces structured reasoning before every code output and filters episodes by environment success predicates, turning raw black-box rollouts into clean multimodal training trajectories. 2. Multimodal SFT distillation. An open-weight 9B VLM student is distilled on the synthesized explanation-and-program targets with both its vision encoder and language backbone kept trainable. This stage eliminates cold-start format failures and grounds visual observations into executable control loops and observation requests. 3. Online outcome-based RL. Starting from the SFT checkpoint with no teacher in the loop, group-relative reinforcement learning optimizes the student VLM directly on its own sampled episodes using a runtime-error-penalized success reward. Online optimization overcomes imitation distribution shift and substantially improves both task success and multi-turn decision efficiency. All three pipelines are unified by a single think-then-code policy representation over one Python execution tool ( execute_python ). Unlike prior code-as-policy agents [ 24 , 2 ] that compose a hand-engineered library of manipulation primitives, EmbodyAgent exposes only two raw runtime operations: env.observation() , which returns primary and wrist camera images together with robot proprioception, and env.action(a) , which advances one simulator control step with a\in[-1,1]^{7} . At every turn, the VLM is required to emit a chain-of-thought explanation planning loop structure, step batching, and observation timing, followed by an executable Python program. Because the policy lives entirely within a standard multimodal text-and-code interface—with no external action decoder and no pre-defined primitives—black-box synthesis, SFT, and RL operate over the exact same output contract without any cross-stage representation shift. We validate the complete pipeline on ten LIBERO Spatial tasks [ 26 ] under a strictly separated evaluation protocol: per-task initial states are partitioned into disjoint train, validation, and test splits; Stage 1 synthesizes 100 successful episodes (1,475 targets) from the black-box teacher Astra Medium on train states; Stage 2 distills Qwen3.5-9B [ 41 ] across vision and language parameters; and Stage 3 runs temperature-one group-relative RL on train and validation states while evaluating greedily on all 100 held-out test states after every round under full episode budgets and zero test-time safeguards. Across this progression, held-out success advances from 0/100 (base 9B VLM) to 14/100 (after SFT) and 84/100 (after 64 rounds of RL), while RL training rollout success rises from 8.4% to 87.7% with all ten tasks improving. Simultaneously, Stage 3 RL sharpens the VLM’s execution efficiency, compressing median model calls per test episode from 36 (SFT) to 11 and yielding a self-contained 9B VLM that executes locally with no ongoing reliance on the black-box teacher. Executing outcome-based RL over multi-turn code generation also surfaces a failure mode absent from fixed-action policies: a self-reinforcing runtime-error loop in which execution tracebacks enter the multimodal history that subsequent turns condition on, allowing error-prone patterns to amplify before recovery. ## 3 The EmbodyAgent Post-Training Pipeline
Figure 1 illustrates the complete EmbodyAgent post-training stack: a unified executable interface shared verbatim across three successive pipelines—Stage 1 black-box teacher data synthesis, Stage 2 multimodal SFT, and Stage 3 online RL (detailed in Section 4 ). Each pipeline consumes the preceding stage’s outputs without altering the model’s observation, reasoning, or action schema. 3.1 Shared think-then-code policy interface We formulate closed-loop manipulation as a multi-turn partially observed interaction conditioned on a language instruction g . At model turn t , the multimodal context h_{t} contains g , previous assistant turns, and environment execution feedback, including any explicitly requested images. Rather than emitting raw action vectors, the VLM policy outputs a public chain-of-thought explanation e_{t} followed by an executable Python program c_{t} : \pi_{\theta}(e_{t},c_{t}\mid h_{t})=\pi_{\theta}(e_{t}\mid h_{t})\pi_{\theta}(c_{t}\mid h_{t},e_{t}). A persistent Python runtime executes c_{t} inside a minimal environment interface exposing only two operations: env.observation() , which returns primary and wrist camera images together with robot proprioception, and env.action(a) , which steps the existing simulator controller with a seven-dimensional end-effector command a\in[-1,1]^{7} (translation, rotation, and gripper). Unlike code-as-policy frameworks that select from a pre-built library of manipulation primitives, this interface provides no primitives: the VLM synthesizes its own control loops, conditionals, and action batches directly over raw sensing and actuation. Executing c_{t} returns feedback f_{t} —stdout, exceptions, the last expression’s value, and any requested images—and advances the history: h_{t+1}=h_{t}\mathbin{\|}(e_{t},c_{t},f_{t}). Because visual observations enter the context only when c_{t} invokes env.observation() , a single program can execute multiple control steps before consulting the VLM again. This keeps the entire policy inside a standard multimodal text-and-code interface with no external action decoder, making both action composition and observation scheduling directly trainable across synthesis, SFT, and RL. 3.2 Stage 1: Black-box teacher data synthesis The goal of Stage 1 is to synthesize high-quality multimodal training trajectories from a strong teacher VLM without requiring access to teacher weights, activations, or token log-probabilities. We deploy Astra Medium (a frontier VLM at medium reasoning effort) strictly as a black-box agent operating through the execute_python harness on official pre-generated LIBERO initial states restricted to the training split. Left unconstrained, the teacher defaults to emitting bare code blocks without explicit planning. The synthesis harness therefore enforces the think-then-code contract at every turn: any response omitting the explanation terminates the episode immediately as a format failure and is discarded. Completed episodes are retained if and only if the environment’s BDDL success predicate passes. Across 1,620 synthesis attempts (32% task-averaged acceptance rate over valid attempts), Stage 1 collects 100 validated episodes—ten per task across the ten LIBERO Spatial tasks—and exports them into 1,475 multimodal assistant-response targets. Each target pairs the full interaction history with the teacher’s explanation and executable program, providing clean supervision for control synthesis, formatting, and observation timing purely from black-box interaction. 3.3 Stage 2: Multimodal supervised fine-tuning An out-of-the-box open-weight 9B VLM fails completely on the interactive contract (0/100 held-out states), making supervised cold-start distillation essential before online exploration.
