# Zero-Shot VLA Adaptation with Retrieved Guidance

## Abstract
Pre-training large parametric "Vision-Language-Action" (VLA) models on large demonstration datasets has recently yielded promising results for general-purpose manipulation. However, these models still struggle under even small domain shifts relative to the training data, such as changes in camera viewpoint, background, object identity, or robot morphology. Motivated by the success of Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) in language models, we pursue retrieval to boost VLA performance. Specifically, we seek to leverage retrieval from domain-mismatched, "in-the-wild" demonstration datasets like DROID to guide a VLA's behavior in a new target domain. To achieve this, we introduce RAGu, which overcomes robotics-specific challenges for RAG: (1) It incorporates simple geometric calibration to transform source demonstrations into the target setup and reduce cross-domain differences. (2) It uses a coarse-to-fine retrieval strategy that selects, from a large pool of pre-warped candidates, the trajectory most compatible with the base VLA. (3) It guides the VLA's flow-matching inference procedure rather than seeking to retrain the base model or expand its limited context window. We evaluate RAGu across real-world, simulation, sim-to-real transfer, and cross-embodiment humanoid manipulation. We find it improves real-world VLA success rates from 20% to 80% and outperforms the closest-related baselines in sim. Project website: raguvla.github.io

## I INTRODUCTION
Vision-language-action (VLA) models have emerged in recent years as a key step toward generalist robots. However, their ability to generalize still falls short of foundation models in language, vision, and coding. One reason is that, in robotics, both pretraining datasets and the models trained on them remain relatively small, with the latter constrained by the need for near-real-time execution. As a result, VLAs still struggle to generalize across variations in camera viewpoint, scene background, object identity, and robot embodiment. For language models, “retrieval-augmented generation (RAG)" [ 1 ] is a well-established technique to boost performance, especially of small models. Given a new query, RAG fetches relevant documents from a large database, typically through nearest neighbor search in an appropriate feature space, and places them into the model’s context to guide its performance on the target task. This augments the limited “parametric knowledge” of the small language model with “non-parametric knowledge” in the form of the retrieved examples. In this paper, we seek to develop a RAG-style mechanism to improve generalization in vision-language-action (VLA) models. We target three key problems in implementing RAG for robotics : (1) Cross-Domain Generalization. Robotic data suffers from severe distribution shifts in camera viewpoints, layouts, and embodiments, unlike the universal token space of LLMs. For a given target scenario, even the best-matched trajectories in large-scale robotics datasets are still likely to exhibit such domain-shifts . Prior robotic retrieval and RAG-style methods therefore restrict retrieval pools to narrow, identical-embodiment datasets or rely on task-specific fine-tuning of policy networks [ 2 , 3 , 4 ] , fundamentally limiting their overall utility. (2) Policy-Aware Retrieval. Good retrievals must be policy-aware . Proximity based on simple measures like geometry do not suffice for a robot policy to follow a retrieved trajectory smoothly and stably. (3) Context-Efficient Adaptation. VLAs have limited context windows that do not permit directly concatenating retrieved demonstration trajectories to current observations. We propose R etrieval- A ugmented G u idance ( RAGu ), a zero-shot, training-free VLA adaptation framework depicted in Figure 1 , that handles all three challenges: 1. Cross-Domain Generalization. It handles domain shifts through explicit injection of geometric and semantic priors based on Cartesian spatial traces in source data. 2. Policy-Aware Retrieval. It selects source trajectories for guidance based on compatibility with the VLA rather than only geometric proximity, and this selection is scaled efficiently through offline pre-warping and lightweight online scoring. 3. Context-Efficient Adaptation. Finally, rather than concatenating retrieved examples to the VLA’s input, it constructs a test-time guidance field to steer the VLA’s outputs.

## IV Cross-Domain R etrieval- A ugmented G u idance for Zero-Shot VLA Adaptation
As motivated in Section I , using retrieved examples from a large out-of-domain source dataset to assist a pre-trained VLA faces three challenges: cross-domain shifts in viewpoint, geometry, or embodiment; the candidate closest in trajectory space is not necessarily one the policy can follow; and the lack of context window for demonstration tokens. As shown in Figure 1 , RAGu first filters semantically relevant demonstrations and warps them into the target scene via semantic correspondences, turning cross-domain demonstrations into usable spatial priors. It then retrieves the guiding trajectory via coarse offline dynamic time-warping (DTW)-based pruning followed by policy-compatible selection at inference time, avoiding control-loop overhead. Finally, it steers the VLA’s flow-matching denoising toward the chosen trajectory through an energy-based guidance field, leaving the policy’s weights and context untouched. Semantic Task-Category Filtering. To ensure alignment with the target task, we first curate an initial pool of structurally compatible trajectories from large-scale datasets. We achieve this by filtering unstructured real-world datasets [ 15 ] using semantic keywords for broad task categories that share similar kinematic profiles. For example, we query demonstrations with keyword "pick" and "place" included for the task, "putting towel on the plate" . See Fig 1 (a). Trajectory Warping Across Different Domains. Unlike in-domain applications, cross-domain environments frequently exhibit significant semantic shifts, often lacking identical object instances or shared categories. To overcome this domain gap, we replace strict in-domain visual matching from prior work [ 10 ] with extracting semantic bounding boxes for task-relevant entities in the target scene via Grounding DINO [ 16 ] , effectively localizing the corresponding target waypoints W^{\prime} . See Fig 1 (b). We define correspondence here not by object similarity, but at the level of functional roles it plays within a task. Once these semantic correspondences are established, the remainder of the trajectory warping process proceeds identically to the formulation described in Section III . Primary Retrieval: Coarse-grained static filtering via DTW. For each task category, we designate a single reference trajectory by taking an arbitrary valid demonstration–typically the first entry–from the semantically filtered pool. This one-time reference requires no per-episode tuning or meticulous curation. Using this trajectory as a reference, we then apply Dynamic Time Warping (DTW) [ 17 ] once for every episode to prune the remaining candidates down to the top- n trajectories, N . Consequently, RAGu maintains n warped candidate trajectories \{W^{(i)}_{t}\}_{i\in[N]} in the target frame, where W^{(i)}_{t}=\{w^{(i)}_{t},\dots,w^{(i)}_{t+H-1}\} denotes the waypoint sequence of candidate i at time step t . Note that RAGu does not assume matched domains in the source data: Across all experiments, target scenes differ from retrieved demonstrations in viewpoint, appearance, object identity, and goal location, requiring only shared task categories. Secondary Retrieval: Fine-grained inference-time policy alignment. At time-step t , we estimate the VLA’s output action chunk \hat{A}_{\tau} referred from Section III with \tau=1 , and map to a Cartesian end-effector path X_{t}=\{x_{t},\dots,x_{t+H-1}\} via forward kinematics applied to \hat{A}_{\tau} . For candidate trajectory W^{(i)}_{t} , we define \mathcal{C}_{i}(X_{t})=\left\|X_{t}-\mathrm{seqMatch}\big(X_{t},W^{(i)}_{t}\big)\right\|_{2}^{2}\qquad(3) where \mathrm{seqMatch} is a monotone nearest-neighbor projection approach (details on website). We select the trajectory whose cost gradient has the smallest norm: i^{\star}=\arg\min_{i\in N}\left\|\nabla_{A_{0}}\mathcal{C}_{i}(X_{t})\right\|_{2}\qquad(4) To understand this choice, observe that this gradient norm is an estimate of how much the VLA must be steered away from its original intent to reduce \mathcal{C}_{i} . By selecting trajectories with small gradient values, we obtain reference paths that stay close to the VLA’s learned priors over optimal actions.
