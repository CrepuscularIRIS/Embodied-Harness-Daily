# Externalise the Memory: A Pre-Registered Study of Where an Embodied Agent's Experience Should Live

## Abstract
Should an embodied agent's experience be baked into its policy weights, or kept external and retrieved at inference? A memory-augmented teacher (a VLM that retrieves lessons, with their episode images, from its own past attempts) produces trajectories on a vision-conditioned reach task; from the same trajectories we distil LoRA student policies and evaluate them under matched protocols, including one that re-attaches the teacher's frozen retrieval buffer to a fixed set of student weights. We pre-registered the hypotheses, arms, tests, and analysis rules before training and before any p-value. Distilling the memory-augmented behaviour into weights beat an action-only control by +1 pp (n.s.); the registered test proves underpowered for its own minimum effect, so we withdraw - rather than refute - the memory-in-weights claim. Attaching retrieval to a fixed set of student weights added +34 pp (z=5.15), reaching the pooled memory-augmented teacher reference (49.3%); the pre-registered protocol contrast agrees (+23 pp, z=3.36); under a pre-registered auto-reset sensitivity count the two are +36 and +29 pp. An exploratory behavioural probe with pre-registered per-arm predictions is consistent with behavioural distillation transferring the marginal correction but not the conditional, situation-specific correction that retrieval supplies. The recipe that follows is to externalise the memory: in follow-up controls registered before they ran, the same frozen buffer lifts the base model with no adapter to 48% (sensitivity 48%) and the other three adapters from 19-26% to 53-55% (sensitivity 17-21% to 50-55%), and the adapter adds nothing detectable once the buffer is attached. On a second, harder task, the retrieval advantage points the same direction but falls short of significance (+6 pp, 95% CI [-4.5, +16.4]); exploratory diagnostics tie the attenuation to memory coverage.

## 1. Introduction
Opening problem When an embodied agent accumulates experience, where should that experience live: folded into the policy’s parameters, or kept outside and retrieved when needed? The question is old and unresolved. The parametric view is seductive: a fluent agent, like a fluent human, should not have to look things up mid-task; skill that has been practised enough becomes automatic, and automaticity feels like something written into the substrate. The contextual view is pragmatic: retrieval systems that keep experience external and look it up have repeatedly matched or beaten parametric memorisation in language tasks. For embodied agents the question is structural: a retrieval context grows with every experience the agent accumulates, while its weights do not, so “bake it into the weights” is architecturally convenient as well as philosophically appealing. Whether it works is an empirical question that, to our knowledge, has not been asked for embodied skill under controlled conditions. Why this test bed answers it We ask it with a design that isolates the variable of interest. A memory-augmented teacher (a VLM that retrieves lessons, with their episode images, from its own past attempts) produces trajectories on a vision-conditioned single-arm reach task (an Isaac Lab bimanual rig with the right arm frozen). From those same trajectories we distil student policies whose training targets differ only in whether retrieved-lesson content is present, and we evaluate them under matched protocols, including one that re-attaches inference-time retrieval to a fixed set of student weights. The teacher’s experience buffer is thus routed to the student either as distillation targets (lesson gist in text) or as retrieval context at inference (lesson text with its episode image). The critical pair shares identical adapter weights and differs only in protocol: memory supplied by baking it into those weights during distillation, versus memory supplied through a retrieval context at inference. This makes “parametric vs contextual memory” a controlled contrast on one axis. Pre-registration as a stance Because the headline outcome is a null, and nulls are the most easily disbelieved result in machine learning, we pre-registered in two locked layers. The hypotheses, comparison arms, primary tests, and their directionality were registered before any adapter trained ; the final analysis rules (the pairing-integrity gate and its mechanical test-selection fallback) were locked after collection but before any p -value was computed . That locked rule kept the raw success counts, which were visible in the collection logs, out of the test-selection decision. The pre-registration lets a reader take the null as evidence rather than as a failure to find an effect we wanted. Every result below is labelled confirmatory or exploratory against that registered plan. Contributions 1. A design criterion for where experience should live : marginal knowledge (the correction that holds on average across situations) transfers into weights, while conditional knowledge (the correction that depends on the current situation) belongs in context, because weights can only approximate a conditional function with a constant whereas context conditions on it for free. We name and operationalise this marginal/conditional distinction; the split we observe is consistent with it. (This is the transferable takeaway; the results below are consistent with it.) 2. A pre-registered null on parametric memory (T1): distilling the memory-augmented teacher’s behaviour into student weights beat an action-only control by +1 pp (n.s.; auto-reset sensitivity -2 pp), and we withdrew the memory-in-weights claim per the registered falsification clause. The withdrawal is scoped to this recipe (single-round SFT + composable-KTO), this task, this data scale, and this adapter capacity (LoRA rank-16). A no-training diagnostic (§4.4b) finds no detectable conditional trace at round 1; whether more data of the same kind would grow one remains open. 3. ## 3. Methods
3.1 Task and environment We use a vision-conditioned single-arm reach task (L0a-Left) in an Isaac Lab ( Mittal et al., 2023 ) bimanual manipulation rig with the right arm held frozen throughout, a controlled setting in which only the left end-effector is actuated toward a target cube. An episode succeeds when the active end-effector comes within 0.05 m of the target; each episode runs up to a fixed round budget (and is subject to an inherited 24 s environment auto-reset, §3.5), where a round is one VLM reasoning step followed by an inverse-kinematics servo to the emitted target. All evaluation runs use --freeze_level (no curriculum auto-advance) so every arm sees the identical task distribution. 3.2 Teacher and memory pipeline The teacher is Gemma-4-31B-it ( Gemma Team, 2024 ) (multimodal, 4-bit) served via llama.cpp, augmented with an image-anchored episodic memory: after each episode it writes a self-recap; at round 1 of a new episode it retrieves the top-K visually-and-state-similar past recaps (DINOv2 ( Oquab et al., 2024 ) image embedding, state-aware ranking) and injects them as a PAST_LESSONS preamble: each retrieved episode’s lesson text and outcome, together with that episode’s initial camera image. The memory-augmented teacher’s trajectories are the distillation source; its retrieval buffer, frozen at a fixed snapshot (SHA-pinned), is what the C_retrieval protocol later reuses at student inference. The recap prompt received an unformatted {target_color} instruction template (disclosed in §4.5); the lessons themselves are unaffected. 3.3 Students, arms, and the 2 \times 2 factorial Students are LoRA ( Hu et al., 2022 ) adapters (rank-16, \alpha =32, dropout 0.05) on the same Gemma-4-31B-it base, targeting the language-model attention and MLP projections only ( self_attn.{q,k,v,o}_proj , mlp.{gate,up,down}_proj ); the vision tower is untouched.
