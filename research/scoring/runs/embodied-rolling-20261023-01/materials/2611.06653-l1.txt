# Detecting Silent Action-Interface Mismatch with Calibrated Relational World Models

## Abstract
A robot action can satisfy shape and range constraints yet carry the wrong semantics: axes may be permuted, signs inverted, rotations rescaled, or gripper polarity reversed. We study learned state-action-effect relations as offline contracts for detecting these mismatches and ranking their likely fields. Clean held-out transitions calibrate alarms; a fixed inverse vocabulary supports closed-set localization. On ten LIBERO Spatial tasks comprising 432 source episodes, a capacity-controlled comparison at 2.5% empirical clean false-positive rate gives 46.1% recall for a ridge forward residual, 61.0% for a four-component joint Gaussian mixture, and 65.7% for the exact conditional of that same mixture. The joint score retains a 14.9 percentage-point advantage over ridge, while conditioning improves recall by a further 4.7 points. Joint scoring instead has higher localization: 95.8% versus 94.6%. These matched-rate comparisons are retrospective diagnostics, not deployment calibration guarantees. Earlier Object/Goal results broaden tasks within the LIBERO convention; DROID, all three HYDRA tasks, short temporal faults, small offsets, exact repair, and simulated mitigation fail their respective gates, while xArm contributes one task only. The evidence supports a bounded offline diagnostic and a detection-localization tradeoff, rather than joint-density superiority or cross-robot safety.

## 1 Introduction
World models usually predict dynamics or support planning. At a controller boundary, they can also ask whether an action’s semantics agree with the observed transition. A seven-dimensional action vector may mix normalized translation, axis-angle rotation and gripper commands. Components can agree on shape and bounds while disagreeing about scale, axes, signs, rotation units or polarity. Such a command is numerically legal but inconsistent with the recorded state change. We study whether recorded state–action–next-state tuples can supply an executable contract for this failure class. A detector is trained on clean transitions, a disjoint clean split fixes its alarm threshold, and in-range semantic mutations are applied only after that calibration is frozen. After an alarm, the system evaluates a declared set of inverse transformations and reports the one that best restores relational consistency. This separates two questions that aggregate anomaly scores often conflate: whether the transition should be inspected and which contract field is the most plausible explanation. The diagnostic addresses interface semantics, not perception, planning, contact or task progress. Replay supplies the factual next state of the original action, not the physical outcome of executing a corrupted action. We evaluate offline alarms and closed-set localization; they do not measure task success or safe correction. The study separates three sources of apparent advantage: using the observed transition, representing multimodal clean behavior, and choosing an alarm operating point. The original joint-mixture comparison outperformed a ridge forward residual, but those methods had different achieved false-positive rates. A post-decision capacity-controlled analysis addresses both confounds. At matched empirical clean FPR, the exact conditional of the fitted mixture has higher detection recall than the joint score; the joint score retains better closed-set localization. We report that reversal alongside episode-level uncertainty and the external and closed-loop failures that bound the method’s use.

## 2 Relation to prior work
World models learn compact predictive representations for control ( Ha & Schmidhuber, 2018 ) ; here prediction is used as evidence about an interface, not as a rollout planner. Learned runtime monitors instead anticipate broad policy failures. Model-based monitoring uses interactive imitation learning ( Liu et al., 2023b ) ; Sentinel combines temporal action consistency with task progress ( Agia et al., 2024 ) ; and RAPT uses calibrated predictive deviation for deployment-time detection and diagnosis ( Munn et al., 2026 ) . These systems address general failure or distribution shift. Our scope is a declared vocabulary of controller-semantics transformations and attribution to a contract field. Model-security work supplies a scope boundary. Backdoor detectability depends on nominal/hidden-task relation and detector assumptions ( Tang et al., 2022 ; Zhu et al., 2024 ) ; TrojAI distinguishes detection, diagnosis and mitigation ( Reese et al., 2026 ) . Backdoor suppression ( Zhu et al., 2023 ; Jiang et al., 2026 ) and model-lifecycle risks ( Chen et al., 2024 ; Wang et al., 2025 ; Zhu et al., 2026 ) concern different interventions. They provide no evidence for this fixed-weight interface diagnostic, and inverse ranking is neither model purification nor adaptive-attack robustness. Action Mahalanobis distance is a natural cheap out-of-distribution baseline ( Lee et al., 2018 ) . It cannot, by construction, use the observed state change: a transformed action may remain marginally plausible while becoming inconsistent with the transition. A field-aligned static comparator can couple translation, rotation, and gripper coordinates to measured displacement, but public benchmark metadata omit the controller gains and absolute-versus-delta semantics needed for a faithful executable specification. We include both checks so that improvement is not measured only against range validation. Robot-testing work explores a complementary axis. VLATest generates diverse manipulation scenes ( Wang et al., 2024 ) , FATE-VLA searches for failure-prone scenes ( Kanwal et al., 2026 ) , and metamorphic testing compares related executions without a task-specific oracle ( Valle et al., 2026 ) . ContractFuzz mutates action semantics rather than observations or scenes. The motivating deployment observation is that weights alone do not specify normalization and controller conventions ( Tai, 2026 ) ; our experiments test when a learned state–action relation can expose that missing contract.

## 3 Executable contract oracle
3.1 Threat model and supported vocabulary Let s_{t}\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{s}} , a_{t}\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{a}} , and s_{t+1} be a clean recorded transition. A fault k applies a transform T_{k}:\mathbb{R}^{d_{a}}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d_{a}} . We evaluate only changed rows whose transformed action remains inside clean training bounds, \mathcal{V}_{k}=\{t:T_{k}(a_{t})\neq a_{t},\;\ell\leq T_{k}(a_{t})\leq u\}. (1) The supported set contains halving the six motion coordinates, swapping the X and Y axes, reversing the X-axis sign, shrinking rotation units, and reversing gripper polarity. A fixed X-offset is retained as an unsupported stress test. Temporal delay, half-frequency hold, and chunk reversal are evaluated separately and fail the main detector gate. This is a controlled counterfactual on recorded data: the detector receives (s_{t},T_{k}(a_{t}),s_{t+1}) , but s_{t+1} is the recorded outcome of a_{t} . That design isolates relational inconsistency and supplies a known mutation label. It does not simulate contact dynamics, policy adaptation, or the task outcome of executing T_{k}(a_{t}) . 3.2 Calibrated relational scores From clean training transitions, we fit a ridge forward model f_{\theta}(s_{t},a_{t})\approx s_{t+1}-s_{t} (2) and normalize each residual dimension by its training root-mean-square error. The conditional score is R_{f}(s_{t},a,s_{t+1})=\sqrt{\operatorname{mean}\left[\left(\frac{f_{\theta}(s_{t},a)-(s_{t+1}-s_{t})}{\sigma_{f}}\right)^{2}\right]}. (3) We also standardize the joint vector z=[s_{t},a,s_{t+1}-s_{t}] . A regularized joint Gaussian gives R_{j}(z)=\sqrt{(z-\mu)^{\top}(\Sigma+10^{-2}I)^{\dagger}(z-\mu)}. (4) The original selected score is the negative log likelihood of a full-covariance mixture, R_{G}(z)=-\log\sum_{m=1}^{M}\pi_{m}\mathcal{N}(z;\mu_{m},\Sigma_{m}+10^{-3}I), (5) with M=4 and deterministic split-seed initialization. To control model capacity, we also evaluate the exact conditional of this fitted mixture. With x=[s_{t},a] and y=s_{t+1}-s_{t} , R_{C}(y\mid x)=R_{G}(x,y)+\log p_{G}(x). (6) The marginal p_{G}(x) uses the same weights, component input means and covariance blocks as p_{G}(x,y) . The conditional mixture therefore has input-dependent component weights and conditional means, without adding parameters or fitting a separate model. Its comparison with R_{G} isolates the contribution of the input marginal to the score; it does not compare independently optimized model classes. A disjoint clean calibration split sets each detector’s threshold to an empirical upper quantile targeting 2.5% false positives. No mutation outcome enters model selection or threshold fitting.
