# Ask to Evolve: A Unified Harness for Lifelong Embodied Navigation with Hybrid Action Space and Persistent Memory

## Abstract
Giving embodied agents the freedom to act autonomously, initiate interactions, and understand their surroundings through experience is a step toward bringing them to life in the physical world. Realizing this vision requires action, interaction, and memory to work together, so that each encounter can inform current decisions and contribute experience for future tasks. We present EvoLife, a unified navigation harness that enables a multimodal embodied agent to choose how to move, when to ask, and what experience to carry forward. Its hybrid action space supports waypoint navigation, local discrete adjustments, pixel-based navigation, and object-directed navigation. Active interaction allows the agent to seek clarification or assistance when it encounters ambiguity or difficulty, while persistent spatial memory binds observations and dialogue to places, retaining useful experience to guide subsequent visits. We evaluate eight benchmarks across four task families: vision-language navigation, object navigation, interactive instance navigation, and continuous multi-task navigation. With an average of only 1.87 active interactions per episode, our method achieves a first-visit success rate (SR) of 100% across four 100-episode sets spanning vision-language navigation and object navigation. Complete-sequence SR reaches 98% on 50 LHPR-VLN sequences and 94% on 50 GOAT-Bench sequences. In the R2R revisit setting, success weighted by path length (SPL) increases from 82.23% to 93.31% with retained experience. Multi-day physical navigation further demonstrates exploration, proactive help-seeking, and revisiting remembered places. Together, these results advance the vision of bringing embodied agents to life through autonomous action, proactive interaction, and the accumulation and reuse of experience.

## 1 Introduction
Lifelong embodied navigation calls for agents that can take initiative in how they act, interact, and accumulate experience. An agent should be able to choose how to pursue a goal according to its environment and task requirements, ask questions when it encounters ambiguity, seek assistance when progress becomes difficult, and carry what it learns into future tasks. These capabilities give concrete meaning to the vision of bringing embodied agents to life in the physical world. Through sustained engagement with their surroundings, agents can connect what they perceive with what they do, use interaction to develop their understanding, and build experience that remains useful beyond a single encounter. People navigating unfamiliar environments naturally ask for clarification, request directions, and seek help when needed. Embodied agents need the freedom to take the same initiative. A question can clarify an instruction, identify a useful continuation, or obtain assistance with a physical obstacle. The value of that interaction can also extend beyond the immediate task: an answer about a place, together with the observations and actions that follow, can become experience for a later visit. This motivates the central idea of Ask to Evolve : an agent actively asks to improve its understanding, acts on the resulting information, and retains useful experience to guide future behavior. Embodied navigation provides concrete settings for studying this process. Vision-language navigation connects linguistic instructions with visual observations and navigation decisions, including movement through continuous environments [ 3 , 27 , 26 , 12 ] . Interactive navigation introduces dialogue as a source of clarification and assistance [ 49 , 38 , 13 ] , while iterative and lifelong navigation make earlier encounters relevant to subsequent tasks [ 25 , 24 ] . Systematic real-world evaluations have documented a substantial gap between simulated and physical navigation performance [ 54 , 55 ] . This gap highlights the importance of coordinating perception, decision-making, and execution, together with the ability to obtain assistance and reuse experience in real environments. Existing methods offer useful foundations. Topological planners connect visual waypoints with continuous control [ 1 ] , and language-model navigators integrate observations, reasoning, and actions through execution interfaces [ 82 , 83 ] . Memory-based systems retain observations and behavior, while navigation harnesses preserve maps and task records across sessions [ 63 , 80 , 7 ] . These advances motivate a unified systems question: how can an embodied agent keep multiple ways of moving, actively requested assistance, and remembered experience available within the same decision loop? The connection matters because an interaction may change the next movement, revise the interpretation of a place, and contribute knowledge that is useful during a subsequent task. We introduce EvoLife , a unified harness for lifelong embodied navigation with a hybrid action space, active interaction, and persistent spatial memory. Its action space supports four complementary modes: waypoint navigation toward a selected spatial location, local discrete adjustments for fine control, pixel-based navigation toward a selected image location, and object-directed navigation toward an identified target. These modes give the agent flexibility in expressing its movement intention. It can select a spatial waypoint, make a short adjustment, indicate a visible location, or approach an object, according to the task and available observations. Execution returns the actual outcome, allowing the agent to inspect its progress and choose its next action. Fig. 2 presents the overall framework. Active interaction is part of this ongoing decision process. The agent chooses when to ask and formulates its question using the current task, observations, and relevant memory. It can request clarification when an instruction is ambiguous, ask for guidance when the continuation is uncertain, or seek practical assistance when the environment prevents progress. After receiving a response, the agent decides how to act and uses subsequent observations and execution feedback to assess the outcome. Interaction therefore gives the agent an active means of obtaining information and assistance while remaining connected to movement and the experience generated through execution. Persistent spatial memory allows these experiences to accumulate across tasks. Place records bind locations to descriptions, observations, historical images, and dialogue. Information acquired through an interaction can update a place description, support a later navigation decision, or help interpret a familiar environment under changed conditions.
