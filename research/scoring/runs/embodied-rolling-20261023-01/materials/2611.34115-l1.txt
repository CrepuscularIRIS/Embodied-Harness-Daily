===ABSTRACT===
Open-world aerial object search methods often rely on persistent spatial maps, learned world models, or task-specific policy training. To examine how far a minimal, training-free design can go, we introduce SimpleUAV, an agent built around a frozen, off-the-shelf vision-language model (VLM). SimpleUAV separates current-view target assessment from history-conditioned action selection, combining fresh candidate evidence with lightweight search context. Local geometry converts visual selections into bounded straight-line movements, depth-based checks halt blocked segments, and measured execution feedback guides subsequent decisions. This closed loop sustains search without persistent spatial maps or task-specific training. SimpleUAV achieves 41.61% Success Rate (SR) on UAV-ON and 11.98% on AerialDojo-200K, compared with 23.80% and 3.20% for the strongest prior baselines, respectively. With limited benchmark-specific adaptation, the same core agent transfers to aerial Vision-and-Language Navigation (VLN) on TravelUAV, improving Full SR by at least 19.66% across all three splits under L1 assistance. These results establish a strong baseline for evaluating when additional architectural complexity benefits aerial search.

===INTRO===
Aerial object-goal navigation requires an unmanned aerial vehicle (UAV) to locate and approach a target described in natural language in an unfamiliar outdoor environment [ 47 , 19 , 10 ] . This capability supports applications such as search and rescue [ 45 ] and infrastructure inspection [ 40 ] . Given neither the target location nor detailed route instructions [ 59 ] , the UAV must decide where to search, which observations warrant further inspection, and when the available evidence supports stopping [ 18 ] . These decisions are tightly coupled in large, partially observed outdoor environments. The target may remain outside the UAV’s field of view for much of an episode, requiring contextual cues to guide exploration [ 23 , 46 ] . Even if a candidate appears, distance, occlusion, and visually similar distractors can make its identity uncertain [ 50 ] . Moving closer or changing altitude may reveal discriminative attributes, but also changes the visible scene and exposes the UAV to new obstacles [ 8 ] . Thus, effective search requires semantic judgments, search context, and three-dimensional motion to remain coordinated as observations evolve. Recent aerial search systems address these challenges with specialized spatial representations and predictive models. Three representative directions are shown in Figure 1 : • Semantic Maps and Beliefs. Task-oriented semantic, attraction, uncertainty, or value maps turn local observations into sustained search guidance [ 19 , 51 , 8 ] . AeroBelief further separates contextual cues from target-specific evidence through dual-layer semantic-spatial belief maps [ 46 ] . • Explicit Spatial Memory. Semantic voxel-ray memory [ 23 ] , sliding local maps with sparse global history [ 15 ] , and adaptive octree memory with instruction-guided queries [ 59 ] organize fragmented observations across changing viewpoints. • Learned World Models. SearchWorld combines explicit spatial memory with learned dynamics to reason about action consequences and improve its exploration policy through imagined rollouts [ 18 ] . These approaches introduce persistent representations, dedicated memory-processing modules, or task-specific model and policy training. At the same time, pretrained vision-language models (VLMs) offer broad semantic knowledge and open-vocabulary visual understanding [ 2 , 36 ] . These capabilities provide a basis for interpreting target descriptions, identifying promising search regions, and assessing visible candidates without navigation-specific fine-tuning. They motivate a systematic evaluation of how effectively a frozen VLM can support aerial search when paired with lightweight search context and local geometric execution. This leads us to ask: How far can a minimal, training-free VLM-based agent go in open-world aerial search? We investigate this question with SimpleUAV , a closed-loop agent built around a frozen, off-the-shelf VLM. The VLM first assesses candidate targets from current multi-view observations alone, then selects actions using localized candidates, recent interactions, and a bounded text summary. This separates current visual assessment from the context needed to maintain search continuity. Local geometry converts visual selections into bounded straight-line movements, while depth-based obstacle checks halt blocked segments. Measured execution outcomes inform the next observation and decision cycle, connecting semantic reasoning to actual motion without persistent spatial maps or task-specific policy training. Without task-specific fine-tuning, SimpleUAV improves success rate (SR) over the strongest compared baselines by 17.81% on UAV-ON [ 47 ] and 8.78% on AerialDojo-200K [ 10 ] . On the aerial VLN benchmark TravelUAV [ 44 ] , it improves Full SR by at least 19.66% across all three evaluation splits under L1 assistance. Even with a compact 4B VLM, SimpleUAV exceeds the strongest prior UAV-ON baseline by 14.13% in SR. Our main contributions are summarized below. • We show that a minimal, training-free agent built around a frozen VLM can substantially outperform specialized systems in open-world aerial search without persistent spatial maps or task-specific policy training. • We propose SimpleUAV , separating current-view target assessment from history-conditioned action selection and closing the loop through local geometric execution feedback to sustain search, while requiring fresh visual and geometric evidence for approach and stopping. • We demonstrate generalization across environments and navigation tasks, transferring SimpleUAV from UAV-ON to AerialDojo-200K and TravelUAV with limited benchmark-specific adaptation and no additional training, achieving the highest SR among compared methods.
