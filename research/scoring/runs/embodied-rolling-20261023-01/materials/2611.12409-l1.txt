# Bridging Task Planning and Execution for Language-Conditioned Bimanual Manipulation

## Abstract
Robots are increasingly expected to execute natural-language manipulation tasks in real-world environments. This is particularly relevant in household assistance and logistics, where clutter requires reasoning about both task objectives and execution feasibility. This challenge becomes even more pronounced for bimanual humanoid platforms, where coordinating two manipulators introduces additional interaction and kinematic constraints. We present a zero-shot vision-language framework for execution-aware bimanual manipulation that jointly reasons about task objectives and physical feasibility. Planning is grounded in a structured scene representation that combines semantic object relations with execution constraints, enabling the selection of obstacle relocations and execution choices while preserving task semantics. Compatible actions are organised into parallel bimanual stages with explicit pre- and post-conditions. A hierarchical validator evaluates these conditions under perceptual uncertainty and uses stage-level execution history to support local recovery or targeted replanning. Experiments on the CENTAURO hybrid wheeled-legged humanoid robot across nine language-conditioned manipulation scenarios show higher task success than a non-execution-aware baseline. In dedicated failure scenarios, hierarchical validation recovers from execution failures with fewer model queries than always replanning. These results demonstrate the benefits of combining execution reasoning directly into task planning. Videos and additional material are available on the Project Webpage: https://advrhumanoids.github.io/bridging-planning-execution/.

## I INTRODUCTION
Robots operating in human-centred environments must translate natural-language instructions into physically executable manipulation behaviours [ 1 , 2 , 3 ] . This problem naturally arises in household assistance and logistics, where robots may need to retrieve requested objects, reorganise cluttered shelves, or manipulate items in dynamic workspaces. In these settings, each manipulation can alter object accessibility and the feasibility of subsequent actions, requiring continuous reasoning about both task objectives and execution constraints. This is particularly relevant in cluttered scenes, where retrieving a target object may require removing obstructions, selecting suitable relocation regions, and preserving the feasibility of later interactions [ 4 , 5 , 6 ] . Such constraints are especially important for humanoid robots performing bimanual manipulation, since coordinating two manipulators introduces additional interaction and kinematic dependencies [ 7 , 8 ] . Task planning must therefore go beyond semantic reasoning and explicitly account for object accessibility, mutual obstruction, and robot kinematic limitations. Recent advances in foundation models have significantly improved language-conditioned robot planning. Vision-Language Models (VLMs) enable robots to interpret open-ended instructions and generate task-level manipulation plans [ 1 , 9 , 2 , 3 ] , while Vision-Language-Action (VLA) policies directly predict robot actions from multimodal observations [ 10 , 11 , 12 ] . Despite these advances, existing approaches often either reason at a high semantic level or learn low-level behaviours end-to-end, providing limited support for explicitly reasoning about the physical interaction choices that determine whether a planned action can be realised. Existing work addresses complementary aspects of this gap. Mechanical-search approaches explicitly reason about geometric obstruction and object rearrangement [ 4 , 5 , 6 ] , whereas planners based on structured scene representations capture semantic and spatial object relations [ 13 , 14 , 15 ] . However, current formulations rarely represent the physical interaction choices required to bridge symbolic task plans and feasible manipulation. Consequently, accessibility constraints and interaction choices often remain implicit during planning, making it difficult to associate execution failures with the decisions that generated them. Explicitly modelling physical interaction choices also provides a basis for structured execution validation. Existing systems can detect, explain, or recover from execution failures [ 16 , 17 , 18 ] ; however, when these choices remain implicit, feedback can reveal that an action has failed without identifying which planning requirement or expected effect was violated. Representing them through explicit conditions allows failures to be localised, execution progress to be assessed, and local recovery to be attempted before resorting to replanning. We propose a zero-shot framework based on VLMs, i.e., requiring no task-specific training, fine-tuning, or demonstrations, that jointly reasons about task objectives and the physical interaction choices required to realise them in cluttered scenes, as illustrated in Fig. 1 . This study builds upon our previous work [ 19 ] . The main contributions are: • a structured scene representation that jointly models semantic object relations, blocking dependencies, side-level accessibility, and robot reachability to support manipulation planning in cluttered environments; • an execution-aware planning framework that selects manipulation actions, specifies their physical realisation, and schedules compatible actions for parallel bimanual execution. A hierarchical validator uses stage-level conditions and execution history to assess progress, resolve perceptual uncertainty, and select local recovery or targeted replanning; • an experimental evaluation on the CENTAURO hybrid wheeled-legged humanoid platform [ 20 ] , including real-world cluttered target-retrieval experiments, dedicated failure-recovery scenarios, and component ablation studies.

## III Method
Figure 2 provides an overview of the proposed framework for language-conditioned bimanual manipulation in cluttered environments. Starting from a visual observation of the scene and a natural-language task instruction, the framework constructs an executable manipulation strategy while explicitly accounting for execution constraints and validating task progress throughout execution. The framework comprises four main components: structured scene representation, language-guided task planning, parallel execution scheduling, and hierarchical execution validation. The individual components are described in the following sections. III-A Object Geometric Representation Objects are represented as oriented bounding boxes defined by their position, dimensions, and orientation. Each bounding box comprises three pairs of opposing parallel faces ( left-right , front-back , and top-bottom ), whose labels are assigned with respect to the robot reference frame and therefore describe workspace-relative approach directions. In particular, the front face is defined as the face oriented towards the robot, while the remaining labels follow accordingly. The proposed framework assumes objects that can be grasped through opposing contacts on one pair of parallel faces, allowing grasp accessibility and object interactions to be inferred directly from geometry. Objects requiring instance-specific grasp affordances, such as a mug that must be grasped by its handle, fall outside the scope of the framework. This is a deliberate scoping choice: many rigid objects commonly encountered in tabletop and logistics settings can be approximated through this geometric representation, and these are precisely the scenarios in which mutual obstruction constitutes the dominant difficulty addressed here. III-B Structured Scene Representation Given an RGB image I_{t} acquired from the robot viewpoint at time t , scene construction is performed in two stages. First, a VLM processes I_{t} to identify the object instances in the scene, infer their semantic attributes and qualitative pairwise relations, and determine the interaction state of the robot end-effectors. This produces a symbolic description of the scene. Second, FoundationPose [ 31 ] , in its model-based mode with CAD models of the objects, provides 6D object pose estimates that geometrically ground the detected objects and their relations. This geometric information is used to define the six robot-relative sides of each object and enrich the symbolic representation with the interaction constraints described below.
