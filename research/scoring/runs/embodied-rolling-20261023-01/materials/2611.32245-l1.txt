===ABSTRACT===
Composing frozen Vision-Language-Action (VLA) skills under an agentic planner promises scalable long-horizon manipulation without long-horizon demonstrations, yet strong atomic skills routinely fail at seams even under a correct plan. Standard remedies reset the robot's pose or draft entry conditions in language, missing the physical cause we uncover via causal interventions: even after re-homing, a frozen VLA rigidly retraces its habitual trajectory and collides with displaced scene residue, whether or not it can see it. We propose the Skill Envelope, a policy-measured 3D geometric precondition capturing the volume a frozen skill sweeps during successful executions. Exploiting the insight that the rigidity making a frozen skill fragile at seams also makes its trajectory predictable in advance, we expose each envelope as two classical 3D tools (CHECK to detect blocking residue and FREE to locate safe placement regions), establishing a division of labor where VLAs execute in-distribution manipulation and code repairs out-of-distribution states. On LIBERO-Goal with frozen $π_{0.5}$, our zero-failure envelope predicts collisions at 0.946 AUROC (0.944 from one RGB-D view) and beats frontier VLMs on seam readiness (0.902 vs. 0.793); using it for skill ordering and code repair lifts success from 82.8% to 92.3% on two-step seams and from 29.7% to 93.8% on drawer-bearing 3-4 step chains, matching privileged world reset. Together, these results show that reliable VLA composition hinges on measuring a policy's physical workspace rather than guessing preconditions in language. Ongoing work closes the loop with autonomous LLM orchestration, synthesized free-object recovery, and cross-policy evaluation on cluttered benchmarks and real hardware.

===INTRO===
Everyday long-horizon manipulation cannot be solved by enumerating end-to-end demonstrations: even ten atomic skills yield 5,040 four-step permutations, each creating distinct intermediate world states. A natural alternative is agentic composition : pairing a high-level planner or LLM agent with a library of frozen manipulation or Vision-Language-Action (VLA) skills [ 1 , 4 , 13 , 16 ] . Yet strong atomic skills routinely collapse at the transitions (or seams ) between them. Consider a two-step kitchen routine in LIBERO-Goal [ 5 ] : open the middle drawer, then push the plate to the front of the stove. A frozen \pi_{0.5} policy [ 9 ] executes both skills reliably in isolation. Chained directly, however, the second skill drops to 0/10; even after returning the robot arm to its home pose ( re-homing ), the standard fix, it remains at 0/10 because the arm runs into the open drawer (Fig. VLAs Execute, Code Recovers: 3D Skill Envelopes Bridge the Compositional Gap in Agentic Manipulation ). Across 64 ordered skill pairs (640 handoff states), mean success drops from 98.0% in isolation to 14.2% under direct chaining, and recovers only to 82.8% after re-homing. The predecessor is done , yet the successor is not ready . Recent work has recognized this compositional gap, but addresses it through proxies rather than querying the policy itself. Skill-chaining frameworks address only the robot’s configuration while leaving scene objects untouched [ 15 , 12 , 7 ] . Agentic orchestrators prompt a language model to draft natural-language entry conditions at seams and invoke programmatic primitives when those conditions fail [ 13 ] , or ask a vision-language model whether to advance or retry [ 10 ] . Others diagnose scene-residue failures only after the policy gets stuck, relying on learned recovery policies or fine-tuning [ 11 ] . What remains missing is a physical question: what exact precondition does a frozen policy require to succeed, and how can an agent check it beforehand? We answer this question in two steps. First, by intervening on the exact same 640 re-homed handoff states, we causally separate visual observation shift from physical world shift. Contrary to the common assumption that seam failures stem primarily from visual distractors, rendering residual objects invisible in the camera feed barely helps, while physically removing them recovers most of what re-homing leaves and slashes collisions (Fig. 2 a). Seam failure is therefore governed by physical, policy-specific hidden preconditions : a frozen VLA follows its habitual trajectory and collides with any residue standing in its way. Second, we turn this weakness into a measurement tool: the rigidity that makes a frozen skill fragile at seams is the very property that makes its trajectory measurable in advance. By running each skill in isolation, without human demonstrations or failure rollouts, we record the 3D volume swept by the arm, gripper, and carried object, yielding a Skill Envelope . Exposed to an agent as two classical 3D tools ( Check and Free ), the envelope establishes a clean division of labor: VLA policies execute in-distribution manipulation, classical code repairs out-of-distribution world states, and the boundary between them is drawn by policy measurement rather than language. In this Stage-1 submission, we establish the empirical foundation and core mechanism of this framework: 1. Causal seam diagnosis (Sec. II ): We decompose the compositional gap across body, view, and world states on the same 640 handoffs, showing that failures remaining after robot re-homing are dominated by policy-specific physical collisions rather than visual shift. 2. Skill Envelopes as agent tools (Sec. III ): We introduce an O(n) , zero-failure 3D envelope profiling method and formalize two geometric tools ( Check and Free ) linking frozen VLAs with classical recovery code, supporting both pre-built primitives and future agent-synthesized skills. 3. Validation across sensing, language, and multi-step seams (Sec. IV ): We show that zero-failure envelopes detect collisions even from one RGB-D view, generalize across unseen predecessors and categories, and beat frontier VLMs. Grounding why cleanup belongs to code over the VLA, envelope-guided planning and repair match privileged reset on two-skill seams and compound on 3–4 step chains.

## II Why Skills Fail at Seams: A Causal Diagnosis
To understand why high-performing atomic skills break when composed, we evaluate a frozen \pi_{0.5} policy on the 64 ordered two-skill pairs of LIBERO-Goal whose goals involve disjoint objects or parts (ten handoff states each, 640 in total). When predecessor A finishes and successor B starts, three factors simultaneously deviate from B ’s training distribution: the robot’s body configuration, the visual appearance of the scene ( view ), and the physical presence of objects moved or articulated by A ( world ). Physical collision dominates visual shift. We disentangle all three factors via controlled interventions on the exact same 640 handoff states, each snapshotted in full and replayed with identical policy noise (Fig. 2 a). Directly chaining A\to B collapses success from 98.0% (isolated) to 14.2%. Standard re-homing removes body shift and recovers success to 82.8%, leaving a 15.2-point residual gap. On these identical re-homed states, we next apply View Reset : hiding residual objects from the renderer while keeping physical colliders intact. Strikingly, View Reset improves success by only + 1.7 points (95% CI [ - 0.5, + 3.9]) and leaves arm-residue collisions virtually unchanged (17.0% vs. 17.8%). In our running example (open middle drawer \to push plate to stove), erasing the drawer from the image still causes the arm to run into it in 10 of 10 handoff states. In contrast, World Reset , which physically restores residual objects via state teleportation, recovers + 10.0 points (92.8%) and drops collisions to 2.0%. While visual shift can affect isolated seams or narrower policies [ 14 ] , in shared-workspace multi-step tasks with a strong pretrained VLA, re-homed seam failure is dominated by physical collision. Policy-specific, not scene-generic. As shown in Fig. 2 b, the exact same open middle drawer devastates five of the nine downstream skills ( {\leq}\, 4/10 success) while leaving three others nearly untouched ( {\geq}\, 9/10). Whether a residue breaks a seam cannot be judged from scene state alone or inferred reliably from a task description: it depends on the 3D corridor a frozen policy’s arm actually traverses. Done is not ready. Together, these results expose the mechanism behind the compositional gap. A frozen VLA encodes its hidden preconditions in its weights: never having seen an open drawer along its approach path, it neither recognizes the obstacle nor replans around it, rigidly replaying its habitual sweep straight into contact. Yet that very rigidity points directly to the cure: because the arm traces a repeatable corridor, we can measure its swept volume in advance and flag obstructions before the skill is ever called.

## III Skill Envelopes and Distribution-Aware Composition
III-A Measuring the Skill Envelope Because a frozen skill k executes repeatable trajectories within its training distribution, we can measure its spatial requirements directly from the policy itself. We run k in isolation from its nominal start states and discard any unsuccessful episodes, keeping N successful rollouts (37 to 89 here, depending on the skill). At each control step, forward kinematics maps the robot’s links, gripper, and grasped object into a world-frame grid of 1 cm voxels, marking those their oriented bounding boxes pass through. Aggregating occupancy across the N executions yields the Skill Envelope , the empirical probability that skill k sweeps through voxel x : E_{k}(x)\;=\;\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\mathbf{1}\!\left[x\in\mathrm{sweep}_{i}\right]\;\in[0,1]. (1) Profiling requires no human demonstrations, no failure rollouts, and no symbolic domain model , and scales linearly as O(n) with the number of skills rather than O(n^{2}) over skill pairs: an envelope describes the successor, whichever predecessor produced the residue. The same rollouts record each skill’s effect (what it displaces and where), so candidate skill orders can be scored pre-execution. III-B Geometric Tools and Readiness Scoring Given a scene state s , its residue R(s) is the set of elements (objects or articulated parts) altered by prior skills, excluding those the successor is itself meant to manipulate. Element e occupies voxels V_{e} , and its overlap with the envelope of skill k is m_{e}(k,s)=\max_{x\in V_{e}}E_{k}(x),\qquad o_{e}(k,s)=\sum_{x\in V_{e}}E_{k}(x), (2) where m_{e} is peak occupancy probability and o_{e} is expected overlapping volume (in \mathrm{cm}^{3} ). We expose the measured envelopes to the agent through two classical 3D tools (Fig. 3 a): • Check (k,s) : computes the overlap m_{e}(k,s) of every residue element with E_{k} . If m_{e}(k,s)\geq\tau , Check flags the seam as blocked and returns the offending residue, ranked by overlap, to repair. We fixed \tau{=}0.05 in advance. • Free (K,s) : given remaining downstream skills K , computes the complement of \bigcup_{j\in K}\{x:E_{j}(x)\geq\tau\} to return the nearest supported, collision-free poses for relocating blocking objects without obstructing future steps. Zero-failure gating vs. readiness scoring. Crucially, both collision scoring ( \sum_{e}o_{e} , RQ1) and the Check repair gate ( m_{e}\geq\tau , RQ4) are purely geometric and require zero failure data . To rank candidate skill orderings (RQ5), compare against VLMs (RQ3), and test generalization (RQ2), we map overlap (m_{e},o_{e}) and residual re-homing pose error to a readiness probability p_{k}(s) via a lightweight shared calibration head: either a logistic scorer \sigma(w^{\top}\phi_{k}(s)) (with displaced-element indicators) or a type-agnostic 5-parameter multiplicative hazard head p_{\text{base}}\prod_{e\in R(s)}\exp(-(am_{e}+co_{e})) that saturates smoothly on unseen object categories. Removing geometry collapses generalization to chance (RQ2), confirming power comes from the zero-failure envelope rather than memorized pairs. III-C Distribution-Aware Division of Labor Algorithm 1 Envelope-guided agent loop 1: goals G ; skills with envelopes and effects; programs \mathcal{P} ; budget B 2: C\leftarrow\mathrm{LLM}(G) ; order \leftarrow\arg\max_{C}\prod_{k}p_{k} \triangleright semantic constraints 3: for skill k in order, with K the skills still pending do 4: Home ; X\leftarrow Check (k,s) \triangleright blocking residue 5: while X\neq\emptyset and fewer than B repairs do 6: execute u\in\mathcal{P} the LLM picks for X , targets from Free (K,s) 7: Home ; X\leftarrow Check (k,s) \triangleright verify 8: end while 9: execute the frozen VLA \pi_{k} \triangleright in distribution 10: end for 11: re-invoke skills whose goals a repair undid; verify G Algorithm 1 integrates Check and Free into an agentic loop: given goals G , the agent respects semantic constraints C while picking the highest-readiness order \prod_{k}p_{k} . Before each skill, if the seam is clear, the frozen VLA executes inside its training distribution; if residue intrudes into E_{k} , a short classical repair program from \mathcal{P} (pre-built analytic primitives in Stage 1, extensible via Free -guided code synthesis in Stage 2) restores the initiation set without changing policy weights, and any undone prior goal is re-invoked at the end.
