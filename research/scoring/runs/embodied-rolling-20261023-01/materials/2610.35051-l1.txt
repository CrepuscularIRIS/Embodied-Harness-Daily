# VISTA: Active Exploration for Self-Improvement in Embodied Agents

## Abstract
Agentic embodied intelligence plays a foundational role in open-ended, dynamic physical environments. Rather than treating policy learning as an end-to-end mapping from instructions to actions, recent studies reveal that embodied systems depend not only on pre-trained foundation models, but also on harness. Prior approaches largely treat rollouts as static logs for post-hoc reflection, producing harness updates without autonomously testing and refining them through subsequent embodied interaction. To address this challenge, we propose VISTA, a meta-agent framework that transforms post-hoc rollout reflection into active self-exploration for embodied harness improvement. Specifically, VISTA maintains a harness graph that records the lineage of proposed harnesses, which enables the meta-agent to retrieve prior hypotheses, build on effective mechanisms, and avoid repeatedly exploring failed directions. Each iteration begins from a retained harness version and its execution evidence. Rather than directly committing an update from these historical trajectories, the meta-agent actively explores in the environment. It autonomously generates new rollouts, probes execution failures and recovery behaviors, and iteratively modifies the harness based on the resulting evidence. The refined harness is then evaluated afresh and added to the harness graph. We evaluate VISTA on the LIBERO-Pro, RoboCasa, and RoboTwin benchmarks. Experimental results demonstrate that VISTA progressively improves task success and is more rollout-efficient than static reflection, achieving higher success rates with fewer simulator rollouts.

## 1 Introduction
Embodied artificial intelligence has emerged as a cornerstone for enabling autonomous systems to perceive, reason, and interact seamlessly within dynamic physical environments, offering transformative potential across household assistance, healthcare logistics, and industrial automation [ 4 , 40 ] . Catalyzed by multimodal foundation models and large-scale embodiment datasets, learning-based paradigms have demonstrated increasingly competitive capabilities in executing atomic manipulation tasks [ 60 , 39 , 25 , 3 , 44 , 51 , 37 , 55 , 28 ] . But scaling end-to-end policies to long-horizon, multi-stage tasks remains notoriously difficult. End-to-end models suffer severely from compounding execution errors over extended trajectories, out-of-distribution generalization under perturbations, and prohibitive sample complexity when attempting to fit multi-modal, long-tail action distributions across heterogeneous task phases [ 1 , 23 ] . To overcome these fundamental limitations, the convergence of frontier large language model (LLM) agents and robotics has established embodied agentic systems as a pivotal research frontier [ 32 , 49 , 22 , 50 , 56 ] . By coupling high-level symbolic reasoning and stateful tool orchestration with low-level control policies, agentic embodied architectures offer decisive advantages in managing long-horizon complexity: they facilitate modular hierarchical task decomposition, support active perception to resolve visual ambiguities, and enable closed-loop error diagnosis and autonomous recovery. The performance, reliability, and long-horizon coherence of such systems depend not only on their pre-trained foundation model weights, but also fundamentally on their harness [ 54 , 48 ] . Existing systems construct and improve these harnesses through code generation, reusable skills, and memory-guided execution [ 32 , 49 , 50 , 56 ] . VISTA focuses on a complementary question: how can a coding agent improve an executable robot harness by acquiring additional evidence through direct interaction with the environment? Existing records do not always contain the observations or interventions needed to test a proposed explanation of a failure. To address this issue, we propose VISTA , a meta-agent framework that unifies active environmental exploration with closed-loop harness adaptation to achieve embodied self-improvement. The meta-agent controls the development process: it selects what to inspect, which public sensing and control capabilities to invoke, and which autonomous tests to request. Evidence from these interactions guides executable harness revisions. After development, the submitted harness is frozen and evaluated through fresh autonomous execution. Its code and evidence are retained in a harness DAG, allowing the meta-agent to retrieve prior hypotheses, reuse effective mechanisms, and explore from earlier candidates. We evaluate VISTA on LIBERO-Pro [ 59 ] , RoboCasa365 [ 38 ] , and RoboTwin [ 9 ] . VISTA improves task success by accumulating executable mechanisms across development rounds. These results support harness development as a practical way to convert embodied experience into persistent changes in agent behavior. Our contributions are summarized as follows: • We propose VISTA, a framework that departs from conventional handcrafted heuristics and static memory augmentation by framing the robot’s operational harness as an evolvable, executable control plane, realizing autonomous embodied self-improvement. • We introduce an active exploration mechanism where the meta-agent empowers the embodied system to actively gather diagnostic physical evidence, linking exploratory evidence to executable harness revisions. Furthermore, we develop a Harness Graph to track the evolutionary lineage of candidate harnesses, facilitating hypothesis retrieval, component reuse, and principled branch exploration. • We conduct comprehensive evaluations on the challenging LIBERO-Pro, RoboCasa, and RoboTwin benchmarks. Experimental results demonstrate that VISTA progressively improves manipulation success rates through accumulated experience, improving over the corresponding initial harnesses.

## 3 Method
VISTA improves an embodied agent by evolving its harness, including the instructions, executable skills, and coordination logic through which the agent perceives, reasons, and acts. The online agent has access to a language-model planner, a VLA model, and public robot capabilities. VISTA keeps the underlying model parameters fixed and optimizes how these resources are composed into autonomous behavior. Figure 2 illustrates the resulting development loop. A meta-agent, implemented as a coding agent, retrieves a harness version and its execution evidence, identifies a behavior to improve, and develops a candidate. During development, it can actively investigate the environment, probe execution failures, and revise the harness in response to newly acquired evidence. It can also request autonomous tests of intermediate versions. The submitted candidate is then frozen and evaluated in fresh autonomous episodes. A host-controlled selection rule updates the incumbent, while the candidate and its evidence remain available for subsequent iterations. Improvement is thus carried forward through both executable harness versions and the experience accumulated while developing them. 3.1 Harness DAG Let L_{\theta} denote the online language model, \pi_{\phi} the VLA, and \mathcal{C} the public capability interface. The interface exposes observation, perception, policy inference, and bounded robot control. A harness is a package H=(I,\mathcal{S},P,M,D) , where I specifies model-facing instructions, \mathcal{S} contains skill descriptions and executable implementations, P provides hooks for request construction, tool selection, and feedback processing, M stores persistent procedural knowledge, and D declares tool bindings and capability dependencies. Skills may compose multiple perception or control operations, while policy hooks govern their use within the host’s execution loop. Harness revisions can therefore change both the operations available to the agent and how it coordinates them. The model parameters, capability interface, and evaluation rule are fixed. For a task instance x , comprising a goal and scene initialization, and execution randomness \xi , let S(H;x,\xi)\in\{0,1\} denote official autonomous task success. A frozen harness still induces closed-loop behavior: its code and persistent knowledge remain fixed during evaluation, while episode-local state and actions depend on incoming observations.
