===ABSTRACT===
Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models have emerged as a promising paradigm for general-purpose robotic manipulation, yet their performance often degrades on long-horizon tasks that require multiple interdependent manipulations. Instead of burdening the VLA policies with long-horizon reasoning, memory, action generation, progress tracking, and error recovery, we argue for leveraging their short-horizon manipulation capabilities within an execution-aware framework. To this end, we introduce \textnormal{\textbf{RePlan-VLA}}, a VLA-agnostic framework that translates a VLA's short-horizon capabilities into reliable long-horizon execution. RePlan-VLA explicitly monitors the robot's motion for progress evaluation, and adaptively revises the future plan based on observed outcomes. We preserve the underlying VLA policy entirely, requiring no additional fine-tuning. Experiments on RoboCasa365 and CALVIN benchmarks show an improvement of 21.9% and 10.4% in success rate over the next strongest baseline. We also validate our approach for real-world deployment on a Franka Research 3 robot.

===INTRO===
Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models [ 27 , 29 , 11 , 23 , 15 , 8 ] have made significant progress toward developing generalist robotic policies capable of performing diverse manipulation tasks. Despite their performance on short-horizon tasks (e.g. ‘Pick up the straw and place it in the cup.’), these models often struggle with reliable execution of long-horizon instructions (e.g. “Open the drawer, pick up the straw, navigate to the cup, and place the straw in the cup”). This can be attributed to their inability to coordinate simultaneous progress tracking and execution of the long-horizon tasks, allowing small errors to propagate and cause downstream failures. Towards this end, we propose decoupling of long-horizon reasoning, action generation, progress tracking, and error recovery from a single-VLA policy to a VLA-agnostic framework, retaining the VLA policy only as a reliable executor of short-horizon tasks. Recent approaches improve long-horizon manipulation through complementary mechanisms: leveraging future prediction [ 10 , 36 , 5 ] , persistent action memory [ 20 , 31 , 10 ] , and action prediction [ 17 ] . Progress-aware approaches [ 34 , 32 , 14 ] , either leverage progress estimates as monotonically increasing rewards for computationally intensive policy training, do not consider explicit failure diagnosis and recovery, or overlook the compositional structure and interdependencies of long-horizon tasks by treating them as monolithic execution problems. Consequently, missed prerequisites and incorrectly assumed completions propagate through subsequent manipulations, motivating the need for an execution-grounded framework capable of handling task dependencies, progress monitoring, execution awareness, and failure-conditioned replanning. To address these challenges, we propose a VLA-agnostic framework for long-horizon task execution. Our approach integrates language-based task decomposition, visual progress monitoring, and failure-conditioned re-planning into a closed-loop execution pipeline. We evaluate our approach on the RoboCasa365 [ 22 ] composite-task benchmark and a focused subset of challenging CALVIN’s [ 21 ] long-horizon task sequences. In summary, our main contributions are threefold: • We introduce RePlan-VLA , a VLA-agnostic framework that enables policies to reliably execute long-horizon tasks without requiring further VLA fine-tuning. • We develop a fine-tuned progress-monitoring VLM that grounds sub-task progress estimation and failure diagnosis via visual observations to guide adaptive re-planning. • We demonstrate the effectiveness of RePlan-VLA on challenging long-horizon manipulation benchmarks, improving average task success rate from 40.6\% to 62.5\% on RoboCasa365 composite tasks, and from 3.58/5 to 4.10/5 on CALVIN ABCD \rightarrow hardD.
