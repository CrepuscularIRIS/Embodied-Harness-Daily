# Toward the Era of Agentic Embodied Intelligence

## Abstract
Foundation models increasingly guide robot actions, yet sustained autonomy requires efficient control and failure recovery. We introduce A2-MEAS, an embodied multi-agent framework built around code as a scaling interface. Learned correction codes combine corrective language, spatial evidence, execution history, and action proposals. MetaBrain provides training supervision and runtime guidance. A correction encoder and adapter absorb this knowledge into policy weights during training, while fresh codes adapt behavior with frozen weights at deployment. The action model, A2-WLA, adds motion-aware history and a latent world model to a vision-language-action policy. Surprisingly, this simple design with action pretraining and task specialization reaches 0.5755 Q-score on the 50-task BEHAVIOR 2026 public subset before online correction, 2.21x the 2025 winner's reported 0.2605 across different benchmark editions and training configurations. On all 100 BEHAVIOR 2026 public tasks, A2-MEAS achieves 0.6411 Q-score and 45.40% task success. On RoboTwin 2.0, A2-WLA achieves 93.98% average success, rising to 95.05% with the complete system. Across 12 real-robot tasks in five household settings, A2-WLA achieves 57.92% average success versus 26.67% for pi0.5. In long-horizon pour-over coffee preparation, a history-conditioned adapter raises post-failure completion at two filter-handling steps from 14% to 94% and from 8% to 82%, compared with A2-WLA retries. Our training infrastructure delivers 427.1 samples per second, 4.03x the throughput of a pi0.5 reference implementation on matched hardware. We will release the system, models, training scripts, and infrastructure to support efficient embodied learning.

## 1 The A 2 -MEAS framework
General embodied intelligence requires a system to connect its intentions to the physical consequences of its actions. Over a long activity, a robot must remember what remains to be done while responding to changes in objects, contacts, and its own configuration. A successful movement is useful only if the resulting state supports continued progress. A 2 -MEAS addresses this problem by coupling learned action generation with task-level reasoning and evidence-based diagnosis. The framework contains three cooperating groups of agents ( Figure 1 ). A 2 -WLA generates continuous actions from the task instruction and observed history. MetaBrain monitors execution in relation to the full goal and determines when the next attempt should change. Diagnostic agents investigate the physical questions needed to make that decision. All three use a shared record of actions and observations. When execution requires intervention, a learned correction interface converts their instruction and evidence into changes inside the action generator. Section 1.1 explains how the shared record coordinates these agents. Section 1.2 introduces the action model, its observation and prediction components, and the adapter that accepts corrective guidance. Section 1.3 follows a recovery example from diagnosis through corrected execution. The local loop repeatedly generates action chunks from the task instruction and recent observations. The task-level loop uses the resulting evidence to decide whether execution can continue, whether the goal has been achieved, or whether recovery is needed. A shared execution record stores the goal, dispatched controls, and observed outcomes. It gives all agents a common account of what has actually happened without requiring them to share a backbone or run at the same frequency. The execution layer dispatches only a prefix of each predicted chunk, then obtains fresh observations before the next policy call. Thus, control is closed-loop across chunks even though an uninterrupted prefix executes without a new policy prediction. A 2 -WLA handles ordinary changes through this repeated observation–action cycle. When monitoring detects an abnormal outcome, the runtime pauses pending controls and asks MetaBrain to review the evidence. Verified recovery returns execution to the nominal loop, and verified completion ends the episode. Diagnostic agents are invoked when a decision depends on an unresolved physical condition. Table 1 groups their functions into perceptual grounding, spatiotemporal grounding, and interaction assessment. Perception locates objects and regions. Temporal analysis tracks how they move relative to one another. Interaction assessment combines those observations with available robot-state, contact, and geometric evidence. For example, seeing an object between the gripper fingers does not establish a secure grasp. Tracking whether it moves with the hand, together with gripper-state evidence, supports a more informative assessment. Depth or reconstructed geometry can supply additional evidence when available. Each finding retains its observation source and timestamp so that MetaBrain can distinguish a supported conclusion from a missing observation. The result determines what happens next. Evidence that supports the required condition permits execution to continue. Evidence of a mismatch prompts a correction. Insufficient evidence prompts another observation within the sensing budget. A negative answer and an unknown answer therefore lead to different decisions. Conditional tool invocation keeps ordinary control inexpensive while allowing several diagnostic capabilities to contribute when a correction needs them. 1.2 Action model and correction interface Let g denote the complete task instruction, retained throughout nominal execution. We index the physical control step at which the policy executes by t . The policy input x_{t} aggregates synchronized multiview visual observations, a finite temporal window of past visual frames, and the current proprioceptive state s_{t} comprising joint positions and end-effector configurations. A 2 -WLA combines three sources of action context. A vision-language backbone encodes the task instruction and visual observations. Its motion-aware history encoder represents changes across recent frames. A latent world model predicts future visual features from a transition representation inferred by the backbone. The action expert uses current context, predicted future context, and proprioception to generate controls. Future observations supervise training but are unavailable at deployment. Section 3 describes these components and the three stages used to train the base policy. During standard nominal operation, A 2 -WLA executes with the correction branch deactivated.
