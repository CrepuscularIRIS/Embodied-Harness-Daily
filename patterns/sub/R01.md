# R01 — Have the LLM write a formal planning model and let a classical solver compute, check or prune the plan
_parent: **Let a Solver Plan from an LLM-Written Formal Model** ([`llm_symbolic_solver_planning`](../main/llm_symbolic_solver_planning.md)) · status: candidate_pattern · source: board-induced 2026-10-21 (wave-1 weak fits)_

## Operation
have a frozen LLM/VLM translate the instruction and scene into a formal model — PDDL problems, a Prolog knowledge base, SMT constraints, a precedence/scheduling graph, or executable search code — let a classical solver or search (Fast Downward, BFS, an SMT task planner, a disjunctive-graph scheduler, backward search over the domain's action graph) compute, check or prune the plan, feed solver failures (no plan, inconsistency, refinement failure) back to the LLM for repair, and compile the result into skills, behaviour trees or robot programs

- Information access: Frozen LLMs/VLMs, nothing trained; the planner gets a hand-written domain (PDDL domain, primitive set, action library) and an abstracted or fully observed symbolic state (ground-truth PDDL domain and abstracted states 2601.11063#board; full environment state as text 2503.01700#board; full observability 2602.21670#board); one variant gives the VLM a scene image plus geometry text before planning (2510.25548#board); one keeps a human in the validation loop (2502.19135#board).
- Contrasts: Contrasts with K201 (an external checker gates a model-proposed action; here the solver produces or prunes the plan itself) and K105 (code cells compose primitives inside the embodied loop; here the formal model or search code encodes the combinatorial planning problem, solved before execution).
- Transfer: Classical task and (task-and-motion) planning with the LLM as a front-end translator; transfers where a symbolic domain and a reliable state abstraction exist. Perception, partial observability and replanning after execution failures are largely untested.
- Status: candidate_pattern (7 member papers, all with full text)

## When it works
- The solver carries the combinatorial step and this is ablated: replacing the LLM-built scheduling graph with LLM-ordered operations drops complex multi-robot SR 0.68 -> 0.00 (dependency-only variant 0.36); removing the hierarchy that keeps each LLM-written PDDL problem small drops SR 0.84 -> 0.25 (2603.02669#board, 2602.21670#board)
- Solver failures are fed back for repair: removing failure-driven prompt optimisation drops SR 0.84 -> 0.47; VLM-written SMT constraints revised after planner failures are the only method at 100% success on a cooking TAMP domain, where the other baselines fail beyond 2 ingredients (2602.21670#board, 2510.25548#board)
- Separate checkers are toggled one at a time and backbones are varied: GPT-4o average 52.3 vs 42.7 without the LLM-written checker and 41.3 without the rule-based complexity checker (Code Interpreter 34.3); averages 63.9 vs 28.8 (Claude) and 46.0 vs 26.9 (Mistral) over code-answer baselines; a six-robot 3D box task reaches 95% vs 35% over 20 runs (2503.01700#board)
- Compared against LLM-planning and LLM+PDDL baselines on multi-robot household benchmarks: aggregate SR 12% -> 55% and GCR 32% -> 72%; action-graph pruning gives overall TCR 78% vs 53% (LLM-corrected PDDL baseline) and 39% (LLM+P), complex tasks 59 vs 24 (2601.11063#board, 2603.08814#board)
- Strong models write consistent formal models: the strongest models classify all 16 domain-validation queries correctly and one needs 1 edit across 12 high-level knowledge bases (2502.19135#board)

## When it fails
- The symbolic domain and state are handed to the planner: ground-truth PDDL domains, abstracted states, full observability or the whole state as text, with open-loop plans and no replanning when execution fails (2601.11063#board, 2602.21670#board, 2503.01700#board, 2603.08814#board, 2603.02669#board)
- Human repair carries the pipeline and is not separated: a human confirms validation and hand-fixes knowledge bases that do not plan, there is no end-to-end success without that repair, the repair loop is not ablated, and the real robot is a demo (2502.19135#board)
- An LLM validation gate is sycophantic: a weaker open model accepts all three deliberately invalid specifications and yields no feasible knowledge base (2502.19135#board)
- Planning time grows and is partly left out: 173 s vs 32 s without prompt optimisation; 62.27 s vs 12.83 s for direct LLM planning; the reported planning time excludes VLM prompting (2602.21670#board, 2603.08814#board, 2510.25548#board)
- Settings are tuned on the evaluation problems: the re-prompt threshold is picked per domain by fewest failures and prompts are optimised on the tasks they are scored on (2510.25548#board, 2602.21670#board)
- Evidence rests on small own benchmarks and single runs: 42 tasks with an ablation row equal to the baseline row on all six numbers, 49 tasks with one run, 50 tasks with one run, 30 problems per domain in simulation (2601.11063#board, 2603.08814#board, 2603.02669#board, 2510.25548#board)

## Counterexamples
- The formal layer adds little on easy or vague tasks: action-graph filtering leaves vague-task TCR at 71 vs 71; parallel-independent SR stays 0.43 with one or two pre-planning stages removed, while removing the behaviour-tree compiler drops it to 0.14; on single-robot tasks a prompt-based baseline performs comparably (2603.08814#board, 2601.11063#board, 2603.02669#board)
- Direct answers can win: with one backbone the box-moving score under the method (52.2%) is below asking the question directly (67.1%), a code interpreter beats it on two tasks (76.7 vs 74.2; 19.6 vs 17.6), and VLM-only planning reaches 100% on packing (2503.01700#board, 2510.25548#board)
