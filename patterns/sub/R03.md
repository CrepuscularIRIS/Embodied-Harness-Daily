# R03 — Let LLM agents allocate subtasks across robots and repair the allocation from feasibility, conflict or event feedback
_parent: **Allocate and Repair Multi-Robot Plans with LLM Agents** ([`multi_robot_llm_coordination`](../main/multi_robot_llm_coordination.md)) · status: candidate_pattern · source: board-induced 2026-10-21 (wave-1 weak fits)_

## Operation
give a team of heterogeneous robots one or more LLM agents that decompose the task and allocate subtasks to robots — centrally (a task manager or chain writer), by dynamic grouping with subgroup negotiation, or per robot with a consensus round — then check the allocation against robot capabilities and shared state (per-robot executor-LLM feasibility feedback, a rule validator for preconditions and same-object conflicts, a learned role selector, an event filter on onboard perception) and replan or re-negotiate only the affected part

- Information access: Frozen LLMs in three members (GPT-4o / Grok 3 task manager with scenario configuration and onboard events 2511.22354#board; a centralized chain writer with simulator ground-truth object and agent states 2509.24230#board; per-robot LLM agents in negotiation groups under partial observability 2602.06967#board); one member LoRA-tunes small per-UAV LLMs on 8,231 filtered GPT-4o samples and trains a role-selection network, with frozen lower-level controllers (2507.01378#board).
- Contrasts: Contrasts with R01 (multi-robot plans are computed by a symbolic solver; here LLM agents allocate and repair them) and K201 (a gate on one agent's proposal; here the check is on inter-robot allocation and conflicts). Contrasts with K202 (a monitor hands control back to one planner; here an event triggers reallocation across robots).
- Transfer: Multi-agent task allocation and negotiation from distributed robotics with LLMs as allocators; transfers where robot capabilities can be written down and shared state is observable; scaling beyond a handful of robots and real-robot statistics are thin.
- Status: candidate_pattern (4 member papers, all with full text)

## When it works
- The feasibility or conflict feedback is ablated: dynamic grouping, subgroup negotiation and executor feedback reach CLiMBench SR 1.000 in 8.8 steps vs 0.700 in 15.8 for the best baseline, and SR falls to 0.200 without the feedback (2602.06967#board)
- Multi-step commitments cut LLM calls without losing success: action chains with a precondition/conflict validator use 29.64K tokens per task vs 70.18K for an LLM-cooperation baseline at similar transport rate (78.54 vs 77.50); removing the action chains raises steps by 62.9% (2509.24230#board)
- Events trigger replanning of the unfinished tasks only: on hardware 9/10 recovery runs, 8/8 coordinated transport and 5/5 human-assisted runs; on a text benchmark correctness 0.91 and replanning 1.0 with one backbone (2511.22354#board)
- Roles are differentiated and tested: three roles beat one role (mean reward about -3,000 for a single role) and the method holds up at swarm sizes 9-11 where a hierarchical baseline drops (2507.01378#board)

## When it fails
- Success is judged on plan text or by experts, with no baseline numbers: correctness is scored by an expert on text plans, replanning is scored only when the initial plan was correct, and event filtering and status monitoring are not ablated (2511.22354#board)
- The validator and the allocator are not separated: the precondition validator is never ablated on its own, constraint violations and hallucinated actions still occur, and success stays below a reflection baseline (transport rate 79.17 vs 87.00) (2509.24230#board)
- Results are figure-only or on tiny own benchmarks: mean rewards over 30 episodes appear only in figures on the authors' own task with qualitative software-in-the-loop checks; 4 scenarios x 5 runs with one LLM (2507.01378#board, 2602.06967#board)
- Coordination is slow: about 15 s per LLM call on one GPU for the tuned 7B model, and the frontier-API baseline is confounded by API latency (2507.01378#board)
- Simulator ground-truth states are given to the central planner, so perception and communication errors are not tested (2509.24230#board)

## Counterexamples
- On an external benchmark the negotiation scheme does not raise success over a prior LLM coordinator (0.975 vs 0.975 average SR), only steps (2602.06967#board)
- More roles can hurt: adding a fourth (decoy) role lowers the reward (2507.01378#board)
- The one hardware recovery failure in 10 came from an incorrect replanning sequence (2511.22354#board)
