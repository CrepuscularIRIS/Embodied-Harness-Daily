# R02 — Fine-tune the high-level VLM that issues language or motion commands to a trained low-level policy
_parent: **Train the High-Level VLM That Commands a Low-Level Policy** ([`trained_vlm_vla_hierarchy`](../main/trained_vlm_vla_hierarchy.md)) · status: candidate_pattern · source: board-induced 2026-10-21 (wave-1 weak fits)_

## Operation
split the robot policy into a high-level VLM that maps images, the user prompt or interjection (and feedback) to an atomic language command, coarse motion instruction or subtask plan, and a low-level VLA, motion-conditioned policy or skill library that executes it; fine-tune the high-level VLM on robot-specific data (human-segmented skill labels with prompts back-generated by a larger VLM, expert motion pairs plus human corrections, LLM-generated multi-robot planning data) instead of prompting a frozen frontier model, and re-run it at a fixed rate or on each user utterance or detected failure

- Information access: Both levels trained in most members (high-level and low-level both from one VLM backbone 2502.19417#board; two fine-tuned MLLMs over a motion-codebook diffusion policy 2504.14588#board; an orchestrator VLM over a VLA exposed as a tool 2510.03342#board); one member trains only the high-level "brain" over pre-trained skills (2505.03673#board). The levels do not know each other's capabilities except through the training examples (2502.19417#board).
- Contrasts: Contrasts with K106 (a compact policy is trained for the calls of a frozen frontier planner; here the planner itself is trained) and K107 (a frozen VLA steered by a frozen planner). Contrasts with K501 (the student is distilled from a frontier harness teacher; here the high-level training data are human labels, corrections or LLM-generated examples, not harness trajectories).
- Transfer: Hierarchical VLA practice (language as the interface between a reasoning VLM and a motor policy); transfers when the command vocabulary is covered by the low level's training data. It removes frontier-API cost at run time but loses the frontier model's zero-shot breadth.
- Status: candidate_pattern (5 member papers, 4 with full text)

## When it works
- The high level is fine-tuned rather than prompted, and this is compared on the same low level: the same decomposition with a GPT-4o high level (not fine-tuned) and a flat VLA both score lower on instruction accuracy and task progress, as do ablations without the synthetic prompt data (2502.19417#board)
- Failure correction is a separate trained module: a motion-correction MLLM over a motion-prediction MLLM gives 57.8% mean success on 9 RoboMimic tasks (50 trials each) vs 46.9% for motion-conditioned prediction alone and 51.3% for a single model co-trained on the same correction data; Coffee_D0 94 vs 68 (2504.14588#board)
- The low level is good enough that reasoning is the bottleneck: with expert-human high-level commands the low-level policy executes nearly flawlessly (2502.19417#board)
- Trained planning data close the gap to large frontier planners on in-distribution benchmarks: multi-robot planning accuracy 81.74 vs 76.21 for the data-generating frontier model and 53.60 for the untrained 7B base; affordance mAP 44.06 vs 27.10 for the previous version (2505.03673#board)

## When it fails
- Planning is scored offline: multi-robot planning, pointing and affordance accuracy on in-distribution synthetic data, with real-world deployment shown as demonstrations and no end-to-end success or ablation of the shared memory, scheduler or error correction (2505.03673#board)
- Real-world evidence is thin: the real-world check of the correction loop is one drawer task with 20 corrections, after training on 3,644 online human interventions and 7,365 offline annotations besides 6,378 expert demonstrations (2504.14588#board)
- Baselines differ in more than the trained high level: the frontier high-level baseline is not fine-tuned on the robot data, and a comparison with an on-device model family is not like-for-like; over 90% of development evaluation episodes ran in simulation (2502.19417#board, 2510.03342#board)

## Counterexamples
- Co-training one model on mixed expert and correction data beats the separated correction module on two tasks (Stack_D1 90 vs 86; StackThree_D1 26 vs 20), and absolute success stays low on assembly (6%) (2504.14588#board)
